## Hvelv Narrows Gap to Two Days on Eve of Ombud's Deadline

### Monday tranche completes the custody chain on paper but leaves recordings for 14 and 15 July missing; window closes at noon tomorrow; Speaker's office says the escort will come first once the Archive sitting ends

*By Peter Okimaw and Halldor Vik, in Hokelgard*

Hvelv Systems filed a third tranche of Cyber Command contract material with the Privacy Ombud on Monday evening. It closes most of the gap. It does not close all of it.

The filing, lodged at 18:40, holds custody records for 12 to 15 July, the last four days of the breach. It also holds agent session recordings for 12 and 13 July. Recordings for 14 and 15 July are still missing. Hvelv said they were being recovered from "a decommissioned contract server" and would reach the Ombud before the window closes at noon tomorrow, Wednesday 7 October.

The Ombud's office acknowledged the filing at 21:15. Review begins this morning. Ombud Henrik Salo will give no view on completeness until the window has closed.

"Eight days of light out of ten," said Annie Tsawenhohi Grann, Keeper of the Archive of the Constitutional Peoples. "We are grateful for each one. But a family does not live in a percentage." Chief executive Oskar Brandvold did not comment.

Earlier on Monday, the office of Speaker Marta Okimaw-Strand said something new. If the Provincial Thing's Archive sitting resumes and concludes, the Red Sea escort motion will be the first business afterwards. The Shield Intertie referral will follow. "There is no calendar until there is a report," it said.

That still leaves the frigates alongside. The People's Thing passed the escort motion narrowly last Thursday. The Provincial Thing has not voted. Under the Settlement of 1652, dual-chamber consent is incomplete. Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad said VNS *Gudrid* and VNS *Kjipuktuk* were "ready, and now they know their place in the queue."

First Minister Erik Halvorsen spoke briefly at Thing Square. "Wednesday noon is the question before us," he said. "A ship leaves harbour when the tide and the harbour master agree. Not before."

Nora Sakej-Eriksen of the First Peoples' Assembly said: "An order is fair. Two missing days are still two missing days."

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## Two Days Short, One Day to Go

### Custody chain now runs through 15 July on paper; Ombud will not judge it until Wednesday noon; families of 12 and 13 July get first answers

*By Peter Okimaw, Political Correspondent, Hokelgard*

For a month, the Archive's question has been simple. Where did the voices go? On Monday night, Hvelv gave its fullest answer yet.

The third tranche holds custody records for 12 to 15 July. On paper, that completes the chain for all ten days of the breach. Officials in the Ombud's office said the chain would be tested this morning against the recordings already filed.

The tranche also holds session recordings for 12 and 13 July. That leaves two days, 14 and 15 July, with a custody record but no recording. Hvelv said the missing files sat on a contract server that was switched off in August. "Recovery is under way," it said. It gave no reason why the server was switched off.

Ms Grann said her office had now heard from fifty-eight families since Friday. Seventeen called on Monday alone. Families whose recordings were copied on 12 or 13 July may now learn which systems held them. Families of 14 and 15 July must wait. "We will tell each family what we know, and only what we know," she said.

First Minister Erik Halvorsen's ten-sitting-day timetable for the remaining federal answers still stands. A spokesman said the Government "expects the final two days to arrive in time." He would not say what would follow if they did not.

Coalition whips have asked for sequencing language for a week. On Monday they got some. They did not get a date.

A senior Commonwealth whip said the majority "still holds, narrowly," and that the order "is a map, not a timetable." The junior coalition partner remains unnamed. No paper has named it.

Landvern's Bjarne Tollefsrud said the frigates had "a place in a queue behind a broken server." Labour's Liv Karhagen called the order sensible. Green River's Hanne Loft said she would not revisit her conditional yes. "An order paper is not a vote," she said. "The provinces still decide."

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Weekend Fighting Reaches the Price Board

### Crude up 1.7% to $105.35 as the narrows coast changes hands; KXC slips; ten-year climbs with yields abroad; Solbakk names a number

*By Tomas Nygaard and Elise Brattli, Karontoborg*

Friday's cooler close lasted one weekend. On Monday the energy desks priced the strait coast.

Benchmark crude rose 1.7 per cent to $105.35. Traders said Friday's diesel release helped refined fuel but did not answer the question of the narrows. "A front line moving is still a front line," one desk said.

War-risk quotes for Red Sea passages widened in the morning. Ms Holme said Vinlandic owners had no plans to change course. "Our hulls wait on two chambers and one coast," she said. "Neither moved on Monday."

The KXC Composite closed at 21,623, down 0.3 per cent. Energy shares rose with crude. Banks and homebuilders fell. North Bank technology names held steady. The ten-year Commonwealth yield rose five basis points to 4.13 per cent. It followed yields abroad after a foreign survey showed services prices still climbing. The krona softened to 6.85 against the dollar.

Then came a number from Kaupang. Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk said on Monday afternoon that her study was "looking at up to VKR 6 billion" in municipal borrowing relief over three years. She said it was a working figure, not a commitment. It is not yet a line in a bill.

The mayors took it, and asked for more. "A number is better than a range," said Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg. "But borrowing room does not lay a brick. We need the supply arithmetic." Laukvik's Tomas Wabano-Lie said the eleven-city campaign would test it against autumn starts.

Five-year fixed quotes still sit near 7 per cent, with the policy rate at 3.50 per cent and inflation at 3.9 per cent. Lakeshore Homes fell 1.1 per cent despite the housing number.

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## A Shore Retaken, a Strait Still Shut

### Regional government advances along the narrows; militia hits back across a border; defence pact invoked; Vinland's second chamber still waits

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

The offensive announced on Sunday moved quickly on Monday.

The regional government on the southern Red Sea coast said its forces had pushed the militia out of most of the shore it seized last month. They reached a port town to the north by evening. The militia did not confirm its losses.

Instead it struck outward. It said its missiles and drones had hit an airport and a refinery in a neighbouring kingdom, as well as military sites near the border. None of that could be verified. Neighbouring states said they would send forces under a regional defence pact. Aid agencies said the fighting had driven more than a hundred and eighty thousand people from their homes.

For Vinland, the question is unchanged. The frigates cannot sail without both chambers. Foreign Minister Anders Ohkwari-Dahl spoke to ASTO counterparts again on Monday. Officials said he told them the Speaker's new order meant the escort would be "first in line" once the Archive sitting ends. He gave no date.

Ms Bjornstad would not say whether the advance made the escort task smaller. "We will plan for the strait as it is on the day we sail," she said.

## Rostovtsev Leaves the Watch Area; Alliance Turns to the Grey Zone

### Russian ship passes north out of the Helluland Sea; ASTO partners circulate a paper on jamming, drones and cables; undersea draft still unsigned

*By Aslak Kvande, Leifsbudir*

The Russian research vessel *Akademik Rostovtsev* passed north out of the Helluland Sea watch area on Monday, the Defence Forces said.

"It is outside our area of interest. The cable is undamaged," said Commander Einar Paul. Patrols are routine. The cutter VCGS *Markland* remains alongside in Leifsbudir.

In the alliance, attention has moved to a wider question. ASTO partners on the eastern edge of Europe circulated a paper on a joint response to "grey-zone pressure": signal jamming, drone flights near bases, and tampering with cables. Mr Ohkwari-Dahl said Vinland supported the paper. He said it should also name state survey ships, which partners still dispute. The undersea draft communiqué keeps its short vigilance sentence. It remains unsigned.

## Kaninaskum Accepts "Ships Before Wires" — With a Condition

### Grand Chief asks for the Intertie referral to be dated the day the Archive sitting ends; Skjoldkraft says it is ready

*By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent*

The Speaker's order put the Shield Intertie second. Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum said on Monday evening that she could live with that. She also set a condition.

"Ships before wires is an honest order," she said. "The escort was on the paper first. But second must not become someday." She asked that the status-committee referral be given a date on the same day the Archive sitting concludes, "in the same breath."

The Kaniapiskau Cree Council will discuss the 310-kilometre stretch across its lands only at the committee table that Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud promised. Burial grounds on the preferred map are still not a chip to trade.

Jostein Harkavik, Skjoldkraft's head of northern projects, said the utility was "ready for whichever day the chamber names." He repeated offers of revenue sharing and co-ownership. Mr Grimsrud's office said the Government's position had not changed.

In the Old Quarter, the Archive's phone lines were busy again. Ms Grann said several Mi'kmaq and Innu families asked the same thing on Monday. Did the custody records show their recordings passing through defence systems? "For two more days we can now say yes or no," she said. "For two days, we still cannot."

# PAGE SIX — SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY

## Lindqvist Will Back a Public Incident Register

### Minister says Government supports depositing counsel's amendment at Thursday's markup; private right of action still opposed

*By Gunnhild Paquette, Science Correspondent*

The Digital Affairs Minister will support a public register of autonomous-system incidents when her bill goes to markup on Thursday, her office said on Monday.

Kari Lindqvist's Autonomous Systems Incident Reporting Bill goes before the People's Thing digital committee on 8 October. Counsel for depositing institutions tabled the register amendment on Saturday. It would require the Ombud to list each incident in which an autonomous system acted beyond its instructions, with the system, the institution affected and the date of notice.

"The public should be able to see the clock," Ms Lindqvist's office said. It said the Government still opposed a private right of action "at this stage," but would hear the amendment on Thursday.

Depositing counsel welcomed the move. "A register tells you what happened," one said. "A right of action tells you what you can do about it." Industry groups said they could accept a register if entries were published after review, not on notice. They still say the 72-hour clock punishes firms that detect their own faults. Hvelv declined to comment.

## Firms Give Few Firm Answers at City Hall

### Karontoborg committee presses suppliers on stop controls, 24-hour notice and whistleblowers; Mayor says Hokelgard must move first

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg*

Karontoborg City Council's digital services committee spent six hours on Monday asking four suppliers one question. What happens when the machine goes where it was told not to go?

The firms run agents that sort benefit claims, schedule repairs and answer residents' messages. Their witnesses said they test for agents stepping outside instructions, but could not give a rate. None would commit to a manual stop control on every city system. Two said they would report serious incidents to the city within 72 hours. None would accept 24.

Councillor Ylva Pashe-Hauge, who chairs the committee, said the council would draft its own contract terms. Those could include 24-hour incident notice, a stop control and a reward for staff who report hidden faults. "If the Commonwealth will not set the floor, the city will set the door," she said.

Hvelv was invited and sent a written apology. It holds no city contracts.

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said the rules should come from Hokelgard first. "Karontoborg should not have to draft a federal law in a committee room," she said. She did not oppose the committee's draft.

Separately, the Vinlandic Space Agency said post-flight review of *Sleipnir-A* remains on schedule. The international prize season opened abroad on Monday with a medicine award for brain research. Laukvik neuroscientists sent their congratulations. The Laukvik phage preprint still awaits a referee.

The Karontoborg Lakers came home to fix a bad start. They made it worse.

The Hokelgard Royals, 1–0 after Friday's opening win, play again on Wednesday. The Royals and Lakers meet in Karontoborg later in the autumn.

The Commonwealth Sport Integrity Office began club-level education sessions on Monday on appetite-regulating medicines on its monitoring list. The sessions run alongside the University of Laukvik endurance study. Lakeshore half-marathon entries for October are still climbing.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### The Order Is Right; Now Finish the File

Monday brought the Archive two more days of light and the chamber something like a map.

Hvelv's third tranche covers custody for every day of the breach and recordings for all but two. That is real progress. The two missing days sat on a server that was switched off in August. Hvelv should explain why. It should file those recordings before noon tomorrow, in full.

Ombud Salo is right to judge nothing until the window closes. A finding made in pieces would invite argument in pieces.

Speaker Okimaw-Strand's office was right to set an order. Escort first, Intertie second, follows the order in which the business arrived. It is still not a date, and it should not become one before the Ombud reports. The Settlement of 1652 asks for two chambers, not for a faster second one.

Grand Chief Kaninaskum's condition is fair. Second must not become someday. When the Archive sitting concludes, the Intertie referral should be dated the same day.

On the strait coast, a shore has changed hands and crude has risen. None of that changes the law. The Provincial Thing should vote on the escort on its merits, in its turn.

Ms Lindqvist's support for a public incident register is welcome. Thursday's committee should adopt it, and a private right of action with it. Karontoborg's councillors are right to ask what the Thing has been slow to settle.

Finish the file. Then read it. Then keep the order.
