## Bjornstad Admits a Gap in the North

### Defence Minister tells committee no cutter was free on 25 October; extra patrol-aircraft hours until Markland returns; review of the northern rota; chair: "Not enough. Publish the review"

*By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad told the defence committee on Tuesday that there had been a gap in the watch on the north.

On 25 October a foreign survey vessel crossed the outer Helluland Sea. No cutter went out to meet it. VCGS *Markland* was in maintenance at Leifsbudir. The minister said no other cutter was free to take its place.

"The vessel was watched," she said. "A patrol aircraft tracked it for most of the day. But a watch from the air is not the same as a ship on the water. On that day we did not have a ship to send. That was a gap, and I will not pretend otherwise."

She announced two steps. Navy patrol aircraft will fly extra hours over the outer Helluland Sea until *Markland* returns on Thursday 19 November. Her ministry will also review how cutters are scheduled for maintenance in the north, so that the region is never left without one.

The committee chair was not satisfied. "An admission is better than a denial," the chair said after the hearing. "It is not enough. We want the review published, with a date."

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## "That Was a Gap"

### Bjornstad gives the committee an account of 25 October; Navy aircraft to add patrol hours over the outer Helluland Sea; maintenance rota to be reviewed; chair wants the review in public

*By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

Ragnhild Bjornstad spoke for about twenty minutes and answered questions for an hour. It was her first public account of the day the north was left without a cutter.

### The account

The foreign survey vessel entered the outer Helluland Sea on the morning of Sunday 25 October. It left northbound the next morning. The Defence Forces have said it did not stop near any undersea cable.

A Navy patrol aircraft tracked it for most of the day. VCGS *Markland* was in scheduled maintenance at Leifsbudir.

"Members asked me why no cutter went out," the minister said. "The plain answer is that none was free. *Markland* was in maintenance. The other cutters were on duties further south that we judged could not be left."

She did not say what those duties were. Asked by one member whether that could be said in public, she replied: "Not in detail. I have given the chair the detail."

### The two steps

Ms Bjornstad announced two measures.

First, Navy patrol aircraft will fly extra hours over the outer Helluland Sea until *Markland* returns to service on Thursday 19 November. She did not give the number of hours.

Second, her ministry will review how cutters in the north are scheduled for maintenance. "The aim is simple," she said. "There should always be a ship that can sail." The review would report "early in the new year".

### The chair

The committee's chair, who had asked for a private briefing in October and received it on 2 November, was cool after the hearing.

"The minister has admitted a gap," the chair said. "An admission is better than a denial. It is not enough. We want the review published, and we want a date for it, not a season."

Members from several parties asked the same question in different words. Will the public see the review? The minister said she would "consider what can be published".

### Labour

Liv Karhagen, the Labour leader, said the minister had been "honest about the past and vague about the future".

"She says there was a gap," Ms Karhagen said. "Good. Now tell us how big the next one could be. A review that reports in the new year, in private, is not a plan. It is a promise to think."

### In Leifsbudir

VCGS *Markland* remained alongside on Tuesday. The Coast Guard said again that it would return on 19 November. That is eight days from today.

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Solbakk's Ministry Takes the Contract

### One ministry will draft the standard terms for every firm in the VKR 14 billion package; cities and the steel union have asked to see the draft early; no reply yet

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

The question has been asked in the People's Thing, in the steel towns and on this page. Who is writing the contract?

On Tuesday the Government answered. Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk's office said the Finance Ministry would draft the standard contract for the industry package.

"The Finance Ministry will hold the pen," a spokesman said. "Other ministries will be consulted. The text will be laid before the People's Thing on Friday 20 November, as promised."

### Why it matters

The package is worth VKR 14 billion. It is meant to keep heavy industry working through a hard winter. Every firm that takes money will sign the same contract. That contract will set the conditions: jobs kept, plants kept open, money paid back if promises are broken.

Until the contract is laid, no money can be paid. Ministers agreed to that lock in the People's Thing last week. It passed by 194 votes to 6.

For weeks the Treasury said the drafting was "a matter for the Government as a whole". Labour pressed. The Treasury named the date three times. It did not name the drafter until Tuesday.

### Early sight

The steelworkers' union has asked to see a draft before the text is laid. So has the city of Laukvik. The ministry spokesman said only that the ministry "will talk to those who need to be talked to".

The spokesman would not say whether the contract would set a minimum period for keeping jobs. "That is what the contract is for," he said. "You will see it on the 20th."

## Prices Steady at 3.9 Per Cent

### October inflation unchanged; energy higher, food a little lower; economists still expect a rate rise on 19 November, but "the case did not get stronger"

*By Elise Brattli*

Inflation did not move in October. Consumer prices were 3.9 per cent higher than a year before, the Commonwealth Statistics Office said on Tuesday. That was the same rate as in September.

Energy prices rose again. Fuel and heating bills reflect the costs of the Gulf crisis and the long route round the Cape. Food prices fell a little in the month. Rents in the Lakes rose again.

Hedda Vang of the Lakes Mortgage Institute said the figure would not change many minds.

"The case for a rise did not get weaker," she said. "It did not get stronger either. The Bank was hoping October would make the decision for it. October declined."

The Central Bank decides on Thursday 19 November. The policy rate is 3.50 per cent. Most economists still expect a rise. Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen did not speak on Tuesday.

Large Lakes lenders kept their five-year fixed rates at 7.2 per cent, except two at 7.3.

## Talks Stall in the Gulf

### Both sides say talks are stuck; more foreign tankers reported burning on an "unauthorized route" near the strait; claims not confirmed; no Vinlandic ship involved

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

The talks to end the Gulf war stalled on Tuesday, a day after one party said fresh strikes were "unlikely while talks go on".

A senior official of one government called the other side's terms unrealistic. An official of the other said there were "no negotiations at present". The mediators did not speak. They have still not published the text of the strait agreement.

### More fires

One of the parties to the war said on Tuesday morning that two tankers had caught fire on what it called an "unauthorized route" near the strait. Later reports said a third had struck a mine. None of the claims has been confirmed, and no ships were named.

No Vinlandic-owned or Vinlandic-flagged ship was involved, the Shipowners' Association said.

### Holme: "One day"

Sigrid Holme of the Shipowners' Association said Monday's hopes had lasted a day.

"On Monday we were told strikes were unlikely," she said. "On Tuesday we were told ships were burning. Our captains do not plan by the day. They plan by the voyage. The long route is the only plan that works."

One more Vinlandic-flagged ship joined the long route round the Cape on Tuesday. The count is now thirty-one.

## The Warning Goes on the Screen

### Public checker now tells users that a missing label "does not show that a file is real"; Lindqvist: "We said it in the report. Now it is where people read it"

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg*

The public checker for machine-made images and recordings has a new line on its result page.

When a check finds no label, the page now says: "No label was found. This does not show that the file is real. It means the checker cannot tell."

The Ministry for Digital Affairs added the line on Tuesday. Minister Kari Lindqvist had promised it on Monday.

"We said it in the report," she said. "Now it is where people read it."

### Why the line matters

The checker looks for a hidden label that some tools add to the files they make. If it finds the label, it can say a machine made the file. If it finds nothing, it cannot say anything.

In its first eight days the checker was used about 222,000 times. About nine checks in a hundred found a label. That means about ninety-one in a hundred did not. Officials worry that people read those ninety-one as proof that a picture is genuine.

"A missing label is silence," a ministry official said. "Silence is not a witness."

The ministry will break the figures down by image and sound in its monthly report.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### The Gap, and the Pen

Tuesday brought two answers we have asked for. Both are welcome. Neither is the end of the matter.

### The gap

Ragnhild Bjornstad told the defence committee the truth. On 25 October no cutter went out because no cutter was free. *Markland* was in maintenance. The others were busy further south. The minister called that a gap. It was.

Ministers do not often say such things plainly. She deserves credit for doing so. An admission is the start of a repair.

But the chair of the committee is right. It is not enough. The minister offers extra flying hours until 19 November and a review that reports "early in the new year". The flying hours help for eight days. The review is the real answer, and it will be written in private unless the minister decides otherwise.

She should decide otherwise. The question is not secret. How many cutters does the north need, so that one can always sail while another is in dock? The public can understand that question. It can understand the answer too. The detail of duties further south can stay with the committee. The rota should not.

We also ask for a date. "Early in the new year" is a season. The committee asked for a date. So do we.

### The pen

For weeks we asked who was drafting the industry package's standard contract. On Tuesday the Government told us. The Finance Ministry holds the pen.

That is good. A contract with no author is a contract nobody answers for. Now Ms Solbakk answers for it.

Mr Ruud is right that naming the pen is not writing the page. Nine days remain to 20 November. The union and the city of Laukvik have asked to see a draft first. The ministry should show it to them. A contract that keeps jobs should be read by the people whose jobs it keeps.

The meeting at Sørstrand on the same day is a happy accident. The terms will be known in the morning. The talking can start in the afternoon.

### The price

Inflation held at 3.9 per cent. That will not make the Central Bank's choice easier. The Bank has now had its October figure, and the figure said nothing new. It should decide on the evidence of the autumn, not of one month.

### The strait

On Monday a foreign government said strikes were unlikely. On Tuesday ships were reported burning. Ms Holme is right to plan by the voyage, not by the day. The long route now carries thirty-one Vinlandic ships. The strait agreement is still unpublished. The mediators should publish it.
