## First Stage of the Strait Deal Slips a Day

### Mediators now say the strait will begin to reopen "within a day"; no ship has passed; the parties dispute whose vessels are checked first; text still unpublished; Holme: "Two days has become three"

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

The first stage of the Gulf strait agreement did not begin on Wednesday. Mediators said last night that it would begin "within a day".

On Tuesday they had said the parties had agreed in principle to reopen the strait in stages, starting within two days. That period ends today. No ship has yet passed through under the agreement.

Officials on both sides said the delay was about inspection. Each wants the other's vessels to be checked first. Neither side would say so on the record. The mediators called it "a question of sequence, not of substance".

The text of the agreement has still not been published. The mediators said they would "not release a document until there is something to release it for".

Benchmark crude fell 1.5 per cent on Wednesday, to $111.04 a barrel. Traders said they were pricing the hope of a deal, not the deal itself.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## Bill Read, Battle Kept for Committee

### Provincial Thing passes first reading 72 to 11; Landvern and Labour vote for; Sakej-Eriksen: "We have let it be read. We have not agreed to it"; Labour will seek a fixed register date in committee

*By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

The Provincial Thing read the Incident Reporting Bill for the first time on Wednesday afternoon. It passed by 72 votes to 11.

The reading had been due a week earlier. It was put back after members of the Lakes bench said they had received a corrected print only that morning. The Speaker, Marta Okimaw-Strand, ruled then that members must have a printed text a full day before a sitting. This time they did. No member raised a point of order.

### The count

The coalition parties voted for the reading. So did Labour, the Greens and Landvern. Landvern's members had said on Monday that they would vote for it, and they did, to a man and a woman.

Eleven members voted no. The Speaker's office said they came from several benches. None of them spoke in the debate.

### The Assembly's reserve

The First Peoples' Assembly did not oppose the bill. Its members voted for the reading. Nora Sakej-Eriksen, the Assembly's leader, said her members had done so to be fair to the House.

"We have let it be read," she said. "We have not agreed to it. The bill speaks of operators and of registers. It does not yet speak of the households whose recordings were taken. That is for the committee."

The Assembly holds the key votes in the Provincial Thing. Its reserve is not a threat, but it is a warning. Ministers will need the Assembly's support at the later stages.

### Labour's date

Runa Kahente-Moe, Labour's digital affairs spokeswoman, said her party had voted for the reading in order to have the argument in committee.

"We will ask for a fixed date for the register," she said. "A register with a date is a promise. A register with a season is a hope." Labour voted against the bill in the People's Thing on 22 October. It said then that it would try again in the second chamber.

### A Landvern view

A Landvern member who spoke in the debate said the party had come to read the bill, not to bury it. "We said we would vote for the reading, and we have," the member said. "The fighting starts in committee."

### What comes next

The bill goes to committee stage. The Speaker's office said the committee would be named "in the coming days". It is still expected to sit in November.

The People's Thing passed the bill on 22 October by eleven votes. If the Provincial Thing amends it, it will have to go back.

## Fifteen Days to the Date

### Solbakk's office has nothing to add on the standard contract; Treasury says drafting is "a matter for the Government as a whole"; nothing new from Karhagen or Tollefsrud

*By Sunniva Rask*

Friday 20 November is fifteen days away. That is the date by which Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk has said, three times, that the standard contract will be laid before the People's Thing.

Her office said on Wednesday that it had nothing to add. A Treasury spokesman was asked who was drafting the contract and when the draft would be ready. He said that was "a matter for the Government as a whole".

Liv Karhagen, the Labour leader, said nothing new. Bjarne Tollefsrud of Landvern also said nothing new. The Green River Alliance made no statement.

The motion passed on Tuesday by 194 votes to 6 has no force in law. It ties no minister to a date. But it was written by the Government, and the date was spoken in the chamber. Members will notice if it goes by.

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Shares Jump on a Broad Day, Gold Adds $71

### KXC up 1.8 per cent to 21,038; gains uneven across sectors; crude eases 1.5 per cent to $111.04; gold climbs to $4,087; yields slightly lower; krona firmer

*By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg*

Karontoborg shares rose sharply on Wednesday. The KXC Composite gained 1.8 per cent to close at 21,038. It has now recovered all of what it lost on Tuesday and a little more.

The gains were uneven. Lenders and builders led the way. Miners and energy producers were mixed. A few sectors ended lower. But the rises outweighed the falls by a wide margin.

Traders pointed to the strait. The mediators' announcement came after Tuesday's close, and Wednesday was the first session to price it. "The market took the word 'agreement' and ran," one trader said. "It did not stay for the part about inspections."

Benchmark crude fell 1.5 per cent to $111.04 a barrel. Gold rose $71 to $4,087 an ounce, its third rise in a row. The ten-year Commonwealth yield slipped two basis points to 3.78 per cent. The krona was a little firmer, at 6.87 to the dollar and 7.94 to the euro.

## Glimra Pulls Its Share Sale and Bond

### North Bank cloud firm postpones both offers, each worth about VKR 1.7 billion, announced on Monday; no new date

*By Tomas Nygaard*

Glimra Compute has postponed its share sale and its bond. Each was worth about VKR 1.7 billion. The firm announced both on Monday.

In a short statement on Wednesday, Glimra said it would "return to the market when conditions are settled". It did not say what conditions it meant, or when it expected them. It gave no new date.

The money was to pay for a third data hall near Forsgard. Glimra said the building work would go on "at the pace the firm can fund". It did not say how that pace would be funded.

Its shares fell on Monday when the plan was announced. They rose with the rest of the market on Wednesday.

"A firm that wants to build that fast has found that the market will not always oblige," an analyst said. "It is not a crisis. It is a lesson."

## The Method Is Still to Come

### Treasury has not published its reasoning for half weight, promised before 12 November; housing working group meets next Thursday; claims table the day after

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

The Treasury has not yet published the method behind its figure for federal land. It promised it "before 12 November". That is a week from today.

The figure is the weight of one half that the Treasury gives to federal sites when it counts a city's land against its target. Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg wants those sites to count in full, with a cap of one third of a city's first-year target. She will put that to the housing working group on Thursday 12 November.

Thirty-seven federal sites remain unclaimed. The other thirteen go to the claims table on Friday 13 November. The fifty sites cover 613 hectares.

The Treasury gave no new word on Wednesday. Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie of Laukvik, who said on Monday that a third was "a number I can be careful about", also gave no new view.

## A Quiet Week for the Steelmaker

### Sørstrand and the union say nothing new; no joint meeting with the city of Laukvik yet fixed; short time still starts on 1 December

*By Tomas Nygaard*

There was no news on Wednesday from Sørstrand Steel or from the steelworkers' union. The union has not answered the company's letter of Tuesday.

The company said in the letter that no worker would be dismissed against their will while the second strip line at Laukvik is paused. A union spokesman said on Tuesday that members would read it "line by line".

Mayor Wabano-Lie has asked the company to meet the union and the city together before the first of December. No date has been set. About 380 workers will go on short time when the pause starts.

## Twelve Days to the Statement

### Autumn statement on Tuesday 17 November; Central Bank decision on Thursday 19 November; the Governor has said nothing; lenders hold five-year fixed rates

*By Elise Brattli*

The autumn fiscal statement is twelve days away. It will be delivered on Tuesday 17 November. Two days later, on Thursday 19 November, the Central Bank will decide on interest rates.

Finance Minister Solbakk's office has said the statement will "hold the line". There will be no new spending beyond the industry package and the housing envelope.

Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen has said nothing about the decision. He has no appearance before a committee of the Thing scheduled before it.

Most economists still expect a rise from the present 3.50 per cent. Inflation is 3.9 per cent. Large Lakes lenders have held their five-year fixed rates at 7.2 per cent, except two at 7.3.

The Lakes Purchasing Managers' Institute said on Monday that its factory index fell to 49.5 in October, the first reading below 50 this year. The Statistics Office said last Friday that the economy did not grow in August.

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## Whose Ships First?

### Mediators say first stage will begin "within a day"; neither side will say who is to be checked first; no text; Holme: "Two days has become three"; no Vinlandic ship reported hit

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

The Gulf strait agreement is still a promise. It was announced late on Tuesday. The first stage was to begin within two days. By Wednesday night it had not begun.

Officials on both sides said the cause was the order of inspection. Each party wants the other's vessels checked first. Neither would say so in public. The mediators described it as "a question of sequence, not of substance".

They said the first stage would now begin "within a day". They did not say who would carry out the inspections. They did not say what would happen to a ship that failed one.

The text of the agreement has not been published. The mediators said they would not release it "until there is something to release it for".

Sigrid Holme, director of the Karontoborg Shipowners' Association, said her members had heard "two days" before.

"Two days has become three," she said. "That is how it goes. A deal that is agreed in principle can be unagreed in detail. Our captains will stay on the long route until a tanker has gone through and come out the other side."

The Navy said there was no change to its posture. Commander Einar Paul said again that it would announce a second escort "when it is under way". The first escort of six ships is complete. The four-month period set by the Things has not changed.

No Vinlandic ship was reported hit on Tuesday or Wednesday, the Association said.

## Rates Hold, Cape Count Back to Thirty

### War-risk houses keep their Gulf rates unchanged for a second day; one more Vinlandic ship takes the long route

*By Tomas Nygaard*

The two larger Karontoborg war-risk houses kept their rates for Gulf voyages unchanged on Wednesday. They have now held them for two days, after raising them five times in about a week.

An underwriter said the houses were waiting for a first transit. "A rate is a view of the risk," the underwriter said. "Our view will move when a ship does. It has not moved yet."

The Shipowners' Association said the number of Vinlandic-flagged ships on the long route round the Cape rose by one to thirty. A ship that had been due to enter the Gulf was ordered to take the detour instead.

"Thirty is not a number anyone wanted to see back," Ms Holme said. "It is a cautious number. It is not a panicked one."

## Markland Waits, Bjornstad Sets a Date

### Coast Guard has no change on VCGS *Markland*, due back in mid-November; Defence Minister to reply to defence committee on Tuesday 10 November

*By Aslak Kvande, Leifsbudir*

The Coast Guard said nothing new on VCGS *Markland*. The cutter is still due back in service in mid-November.

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad's office said on Wednesday that she would reply to the defence committee's report on Tuesday 10 November. Until now the office had said only "in weeks". The date is the first the committee has been given.

## Flu Season "Under Way": Figures in Full

### Positive tests rise from 7 to 13 per cent in a week; agency repeats vaccination advice for over-sixties and vulnerable groups; clinics urged to keep stocks

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg*

The flu season in the Commonwealth is under way. The Commonwealth Public Health Agency said so on Wednesday, when it published its weekly figures.

Thirteen per cent of tests were positive last week. The week before the figure was seven per cent. The agency had said on Monday that the season "has begun early" and that the share was rising. It had not said how fast.

An agency spokeswoman said the rise was "steep but not unexpected". She said the agency had no plans to change its advice. People over sixty-five and other vulnerable groups should be vaccinated now. Children's clinics, she said, should check their stocks.

Vaccine uptake among people over sixty-five is "ahead of last year", the agency said on Friday. It did not give a new figure on Wednesday.

Dr Liv Tobiassen of Stadakona University said last week that the virus now spreading looked close to the strain in this year's vaccine. She said the sample was small. Her laboratory is still sequencing.

"A match is good news," she said on Wednesday. "It is not a reason to wait."

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### A Reading Is Not a Reckoning

The Provincial Thing has read the Incident Reporting Bill. It was a clean reading. The text was printed a day early, as the Speaker had ruled it must be, and no member had cause to complain.

That is welcome. It is also not very much. A first reading is a courtesy. The House says that a bill exists. The reckoning comes later, in committee, and it will be harder.

### The Assembly's question

Ms Sakej-Eriksen has put her finger on the gap. The bill speaks of operators, clocks and registers. It does not yet speak of the households whose recordings were copied. The bill was tabled after the breach of the Archive, and it should not forget whom the Archive belongs to.

We do not think this is a point to be settled by a sentence in a schedule. The households should have a right to be told, and a right to have their material back or destroyed. If the committee writes that into the bill, the Assembly will have done the House a service. If it does not, the bill will not pass the later stages with the Assembly's help.

### A date and an owner

The contract is due on Friday 20 November. That is fifteen days from today. We asked on Wednesday who was drafting it. The Treasury says that is a matter for the Government as a whole.

That is not an answer. A document of that weight has an author, or it does not exist. We would like to hear by next Wednesday, in plain words, which ministry holds the pen. A Government that has said a date three times can say who is writing to it.

### Another day in the strait

The strait is not yet open. The agreement, such as it is, has no text and no schedule. It now has a quarrel as well, and the quarrel is about the order of the inspections.

That is a small thing to stop a large one. It is also the kind of small thing that stops large ones. We hope the mediators will see to it. Shipowners are right to keep their captains on the long route until a ship has passed. The war-risk houses are right to wait.

### A postponement

Glimra has pulled its share sale and its bond. We do not think this is the end of anything. We do think it is a lesson for a firm that tried to raise VKR 3.4 billion in a week when rates were about to be decided. Markets can be patient. They are rarely generous.

### The flu

The share of positive tests is up from seven per cent to thirteen. It is early to be alarmed. It is not early to be vaccinated. The agency's advice is plain, and it is the same as last week's. People over sixty-five should not wait.
