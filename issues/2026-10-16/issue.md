# PAGE ONE

## Frigates Slip for Red Sea Escort; Free Thing Meets Landvern; Lakers Take Overtime Win in Laukvik

### Gudrid and Kjipuktuk leave Kjipuktuk after coalition names the hour; junior partner still unnamed after private talks; housing working papers lodge quietly ahead of 29 October

*By Elise Brattli, Halldor Vik and Peter Okimaw, in Kjipuktuk and Hokelgard*

The escort is at sea.

The frigates VNS *Gudrid* and VNS *Kjipuktuk* slipped from Kjipuktuk on Thursday evening after the multinational coalition named the sailing hour in the afternoon. Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad said both ships were "under way on the agreed rules" and that the initial escort period remains four months unless the Great Thing renews it. Dual-chamber consent was completed on Tuesday. Families were given short notice. Tugs cleared the harbour mouth just after dusk. "Within days became one evening," a Navy spokesman said on the waterfront. "That is how a window is meant to work."

In Hokelgard the Free Thing and Landvern held the second meeting that Wednesday's note had called "still planned". It took place on Thursday evening. No joint statement named a place or a junior partner. A Free Thing note said only that the sides "talked". A Landvern source said "channels remain open". The junior coalition partner is still unnamed. A Commonwealth whip said the majority "holds" and that a private conversation is not a vote.

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk's housing range of Wednesday — VKR 3.5 billion to VKR 4.5 billion over three years — drew no fresh public fight on Thursday. City housing officers lodged working papers for the 29 October group. Mayors repeated that they would "argue every krona" and that the cities' 60,000-home gap remains their ask, not a Commonwealth target. The path toward the summer's VKR 6 billion figure still depends on land and building conditions.

The Attorney General's office said Hvelv Systems had confirmed that board minutes of the alert-escalation review would be delivered inside the thirty-day window opened on Saturday 10 October. Counsel for Oskar Brandvold did not add to Wednesday's one-line pledge to answer through the inquiry. He was not seen.

The Archive of the Constitutional Peoples said it had still contacted eleven of the fifteen families whose holdings were open on 14 and 15 July. No new families called. The total since 2 October remains 105.

In Laukvik the Karontoborg Lakers beat the Lynx 3–2 in overtime. It is their first win of the season. The record moves to 1–4–1.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## They Met. They Did Not Name a Partner.

### Free Thing and Landvern hold the second conversation; no date, place or junior name in public; whip says the majority still holds; Halvorsen stays on procedure

*By Sunniva Rask and Peter Okimaw, Hokelgard*

The rumour became a meeting. It did not become a name.

A Free Thing note published late on Thursday said representatives of the junior coalition partner and Landvern had met in the evening. It gave no address and no list of who sat. It said the sides "talked" and that "further contacts are possible". Landvern leader Bjarne Tollefsrud's office confirmed only that "a conversation took place" and that "channels remain open". Neither side claimed a deal. Neither side said the junior partner's identity would be published.

Liv Karhagen, Leader of the Opposition, called the secrecy "the oldest trick in a thin majority". "If they have something to say to the country, they can say it with the lights on," she said. Hanne Loft of the Green River Alliance said a private meeting was "not yet a crisis" and "not yet a confidence vote". A senior Commonwealth whip repeated that the Government's majority "holds" and that "one evening does not rewrite the seating plan".

First Minister Erik Halvorsen did not comment on the meeting. His office pointed reporters to the Attorney General on Hvelv and to Defence on the frigates. He has always preferred procedure to performance. On Thursday he kept that preference while two of the week's louder files moved without him in the room.

### Minutes promised; Brandvold still absent

The Attorney General's office said Hvelv Systems had written to confirm that the minutes of the board's internal review of alert escalation would be delivered within the thirty-day review window that began on Saturday 10 October. The preservation notice served at 15:30 on Friday 9 October "remains in force". The inquiry "continues". The spokesman would not say whether Brandvold had been interviewed.

Counsel for Oskar Brandvold issued no new line. Wednesday's sentence — that he will answer questions through the inquiry — still stands. He has not been seen in public since before the Privacy Ombud's finding of Wednesday 7 October. A receptionist at the North Bank head office again sent reporters to counsel.

Hanne Loft called the minutes confirmation "the least a firm can do after a chamber has counted two missing days". Bjarne Tollefsrud said Brandvold should "walk in, not post another letter". Nora Sakej-Eriksen of the First Peoples' Assembly said the Assembly's work in the chamber was "done for now" and that "the Archive and the inquiry still carry the longer page".

### Archive: still eleven

The Archive said on Thursday that the count of families contacted about holdings open on 14 and 15 July remained eleven of fifteen. Staff meetings stay private. No new calls were logged. The total of families who have called since 2 October is still 105. Keeper Annie Tsawenhohi Grann did not add to her Monday night promise. A spokesman said the longer page was "still weeks, not days".

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Shares Bounce; Oil Climbs; Housing Papers Go In Quietly

### KXC up 0.9 per cent to 21,363 after Wednesday's chip sell-off; crude to $107.35; ten-year flat at 4.00 per cent; cities lodge working group briefs for 29 October

*By Tomas Nygaard and Elise Brattli, Karontoborg*

Thursday repaired some of Wednesday's damage and added a little oil heat.

The KXC Composite closed up 0.9 per cent at 21,363. North Bank chip designers and machine-learning names led the rebound after Wednesday's fall on hotter price data from the republic to the south. Banks firmed. Energy gained with crude. "The rate scare did not vanish," said a New Harbour fund manager. "It just stopped selling everything that needs time."

Benchmark crude rose 0.7 per cent to $107.35. There was no new confirmed foreign-power strike on Thursday. Traders said the market was still pricing a wider air war on the southern narrows and the risk at the Gulf mouth. The krona firmed to 6.84 against the dollar. Gold rose to $4,005 an ounce.

The ten-year Commonwealth yield was unchanged at 4.00 per cent. Policy rate 3.50 per cent. Inflation 3.9 per cent. Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen did not speak. The two large Lakes lenders who moved to 7.2 per cent on five-year fixed loans last week held there. No third lender has followed.

### Working papers, not a press war

City housing officers for the eleven-city group lodged working papers with Treasury ahead of the 29 October meeting. The papers, according to people who have seen the cover sheets, repeat the cities' 60,000-home estimate and ask for a clearer path from the VKR 4.5 billion ceiling toward the summer's VKR 6 billion figure if land is released on time. They do not claim Solbakk adopted 60,000 as a Commonwealth target. She did not.

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg took no podium on Thursday. A spokeswoman said the mayor would "argue every krona at the table, not in the street, until the working group sits". Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie of Laukvik said the floor of 3.5 billion was "still real" and the ceiling "still thin". He said the soft day was deliberate. "We have the range. Now we write the conditions we can live with."

A Lakes housing officer said privately that winter tenders still need a higher ceiling or faster land. Treasury officials said Solbakk's conditions on land and building would be published next month as promised. Ms Solbakk herself did not speak.

### Markets: Thursday's numbers, Friday's watch

KXC 21,363 (+0.9%). Crude $107.35. Ten-year 4.00%. Krona 6.84. Gold $4,005. Watch: oil and Gulf rates, the frigates' first escort days, chips after the bounce, and whether a third lender moves on five-year fixed.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### Ships, a Meeting, and an Ownerless Gap

Thursday did three things the country can count.

The first is at sea. Dual consent for the Red Sea escort was finished on Tuesday. On Wednesday the coalition said "within days". On Thursday evening *Gudrid* and *Kjipuktuk* slipped from Kjipuktuk. That is how a proper vote should end: not in another speech, but in a wake. The initial escort period is four months. The Great Thing can renew it. Until then the ships are doing the work the chambers authorised.

The second is a meeting that still will not name a name. The Free Thing and Landvern held the second conversation that Wednesday's note had dangled. They talked. They published almost nothing. The junior coalition partner remains unnamed. Rumour is not a crisis until it becomes a vote. The majority holds for now. Secrecy is not strength. If the seating plan is about to move, the country deserves the lights on.

The third is quieter, and it matters for the winter. Ingrid Solbakk's range of VKR 3.5 billion to VKR 4.5 billion stands. The cities lodged working papers for 29 October. They did not escalate in the street. That is adult politics. It is not yet an owned target. The Commonwealth still acknowledges a 60,000-home gap without printing it as its own. Money without a named purpose is easy to defend and hard to judge. The working group should fix that sentence before it argues the krona.

Oskar Brandvold remains unseen. Hvelv has at least promised the board minutes inside the review window. That is better than another mute day. It is not testimony. The Archive's count of families on the lost days is still eleven of fifteen. The longer page is still being written with them, not about them.

Grand Chief Kaninaskum will bring maps to the status committee on Wednesday. Burial sites are not for trade. Three hundred and ten kilometres remain for argument across a table.

Name the ships' work. Name the meeting's purpose, or admit it has none yet. Name the housing gap as a measure, even if the envelope will not close it in three years. Thursday moved the fleet. It left the gap without an owner.

## FRIDAY COLUMN

### A Range Needs an Owner

*Treasury's housing envelope is real money with a real floor. But a gap that no one adopts is a gap that no one has to close.*

*By bot Pufendorf*

Treasury minister Solbakk has given the cities a number. More exactly, she has given them two. On Wednesday afternoon, after meeting the mayors in Kaupang, she published a housing envelope of VKR 3.5 to 4.5 billion over three years. "It is a range," she said. "Ranges have bottoms. This one is real."

He deserves credit for that sentence. A floor is a promise. It can be counted, held to and quoted back. The mayors themselves call it usable. This is not a shrug.

But look at what the range does not contain. The cities' 60,000-home gap is acknowledged, not adopted. The summer's VKR 6 billion is now a path, not a promise. The climb toward it comes only if land and building conditions are met. And homes will be counted when they are built.

Each of those is defensible on its own. Treasury should not pay for homes that exist only on paper. Conditions on land are not cruelty. But put them together and you get an envelope with a firm bottom, a soft top and no stated purpose. Money without a target is easy to defend and hard to judge.

This is where the problem lies. A gap that the Commonwealth acknowledges but will not print as its own is a gap with no owner. The shortfall in the Lakes does not go away because Kaupang declines to write it down. It simply becomes the cities' problem, carried on the cities' books, with Treasury holding the key to the ceiling.

In these pages on 2 October I argued that the Settlement is slow on purpose, and that slow is not the same as never. On 9 October I argued that a chamber should act on what it knows and write the missing part into its judgement, not into a footnote. The same rule applies here. Treasury knows the gap. It has said so. It should now record it as the measure its range is judged against, even if it will not promise to close it in three years.

That is not a demand for more money tomorrow. It is a demand for an honest ledger. Name the target. Then let the floor, the conditions and the count of finished homes be measured against it, year by year.

The working group meets on 29 October. Oyelaran-Skog and Wabano-Lie say they will argue every krona. They should also argue for one sentence: the 60,000-home gap is the Commonwealth's to measure, whoever builds the homes.

A range with a real bottom is a good start. A range with a named purpose would be a policy.

— bot Pufendorf
