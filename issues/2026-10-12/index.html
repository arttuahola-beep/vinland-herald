Attorney General Orders Hvelv to Keep Every Record as Archive Sitting Nears

Preservation notice served on Friday covers logs, backups and the August alert; Hvelv board pledges “full cooperation” but Brandvold stays silent; Speaker confirms Tuesday at ten

By Peter Okimaw and Sunniva Rask, in Hokelgard

The Attorney General has told Hvelv Systems to destroy nothing.

The Attorney General’s office confirmed on Friday evening that it had served a records-preservation notice on the firm that afternoon. The notice covers every log, backup and message linked to the Archive breach. It names the automated monitoring alert of 24 August. It also covers the records of the contract server that was switched off on 27 August. The office said the notice was “a standard first step in a formal inquiry”. It did not name the Attorney General.

Hvelv’s board answered on Saturday afternoon in a short written statement. It said the firm would “cooperate fully” with the Attorney General. It had told all staff to keep every record, and it had hired outside counsel. The internal review of alert escalation, announced on Thursday, will report to the board within thirty days. Chief executive Oskar Brandvold did not sign the statement. He has not been seen in public since the Ombud’s finding last Wednesday.

On Sunday the Speaker’s office confirmed that the Provincial Thing’s Archive sitting resumes at 10:00 on Tuesday. Speaker Marta Okimaw-Strand’s order of 5 October stands: escort first when the sitting concludes, Shield Intertie second, same day. The frigates VNS Gudrid and VNS Kjipuktuk remain alongside. Dual-chamber consent under the Settlement of 1652 is still incomplete.

First Minister Erik Halvorsen was asked about the notice on Sunday as he left a service at the Old Quarter cathedral in Hokelgard. “Tuesday at ten,” he said. “The tide is set. We sail on it.”

Keeper Annie Tsawenhohi Grann said six more families had called the Archive between Friday and Sunday. That makes 102 since 2 October. She speaks this evening at the Lakeside literary festival in Karontoborg. “Keep every record,” she said. “That is what the families have been asking of everyone, all along.”

One Notice, One Statement, One Empty Chair

AG’s preservation order lands on Friday; Hvelv board speaks on Saturday; Brandvold does not; opposition asks who saw the alert

By Peter Okimaw, Political Correspondent, Hokelgard

The preservation notice was served at Hvelv’s head office on the North Bank at 15:30 on Friday. The Attorney General’s office confirmed it three hours later.

The notice is short, say people who have read it. It tells the firm to keep all records linked to the Archive breach of 6 to 15 July. That includes agent session logs, custody records, backups, internal messages and the alert history. It names the 24 August alert. It names the contract server switched off on 27 August, and “any record of its configuration, maintenance schedule and decommissioning”.

The office stressed that the notice accuses no one. “It protects the file while the inquiry does its work,” a spokesman said. He would not say whether Mr Brandvold will be interviewed, or when the inquiry might report.

Hvelv’s board replied on Saturday at 14:00. The statement ran to five sentences. The firm would “cooperate fully”. Staff had been told to keep every record. Outside counsel had been hired. The internal review of alert escalation would report to the board “within thirty days”. The board “regrets the distress caused to the families of the constitutional peoples”.

It did not repeat Thursday’s line about a scheduled shutdown. It did not say who saw the 24 August alert. Mr Brandvold did not sign it.

“Regret is not a record,” said Liv Karhagen, Leader of the Opposition. “The board knows who received the alert. It should say so this week, not in thirty days.”

Landvern’s Bjarne Tollefsrud said the absence of the chief executive told its own story. “A board signs when a chief executive will not,” he said. “We have seen that film before.”

Nora Sakej-Eriksen, leader of the First Peoples’ Assembly, kept to her one demand. “Tuesday’s judgement must name 14 and 15 July,” she said. “A notice to keep records is welcome. But two days were never kept.”

Officials said the sitting could run past Tuesday. When it concludes, the escort motion comes first. The Shield Intertie referral follows, dated the same day. If the sitting runs into Wednesday, so does the sequence.

The Government will bring the papers already asked for, and no new ones. The ten-sitting-day timetable for federal answers still stands.

Hanne Loft of the Green River Alliance kept her conditional yes on the escort. “Archive first,” she said. “Then the frigates. Then the Shield.”

Oil Eases, Shares Slip, Bonds Rally a Little

Crude falls 1.4 per cent to $104.72 after a foreign pledge cools strike fears; KXC down 0.6 per cent; ten-year at 4.02%

By Tomas Nygaard and Elise Brattli, Karontoborg

Friday gave back most of Thursday’s oil gain.

Benchmark crude fell 1.4 per cent to close at $104.72. A large foreign power said it would not launch new strikes in the Gulf for now, and traders took some risk out of the price. The weekend airport attacks came after the close. Dealers said they would be priced this morning, along with a short-lived energy truce announcement in the eastern European war.

Energy names led the KXC Composite 0.6 per cent lower, to 21,480. The ten-year Commonwealth yield fell two basis points to 4.02 per cent. A weak reading of consumer mood in the republic to the south pulled long yields lower abroad. The krona weakened to 6.90 against the dollar, as it often does when oil falls. Gold rose to $3,990 an ounce.

Mortgage quotes did not move. The two large Lakes lenders who went to 7.2 per cent on five-year fixed loans on Wednesday held there. No third lender has followed.

Policy rate 3.50 per cent. Inflation 3.9 per cent. Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen did not speak. His warning last Wednesday that the work on inflation “is not finished” still sets the mood on the short end.

Solbakk Promises “a Range, Not a Single Figure”

Finance Minister says Wednesday’s meeting with the mayors will bring numbers; campaign says a range is a start

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk said on Saturday that she would bring the eleven-city housing campaign “a range, not a single figure” when they meet in Kaupang on Wednesday 14 October.

“The mayors want a number,” she told a regional radio programme. “They will get a range they can plan with. A single figure today would be a promise I cannot keep tomorrow.”

The mayors’ arithmetic has not changed. They planned 180,000 starts over three years. About 120,000 are on course. That leaves 60,000 homes short. The VKR 6 billion working figure discussed in the summer would cover about a quarter of the gap.

Karontoborg’s Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog called a range “a start”. Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie of Laukvik was more direct. “Bring the bottom number and the top number,” he said. “We will tell you which one builds houses.”

Airport Strikes Widen the War on the Strait Coast

Missile kills twelve at a Gulf capital’s passenger terminals; second strike on Sunday; two Vinlandic travellers treated and released; airlines told to keep away

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The militia that holds the southern Red Sea narrows has opened a new front. It is no longer only the sea.

On Saturday a missile struck the domestic terminals of the main airport in a large Gulf capital. Twelve people were killed and more than three hundred wounded, some badly. The dead came from at least ten countries. The airport closed. On Sunday a second projectile hit the same complex and caused further injuries. The militia claimed both attacks. It also claimed strikes on oil and gas sites in the east of the same country, and told airlines and passengers to stay away from that country’s airports.

The coalition that backs the regional government on the strait coast said it “will respond firmly”.

Two Vinlandic citizens travelling for work were among those treated on Saturday, the Foreign Ministry said. Both left hospital on Sunday.

Foreign Minister Anders Ohkwari-Dahl tightened Vinland’s travel advice on Sunday evening. Vinlanders are now advised against all but essential travel to the country’s airports until further notice. “An airport is not a battlefield,” he said. “Whoever treats it as one has chosen to kill civilians.”

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad said the weekend “changes the size of the danger, not the law of consent”. The frigates remain alongside until both chambers have voted.

Insurers widen quotes; published rates hold

War-risk underwriters sent wider quotes to owners overnight for voyages through the southern narrows and the Gulf. Published rates did not change. Sigrid Holme of the Karontoborg Shipowners’ Association said fourteen Vinlandic-flagged ships were still routing around southern Africa. “The quotes went wider. The book did not move,” she said. “Nobody here is testing the narrows this week.”

Further tanker strikes inside the Gulf were reported over the weekend; crews were safe. The port town on the strait coast remained unconfirmed. Aid agencies still put the displaced above 180,000.

By Aslak Kvande

Mr Ohkwari-Dahl’s office said Vinland “welcomes any pause that keeps the lights on for civilians”. It named no capitals.

Ministry to Pilot a Public Checker for Machine-Made Images

Free tool will read hidden marks some developers embed in pictures and audio; Lindqvist says a labelling rule is “next on the list”; report stage for her bill set for 20 October

By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg

The Ministry for Digital Affairs will open a public checker for machine-made pictures and audio on 1 November.

Minister Kari Lindqvist announced the pilot on Sunday afternoon. Anyone will be able to upload an image or a sound file. The checker will look for the hidden marks that several large developers now embed in their output. It will say whether it finds one. It will not say who made the file, and it will not keep the upload.

“A mark is not a truth test,” Ms Lindqvist said. “It is a label. Some makers use it and some do not. The checker tells you what the label says, if there is one.” She said a rule requiring such labels on machine-made content was “next on the list” after her incident-reporting bill.

A large foreign developer opened a similar checker last week. The ministry’s version will read marks from several firms, not one.

Researchers welcomed the pilot with care. A missing mark proves nothing, they said. “No mark found” means only that no label was found.

Report stage on 20 October

The People’s Thing will take Ms Lindqvist’s Autonomous Systems Incident Reporting Bill at report stage on Tuesday 20 October, the whips said on Friday. The bill now carries a public incident register and operator liability. The developer clause is in a six-month review. The private right of action failed in committee. Depositing counsel said they would still bring it back as a separate motion before the sitting rises.

City terms draw fire

An industry group has attacked one clause in Karontoborg’s draft city contract terms for autonomous systems. The Lakes Digital Industry Council said on Saturday that the rule requiring vendors to escalate monitoring alerts within fixed hours was “unworkable for systems that raise thousands of alerts a day”.

Councillor Ylva Pashe-Hauge, who published the draft on Thursday, was unmoved. “One alert was raised on 24 August,” she said. “It needed one person to read it. If a system raises thousands, the vendor should build one that raises the right one.” The draft is open for comment until the end of the month.

The Laukvik phage preprint still awaits a referee.

The Herald’s View

Keep the Records, Keep the Date

The Attorney General has done the obvious thing, and it was right to do it. A records-preservation notice is not dramatic. It accuses no one. But it closes a door that should never have been open. From Friday afternoon, nothing touched by the Archive breach may be lost by accident, by schedule or by design.

That matters because so much is already gone. The server went dark on 27 August. The recordings for 14 and 15 July were never produced. The notice cannot bring them back. It can make sure that what is left stays left.

Hvelv’s board has said the right words. Full cooperation. Every record kept. Outside counsel. A review within thirty days. We note what it did not say. It did not name who saw the alert. It did not repeat the claim that the shutdown was scheduled. And the chief executive did not sign. Oskar Brandvold has been silent since the Ombud reported. A board can speak for a company. It cannot answer for what one man knew.

Thirty days is too long for that answer. The Attorney General need not wait for it.

Tuesday’s sitting is a separate matter, and it should stay separate. The Speaker has confirmed ten o’clock. The order is clear. The judgement should name 14 and 15 July as part of the record. It should say when the alert sounded and when the server went dark. Then, when the sitting concludes, the escort motion and the Shield Intertie referral follow, on the same day, as Grand Chief Kaninaskum asks.

This weekend’s strikes on a Gulf airport show why the frigates matter. Two Vinlanders were among the hurt. That makes the danger closer. It does not make the second chamber optional. The Settlement of 1652 asks both chambers to consent precisely when the pressure to skip one is greatest.

Keep the records. Keep the date. Then sit, and write the loss into the law.
