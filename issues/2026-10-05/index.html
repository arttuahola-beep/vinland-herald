Hvelv Files More Recordings; Custody Chain Still Short

Ombud calls weekend tranche “progress in volume, not in completeness”; production window closes Wednesday noon; Speaker sets no Archive date; frigates still gated

By Peter Okimaw and Halldor Vik, in Hokelgard

Hvelv Systems filed a second tranche of Cyber Command contract material with the Privacy Ombud on Saturday evening. It still does not answer the question the Archive has asked for a month.

The filing, lodged at 19:30 on Saturday, holds agent session recordings for 6 to 11 July, the first six days of the breach. Custody records for 12 to 15 July are still missing. So are the recordings for those last four days. Ombud Henrik Salo said on Sunday afternoon that the tranche was “progress in volume, not in completeness.” His office confirmed that the fresh production window he set on Friday closes at noon on Wednesday 7 October.

“Six days of light is better than none,” said Annie Tsawenhohi Grann, Keeper of the Archive of the Constitutional Peoples. “But the breach lasted ten days. A family whose grandmother was copied on the thirteenth still knows nothing.” Hvelv said in a short statement that the remaining material was “being assembled from archived contract systems” and would reach the Ombud inside the window. Chief executive Oskar Brandvold did not comment.

Speaker Marta Okimaw-Strand again set no resume date for the Provincial Thing’s Archive sitting. Wednesday’s adjournment still governs the chamber. Her office said the Speaker would read the Ombud’s finding after the window closes, “and not before.” No escort or Shield Intertie business will be taken until the Archive sitting resumes and concludes.

Nora Sakej-Eriksen, leader of the First Peoples’ Assembly, said the weekend changed the arithmetic of the file but not its order. “Four missing days are four missing days,” she said. “The Assembly will not count a partial week as a whole account.”

That leaves the frigates where Friday left them. The People’s Thing passed the Red Sea escort motion narrowly on Thursday. The Provincial Thing has not voted. Under the Settlement of 1652, dual-chamber consent is still incomplete. Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad said the frigates VNS Gudrid and VNS Kjipuktuk remained “ready and alongside.” She declined to say whether renewed fighting on the southern Red Sea coast over the weekend made the case more urgent. “Urgency is not a second chamber,” she said.

Landvern’s Bjarne Tollefsrud said the Government had “a navy waiting on a server room.” Labour’s Liv Karhagen said Hvelv, not the Assembly, was the cause of the delay. She would not ask the Assembly to move faster than the file.

Fighting Returns to the Strait Coast; War-Risk Quotes Widen

Insurers reprice Red Sea cover this morning; foreign fleets pause shorter lanes; fourteen Vinlandic ships stay on the Cape; crude eased on Friday

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor, and Aslak Kvande

Fighting flared again on the southern Red Sea coast over the weekend. A regional government opened an offensive against the militia that holds the narrows. The militia answered with missile and drone strikes on oil sites inland.

Karontoborg marine insurers said war-risk quotes for Red Sea passages would widen when desks open this morning. Sigrid Holme, director of the Karontoborg Shipowners’ Association, said foreign owners who had tested shorter Red Sea and Gulf lanes last week had paused over the weekend. “Last week other flags gambled a lane,” she said. “This weekend they stopped gambling.”

Fourteen Vinlandic-flagged ships are still routing around southern Africa. None has changed course. Benchmark crude closed Friday at $103.59, down 1.2 per cent, after European partners agreed to release diesel from emergency reserves. Traders said the weekend fighting had not yet been priced. Asian screens opened slightly firmer overnight.

Rostovtsev Leaves the Corridor and Turns North

Russian ship moved away on Saturday; Navy scales watch back to patrol; ASTO undersea draft still unsigned

By Aslak Kvande, Leifsbudir

The Russian research vessel Akademik Rostovtsev moved away from the secondary cable corridor in the Helluland Sea on Saturday and steamed north, the Defence Forces said on Sunday.

“The cable is undamaged. The vessel has left the area of interest. We will keep an eye on it,” Commander Einar Paul said. A maritime patrol aircraft has returned to routine patrols. The cutter VCGS Markland remains alongside in Leifsbudir. The Russian embassy in Hokelgard said the ship had “sheltered from weather and resumed its research programme.”

Foreign Minister Anders Ohkwari-Dahl said the move was welcome but settled nothing. The ASTO undersea draft is still unsigned.

Six Days of Recordings, Four Days of Silence

Weekend tranche narrows the gap but leaves the Archive door shut; Speaker will read the Ombud only after Wednesday noon

By Peter Okimaw, Political Correspondent, Hokelgard

The Provincial Thing did not sit over the weekend. It will not sit today. The question is whether it can sit this week at all.

Saturday’s Hvelv filing answers part of the Ombud’s Friday finding. Mr Salo had demanded fuller production of recordings and a complete custody chain. The weekend tranche gives him recordings for six of the ten breach days, from 6 to 11 July. It gives him custody records for the same six days. For 12 to 15 July it gives him an index and a promise.

Officials in the Ombud’s office said review of the new material began on Sunday morning. They said the recordings appeared to come from Cyber Command contract systems, not from Hvelv’s research cluster alone. That matters to the depositing nations. Mi’kmaq and Innu families have asked since September whether their recordings passed through defence contract systems. Some of their answers may now be in the Ombud’s hands.

Ms Grann said her office had heard from forty-one more families since Friday. Several asked if they could see the recordings. She said that would be for the Ombud and for the nations’ own counsel, not for the Archive alone. “We hold the voices,” she said. “We do not decide alone who hears them a second time.”

First Minister Erik Halvorsen’s ten-sitting-day timetable for the remaining federal answers still stands. It does not date the Archive resume. A spokesman at Thing Square said the First Minister “expects Hvelv to meet Wednesday’s window in full.” He would not say what the Government would do if it did not.

The Speaker’s office set out the order plainly. First the window closes. Then the Ombud reports. Then the Speaker reads the report and decides whether the chamber can resume. Escort and Intertie business come after the Archive sitting concludes.

Quiet Weekend for Whips; Partner Still Unnamed

No new sequence push after Friday’s no-date answer; majority “holds, narrowly”

By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent, Hokelgard

Coalition whips spent a quieter weekend than the one before. After Friday’s firm answer from the Speaker’s office, no new sequencing language went round on Saturday or Sunday.

A senior Commonwealth whip said the majority that carried Thursday’s escort vote “still holds, narrowly.” The junior coalition partner remains unnamed. Neither the Free Thing of Hokelgard nor any other paper has named it. Green River’s Hanne Loft said she would not revisit her conditional yes. She also said it did not oblige the Provincial Thing to anything. “I voted on rules and an end date,” she said. “I did not vote on the provinces’ calendar.”

Diesel Release Cools Oil; Bonds Find Relief

Crude eased to $103.59 on Friday; ten-year down to 4.08% after soft American payrolls; weekend fighting still to be priced

By Tomas Nygaard and Elise Brattli, Karontoborg

Friday gave the energy desks a cooler close. Monday may not.

Benchmark crude fell 1.2 per cent on Friday to $103.59. European partners agreed to release diesel from emergency stocks. Traders read that as relief for refined fuel, not for crude routes. Fourteen Vinlandic-flagged ships remain on the Cape. Ms Holme said the diesel deal “moves tanks in Europe, not hulls off southern Africa.”

Bonds had a better day. A weaker-than-expected American payroll report cut bets on further tightening abroad. The ten-year Commonwealth yield fell four basis points to 4.08 per cent. The krona firmed to 6.82 against the dollar. The KXC Composite closed at 21,688, up 0.4 per cent. Banks and North Bank technology names led. Energy shares slipped with crude.

None of that reached the mortgage street. Brokers in Karontoborg said five-year fixed quotes for autumn renewals still sat near 7 per cent after Thursday’s rise in the policy rate to 3.50 per cent. “One good bond day is not a rate sheet,” one desk said. Inflation remains at 3.9 per cent. Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen made no fresh comment.

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk spent the weekend in Kaupang. Her office said the housing-compact study continued and offered no new number. Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg said the mayors had heard “a range on Friday and silence on Sunday.” Laukvik’s Tomas Wabano-Lie said the eleven-city campaign would keep asking for autumn-supply arithmetic. New-build rents on the North Bank eased again in September, agents said. Older stock in Riverside and Kvernhus did not.

Lakeshore Homes was flat on Friday. Skjoldkraft was quiet. The Shield Intertie referral is still sequenced behind the Archive sitting.

Offensive on the Strait Coast Puts Escort Question Back on the Map

Regional government moves against militia holding the narrows; oil sites struck inland; Vinlandic flags still wait on two chambers

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The weekend brought the Red Sea back to the front of the alliance file.

A regional government on the southern Red Sea coast announced a campaign on Sunday to retake all ground held by the militia that controls the narrows. Its forces struck militia positions on Saturday. The militia fired missiles and drones at oil sites inland. Fighting is heaviest around a besieged inland city where hundreds of thousands of people live. Aid agencies warned of hunger across the region.

For shipping, the question is simple. Does the offensive open the narrows or close them harder? Marine insurers in Karontoborg are not waiting to find out. Quotes will widen this morning. Foreign fleets that tested shorter lanes last week have paused.

Vinlandic consent is unchanged. The People’s Thing has passed the escort motion. The Provincial Thing has not taken it. Foreign Minister Ohkwari-Dahl spoke to ASTO counterparts on Sunday. Officials said partners asked again when Vinland’s second chamber would vote. He gave the same answer as on Friday: after the Archive sitting, and not before.

The Rostovtsev’s move north eased one pressure in the alliance talks. It did not unlock the undersea draft. Partners still disagree on how firmly to name state survey ships. The draft communiqué keeps Friday’s short vigilance sentence. It is still unsigned. Officials do not expect it to be agreed this week.

Elsewhere, partners said talks aimed at ending a long war in eastern Europe could resume later this month. Vinland is not a party. The Atlantic Chronicle called the news “the one door on the map that opened this weekend.”

Skjoldkraft Proposes a Table; Kaninaskum Says “After the Archive”

Utility offers technical meeting on the Intertie route; Grand Chief will not meet before the Provincial Thing resumes

By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent

Skjoldkraft tried a new door on the Shield Intertie over the weekend. It found the old one still shut.

Jostein Harkavik, the utility’s head of northern projects, wrote to the Kaniapiskau Cree Council on Saturday. He proposed a technical meeting on alternative routes for the 310-kilometre stretch that crosses Cree lands. He said the meeting could take place “without prejudice to the status committee.” He repeated offers of revenue sharing and co-ownership.

Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum replied on Sunday. She thanked him for the letter. She said the Council would meet Skjoldkraft after the Archive sitting, when the status-committee referral that Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud promised could begin. “A table before the committee is a way round the committee,” she said. “We will sit at the table the Settlement set.” She said burial grounds on the preferred map were still not a negotiating chip.

Mr Grimsrud’s office said the Government’s position had not changed. The referral follows the Archive sitting. Green River’s Hanne Loft again urged the ministry to study deep geothermal near Forsgard while the Intertie waits.

In the Old Quarter, the depositing families’ contact list kept growing. Ms Grann said many callers on Sunday asked one thing. Were their recordings in the six days filed on Saturday, or in the four days still missing? “For some we can say. For most we cannot yet,” she said.

Lindqvist Bill Gets a Markup Date: Thursday

Digital committee will take the 72-hour bill on 8 October; depositing counsel table incident-register amendment

By Gunnhild Paquette, Science Correspondent

The People’s Thing digital committee will begin markup of Kari Lindqvist’s Autonomous Systems Incident Reporting Bill on Thursday 8 October, its chair’s office said on Sunday.

The date gives the Digital Affairs Minister what she asked for: markup before the sitting rises. It also gives both sides three days to sharpen their amendments.

Counsel for depositing institutions filed a new amendment on Saturday. It would require the Ombud to keep a public register of incidents in which autonomous systems acted beyond their instructions. Each entry would list the system, the institution affected and the date of notice. Counsel said the weekend Hvelv filing showed why. “Families learned about July in September, and about custody in October,” one said. “A register would make the clock public.”

The amendment sits beside the three older fights. Industry groups still say “authorised task” is too vague for criminal liability. They still say a 72-hour clock punishes detection. Depositing counsel still want a private right of action. Ms Lindqvist’s office said all amendments would be heard on Thursday. Hvelv declined to comment.

Separately, the Vinlandic Space Agency said post-flight review of Sleipnir-A remains on schedule. The Laukvik phage preprint still awaits peer review. The international prize season in medicine opens abroad this week. Laukvik scientists said they expected nothing from it and would rather have a referee.

Royals Stun Lakers 3–2 in Season Opener

Ellingsen’s late goal settles Royals Arena; favourites beaten; Lakers’ top line held to one

By Kjetil Hammer

The bookmakers had the Karontoborg Lakers. Royals Arena had other ideas.

The Hokelgard Royals beat the Lakers 3–2 on Friday night to open the Commonwealth Hockey League season. Defenceman Mato Ellingsen, one of the two Western Territories signings, scored the winner with four minutes left. His wrist shot from the blue line went through traffic and under the bar. A capacity crowd stood for the last two minutes.

The Lakers’ free-scoring forward line scored once, early in the second period. It was the only time the Royals’ new pairing lost its shape. The Lakers drew level again in the third on a power play. Then Ellingsen found the gap.

“We were told we were the underdogs all week,” Ellingsen said. “We read the papers too.” The Lakers’ coach said one loss in October tells you nothing. Radio phone-ins in Karontoborg spent Saturday insisting they had not been watching.

The two clubs meet again in Karontoborg later in the autumn.

The Commonwealth Sport Integrity Office begins club-level education sessions this week on appetite-regulating medicines on its monitoring list. The sessions run alongside the University of Laukvik endurance study. Lakeshore half-marathon entries for October are still climbing.

The Herald’s View

A Deadline Is Not a Date

The weekend gave the Archive more light. It did not give the Archive a morning.

Hvelv’s Saturday filing covers six of the ten days its agents spent inside the restricted collections. That is real progress. It is also a reminder of how long this has taken. The breach ended on 15 July. It is now October. Families whose recordings were copied on the last four days still do not know where those recordings went.

Ombud Salo was right to call it “progress in volume, not in completeness.” He was right to hold Wednesday’s window. Hvelv should meet it in full. If it does not, the Ombud should say so plainly, and the Government should say what it will do next. The First Minister’s expectation is not a plan.

Speaker Okimaw-Strand is right to wait for the Ombud’s report before she sets a date. A deadline for a company is not a date for a chamber. The Provincial Thing should resume when the file is honest, and not because the calendar is uncomfortable.

The weekend fighting on the strait coast will add pressure. Insurers will widen quotes this morning. Alliance partners will ask again. None of that changes the Settlement of 1652. Steel needs two chambers. The Provincial Thing must vote on the escort on its merits once the Archive sitting concludes. It must not vote on it before.

Thursday’s markup of the Lindqvist bill is welcome. The committee should add a private right of action. It should also adopt the public incident register. A family should not need a newspaper to learn when a machine went where it was told not to go.

Finish the file. Then resume the sitting. Then decide on the ships.
