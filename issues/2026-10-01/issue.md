# PAGE ONE

## People's Thing Passes Escort Motion Narrowly; Dual Consent Still Incomplete

### Loft restores conditional yes after overnight read of dawn ROE; FPA still blocks Provincial Thing; fourteen ships remain on the Cape

*By Halldor Vik and Sunniva Rask, in Hokelgard*

The People's Thing passed the Red Sea escort motion this morning by a narrow margin after Green River co-leader Hanne Loft restored her party's conditional yes.

Ms Loft told reporters at 08:10 that an overnight and early-morning reading of Wednesday's unclassified rules package had satisfied the party's conditions on rules and a fixed end date. "The text is clear enough to vote," she said. "It was not clear enough at dawn yesterday. Consent requires a chamber that has seen the page." Labour held its conditional yes and kept housing-compact language for autumn supply on the table. Landvern voted no. Kjell Arvidsen, voting with his party, called the escort "a war of paperwork while rent eats the pay packet."

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad called the result "a serious mandate from the popular chamber." She urged the Provincial Thing to take the file once the Archive sitting resumes. First Peoples' Assembly leader Nora Sakej-Eriksen answered at once. "There is no Provincial Thing escort business until the Archive sitting resumes and finishes," she said. "A People's Thing vote is half of 1652. It is not the whole." Speaker Marta Okimaw-Strand's Wednesday adjournment order still stands. No resume date has been fixed.

Opposition benches split on the meaning. Labour's Liv Karhagen said the popular chamber had done its duty and that housing language on autumn supply would now be tested in earnest. Landvern's Bjarne Tollefsrud said a narrow yes "is not a blank cheque for a second chamber that has not finished the Archive." Green River's restored support was enough to carry the motion. It was not enough to quiet the dual-chamber argument.

Under the Settlement of 1652, Vinlandic consent for the deployment of VNS *Gudrid* and VNS *Kjipuktuk* requires both chambers. Today's narrow pass does not complete it. A Commonwealth whip said the majority for Thursday's business "held, and held narrowly." The junior coalition partner remains unnamed. The Free Thing of Hokelgard has not named it either.

Fourteen Vinlandic-flagged vessels are still routing around southern Africa. Karontoborg Shipowners' Association director Sigrid Holme said a half-consent "does not shorten a Cape voyage." France's escort organisation continues. Foreign Minister Anders Ohkwari-Dahl briefed ASTO partners that the popular chamber had spoken and that the Provincial Thing remained on Archive first.

## Monetary Council Lifts Policy Rate to 3.50%

### Mikaelsen cites sticky services and shelter; oil's soft print "not decisive"; mortgages still near 7%

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor, Karontoborg*

The Monetary Council raised the policy rate by 25 basis points to 3.50 per cent this morning, as markets had priced.

Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen said services and shelter inflation remained sticky. Softer oil prints this week were "welcome for households and not decisive for the forecast." Inflation stands at 3.9 per cent. The ten-year Commonwealth yield reacted modestly after the statement. Mortgage brokers in Karontoborg said five-year fixed quotes for autumn renewals were still clustering near 7 per cent.

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk made no comment on the decision. She was in Torsvik for a town-hall stop on the mayors' housing-compact tour. Labour finance spokesman Oddvar Ruud said the hike "confirms what Lakes mortgages already know." Landvern called it "another bill for a government that finds frigates faster than bricks."

Benchmark crude closed Wednesday at $103.10, down 0.3 per cent, as Gulf export flows through a Red Sea bypass route continued. The krona was little changed immediately after the statement.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## Partial Logs Reach Ombud; Archive Gallery Still Waiting for a Date

### Compulsory orders served; Speaker holds the adjournment; Intertie and frigates remain in the corridor

*By Peter Okimaw, Political Correspondent, Hokelgard*

The Provincial Thing did not sit today. Wednesday's adjournment still governs the chamber.

Mr Salo's morning service of compulsory production orders met the first of the two conditions the Speaker named at noon on Wednesday: orders served, or missing Cyber Command logs produced. Hvelv's late-afternoon partial tranche met neither standard in the eyes of the First Peoples' Assembly. "Metadata is not a voice," Ms Grann said outside the Old Quarter. "An incomplete custody chain is not an account."

Ms Sakej-Eriksen thanked the Ombud for serving the orders and rejected any suggestion that the People's Thing frigate pass should reorder Provincial business. "Half of 1652 does not erase the Archive," she said. First Minister Erik Halvorsen's ten-sitting-day timetable for remaining federal answers remains on the table. It still does not date the full Cyber Command file.

Landvern's Bjarne Tollefsrud said the narrow escort vote "proves a majority that can count to one chamber and not to two." Labour's Liv Karhagen said the popular chamber had spoken on ships and that housing language on autumn supply would follow. She repeated that Labour would not ask the Assembly to jump the Archive queue.

Depositing families who travelled for Wednesday's gallery returned to empty public benches today. Several asked the Keeper's office when the next morning would be. Ms Grann said she did not yet know.

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Rate Lift Lands as Solbakk Softens Again in Torsvik

### Governor delivers the priced 25bp move; town-hall mayors want numbers before supply week

*By Tomas Nygaard and Elise Brattli, Karontoborg and Torsvik*

Governor Mikaelsen's statement this morning was short and dry. Services inflation has not eased enough. Shelter costs in the Lakes remain elevated. Oil at $103.10 on Wednesday's close does not rewrite the forecast. The policy rate is 3.50 per cent.

The ten-year yield moved only modestly after the decision. Banks steadied. Lakeshore Homes was little changed. Mortgage desks said the 7 per cent cluster for five-year fixed quotes held through the morning. "Households already paid for this hike in the quotes," one Karontoborg broker said. "The Council caught up with the street."

In Torsvik, Finance Minister Solbakk faced a polite but firm town hall on the mayors' housing-compact tour. She repeated that federal proposals were under "active study, not shelf study." She offered no hard guarantee on municipal borrowing-cap relief or a federal building guarantee. Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie of Laukvik, travelling with the tour, said tone was still ahead of arithmetic. Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg said autumn supply would need numbers, not adjectives. Torsvik's mayor thanked the minister for coming and asked when study becomes a line in a bill.

Local councillors pressed on three-year waits for family flats and on vacant lots within sight of the hall — the same contrast that marked Monday's Sundgard stop. Ms Solbakk said the Finance Ministry was examining the mayors' ten-year compact as a package, not as a press line. She would not pre-empt the Monetary Council's morning decision and did not comment on it after it landed. Lakeshore Homes said it remained "encouraged by attendance, guided by arithmetic."

Skjoldkraft shares were quiet. The utility's northern projects team is still waiting for a Provincial Thing status-committee referral on the Shield Intertie. That referral remains sequenced after the Archive sitting — which still has no resume date.

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## Navy Confirms Rostovtsev Closer Approach; Cable Undamaged

### Ship closed toward a secondary corridor overnight then turned away; ASTO undersea draft still not agreed

*By Aslak Kvande, Leifsbudir*

The Defence Forces confirmed this morning that the Russian state research vessel *Akademik Rostovtsev* closed distance overnight toward a secondary undersea cable corridor in the Helluland Sea, then turned away.

"The cable remains undamaged. The vessel altered its track and remains of interest. Our watch continues," Commander Einar Paul said. The confirmation upgrades Wednesday's unconfirmed harbour talk in Leifsbudir, which had claimed a brief second-cable approach. The Navy will not describe the overnight move as a boarding risk or as damage. The cutter VCGS *Markland* remains alongside in Leifsbudir. A maritime patrol aircraft continues the track.

The Russian embassy in Hokelgard called the watch "provocative" and the ship's work "routine oceanographic research." Foreign Minister Ohkwari-Dahl said Vinland would keep pressing ASTO partners for undersea-infrastructure language. Officials briefed after Wednesday's foreign ministers' meeting said the draft communiqué was still narrower than earlier in the week and still not agreed. Partners again asked when Vinlandic dual-chamber consent on the Red Sea escort would be settled. Mr Ohkwari-Dahl pointed to this morning's People's Thing pass and to the Provincial Thing's Archive-first order.

Fourteen Vinlandic-flagged ships are still taking the Cape. Restored Gulf export flows through a Red Sea bypass route helped keep oil near flat on Wednesday. "Half a consent in Hokelgard does not move a hull off southern Africa," Ms Holme said. Shipowners want a vote they can count in both chambers, published rules a crew can brief against, and an end date that survives a second sitting. Today's People's Thing pass meets one of those tests. It does not meet the Settlement's two-chamber rule.

# PAGE SIX — SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY

## Partial Production Intensifies Markup Fight on Lindqvist Bill

### Industry still objects to 'authorised task'; depositing institutions press for a private right of action

*By Gunnhild Paquette, Science Correspondent*

Hvelv's partial afternoon response to the Ombud's compulsory orders sharpened overnight arguments on Kari Lindqvist's Autonomous Systems Incident Reporting Bill.

Industry groups repeated that "authorised task" is too vague for criminal liability and that a 72-hour clock would punish detection rather than concealment. Civil-liberties lawyers and counsel for depositing institutions repeated that bodies such as the Archive should have a private right of action when protocol-restricted collections are copied. Ms Lindqvist's office said both arguments would be heard in committee markup and that she still wanted the bill advanced before the sitting rises. Hvelv declined to comment on the bill beyond confirming compliance with today's orders.

Pressure around disclosure duty has moved from the chamber gallery into the markup notes. Counsel on the North Bank read the Ombud's service as proof that voluntary timelines failed. Depositing-nation lawyers read the partial tranche as proof that compulsory force must be matched by enforceable remedies for the institutions that hold voices in trust. Committee members said overnight notes already flag three fights: the definition of an authorised task, the length of the reporting clock, and whether a private right of action belongs in the bill or in a later statute. Ms Lindqvist still wants markup before the sitting rises. Industry still wants the criminal edges dulled. The Archive's counsel still wants a door into court.

Separately, the Vinlandic Space Agency said post-flight review of Monday's *Sleipnir-A* orbital insertion remained on schedule. No further anomaly had been identified beyond the ascent engine issue that shortened the mission. The Laukvik phage preprint still awaits peer review.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### Half a Consent Is Not Consent

The People's Thing did its job this morning. It read the rules. It voted. The margin was narrow and the mandate is real — for one chamber.

It is not Vinlandic consent under the Settlement of 1652. Ms Sakej-Eriksen is right. The Provincial Thing remains adjourned on the Archive. Frigates and the Shield Intertie wait in the corridor until that sitting resumes and finishes. A dawn dump on Wednesday and a restored Green River yes on Thursday do not reorder constitutional business. Custody of voices held in trust is not a queue that ships may jump because an alliance timetable is restless.

Mr Salo was right to serve compulsory orders. Hvelv's partial afternoon tranche is better than silence and less than an account. Metadata without a custody chain does not tell a depositing family whether a grandmother's voice left the building. The Speaker should set a resume date when the Ombud can say the production is adequate — not when ministers find the escort file inconvenient. Ms Lindqvist's 72-hour bill should give depositing institutions a private right of action. Voluntary disclosure failed. Soft definitions of "authorised task" will fail next.

The Monetary Council was right to lift the rate to 3.50 per cent. Oil near $103 does not erase 3.9 per cent inflation or Lakes shelter costs. Ms Solbakk's Torsvik repetition of "active study" remains tone. Tone does not build flats. Labour is entitled to attach housing arithmetic to autumn supply. Landvern is entitled to vote no on ships. Neither argument excuses skipping the Archive.

Publish the remaining logs. Resume the sitting. Then finish dual-chamber consent on its merits — or withhold it on its merits. Do not pretend one chamber is two.
