## Mill Pause Put Back to January as Contract Lands Without a Number

### Sørstrand delays its strip-line pause to 4 January; the contract promises "a minimum" of training places but names none; Landvern decides today

*By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent*

Sørstrand Steel has put back the pause at its Laukvik mill by more than a month.

The company told the union and the city on Friday that one of its two strip lines will now stop on 4 January, not on 1 December. It said it wanted to see the steel contract settled first. About 380 workers would still go on short time when the pause begins.

A few hours later the Government laid the standard contract before the People's Thing. It says the steel makers must train "a minimum number" of young workers. It does not say how many. A covering note from the Finance Ministry promises the number "before the statement vote".

That vote is now set for Thursday. Landvern, whose votes the Government needs, meets this morning to decide what it will do. Last week its spokesman said it wanted the number in the contract.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## A Contract With a Promise, and a Gap

### The standard contract sets out local hiring in detail but leaves the training number to a covering note; the vote on the autumn statement is set for Thursday

*By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent*

The standard contract for the steel package was laid before the People's Thing at about three o'clock on Friday afternoon. It runs to forty pages. Members have the weekend behind them and four days ahead before they vote.

The contract covers the first VKR 4.5 billion of the VKR 14 billion package. It sets the terms on which steel and vehicle makers receive public money.

The local-hiring clause is long and precise. Firms must report how many of their new workers live within a set distance of the plant. They must report every six months. If they fall short, part of the money can be clawed back.

The training clause is shorter. It says that each firm must train "a minimum number" of young workers in each year of the contract. The minimum is to be "set by the Minister". The clause gives no figure.

### The covering note

A two-page note from the Finance Ministry was laid with the contract. It says the Minister "will set the minimum before the statement vote". It does not say whether the minimum, once set, will be added to the contract or published in a separate order.

That is the question the union asked last week. A number in the contract can be enforced in the courts. A number in a ministerial order can be changed by the next minister.

A Finance Ministry spokesman said on Sunday that the minimum would have "the same force as the contract". He did not say how.

### The vote

The Government has set the vote on the autumn statement for Thursday 26 November. The Finance Ministry will publish its note on how the package fits the Central Bank's forecast on Tuesday. That is two days before members vote, not on the day.

## Solbakk: "The Money Is for Steel and Homes"

### The Finance Minister defends the VKR 105 billion on Sunday radio; the Bank "has the numbers", she says

*By Sunniva Rask*

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk said on Sunday that the Government's borrowing next year was "for steel and homes, and for nothing else".

She was speaking on a Sunday morning radio programme. She was asked about the Central Bank's decision on Thursday, when the Monetary Council held rates by four votes to three. The Governor had said the Commonwealth's VKR 105 billion of borrowing was "in our forecast".

"The Bank has the numbers," Ms Solbakk said. "It has had them since Tuesday. On Tuesday this week the People's Thing will have our note on how they fit. Members can read it before they vote."

She was asked whether the extra VKR 4 billion of borrowing would make a rate rise more likely. "That is a question for the Bank," she said. "What I can say is what the money buys."

Liv Karhagen, the Leader of the Opposition, made no comment over the weekend. Bjarne Tollefsrud of Landvern also said nothing new.

## Landvern Meets This Morning

### The group said it was "close" if the training number was in the contract; the contract has a promise instead

*By Sunniva Rask*

Landvern's members meet at nine o'clock this morning in the People's Thing. They will decide how to vote on the autumn statement on Thursday.

On Thursday last week a spokesman set a condition. "We want to see a training number in the contract," he said. "If it is there, we are close. If it is not, we will want to talk."

The number is not there. A promise of a number is.

The group could back the statement, oppose it, or ask the Government for talks before Thursday. It has said it will vote for the first VKR 4.5 billion of the package in any case. The statement as a whole includes the later parts, which Mr Tollefsrud has called "three chances to cut".

The Government needs Landvern's votes to keep its majority.

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## The Treasury Shows Its Working

### All thirteen sites accepted, all at half weight; a four-page method defines a "slow site" for the first time

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

The Treasury's claims table sat for just under two hours on Friday morning. When it rose, it had accepted every one of the thirteen federal sites that Karontoborg put forward.

None was rejected as not ready. But each will count at half weight when the Treasury works out how many homes the city can build. That is the present rule, and the Treasury applied it.

What was new was the paper that came with the table. For weeks the Treasury had said only that "the method is the method". On Friday it published the method.

### What a slow site is

The four-page note defines a slow site as land where the Commonwealth expects building to start more than three years after a claim. It says federal land is usually slow for three reasons. Other departments must first agree to give it up. Old buildings must often be cleared. And the land must be valued before it is sold.

The note says that, on past figures, homes on federal land have taken about twice as long to start as homes on city land. That is why it counts for half.

It also says that the rule "will be reviewed in the light of the claims now made". It gives no date for the review.

### The Mayor's reply

Mayor Oyelaran-Skog said on Friday afternoon that the table was "a start".

"They counted all thirteen. That matters," she said. "Now we will read their method. Our maps show pipes and roads already in the ground. If a site is ready, it is not slow. We will ask them to count again, site by site."

She said the city would write to the Treasury once it had read the note.

## Sørstrand Waits for the Contract

### The strip-line pause moves to 4 January; about 380 workers still face short time; the Laukvik Mayor says the town has "five more weeks to make the case"

*By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg*

Sørstrand Steel, the steelworkers' union and the town of Laukvik met at the mill on Friday morning. The meeting lasted three hours.

When it ended, the company said it would delay the pause of one of its two strip lines. The line will now stop on 4 January, not 1 December.

"We have listened," a company spokesman said. "We want to see the contract settled before we make a final decision. A month gives everyone time."

The pause has not been cancelled. About 380 workers would still go on short time once it begins.

Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie, who attended in person, said the town had "five more weeks to make the case". "It is not the answer we wanted," he said. "It is a better answer than we had on Thursday."

### The union's view

The union said it would give its view of the final contract today. It spent the weekend reading the text laid on Friday.

"We asked for a number," a union official said on Saturday. "We have been given a promise of a number. We will say on Monday what we think that is worth."

The union has not yet recommended a vote to its members. It hopes to count their votes within a week of its recommendation.

## Shares Fall Again as Computing Slide Deepens

### KXC loses 1.1 per cent; the largest foreign index has its worst week in more than eighteen months; gold falls $78

*By Tomas Nygaard*

The KXC Composite fell 230 points, or 1.1 per cent, to 20,170 on Friday. Computing shares again led the fall.

Abroad, the selling was much heavier. The largest foreign index fell nearly 4 per cent on Friday, its seventh fall in a row. It ended its worst week in more than eighteen months. Its computing-heavy rival fell more than 5 per cent and is now lower than at the start of the year.

Traders there said a large listing of a computing company had made investors take profits rather than buy more.

"At home we fell by a little. Abroad they fell by a lot," one Karontoborg trader said. "We are not a computing market. This week that was a comfort."

Investors bought Commonwealth bonds. The ten-year yield fell four basis points to 3.82 per cent. The krona was steady at 7.00 to the dollar and 8.00 to the euro.

Gold fell $78 to $3,654 an ounce. It has fallen for most of the month.

Benchmark crude fell 0.3 per cent to $110.46 a barrel. Oil prices abroad rose a little on Friday, but fell for a third week.

### Mortgages

One Lakes lender raised its five-year fixed rate to 7.4 per cent on Saturday. It is the first to go above 7.3 per cent. Most others still quote 7.2 or 7.3 per cent.

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad will make a statement in the People's Thing on Tuesday. Her office said so on Friday evening.

Last week she briefed the defence committee in private. Its chair said she gave "a clear account". Members from several parties then said the country should hear what the committee had heard.

The statement will cover the Red Sea escorts. The office did not say whether it would cover a third escort. Last week Ms Bjornstad said none was planned this month.

The committee gave its consent to the escorts on 1 October. Since then rebel fighters have taken the heights above the strait and reached the coast west of the main southern port.

### On the coast

Over the weekend rebel forces shelled the edge of the main southern port. No deaths were reported. The government said its line on the road to the port still held.

The strait itself was quiet. No attacks on ships were reported there.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### A Minimum Is Not a Number

Last week this newspaper asked for four things: a training number in the contract, the Treasury's method, a Defence Minister in the chamber, and the Finance Ministry's note before the vote. By Sunday we had three of the four. That is worth saying. So is what is still missing.

### The method

The Treasury has published its working. It took weeks, and it should not have. But the note is clear. It says why federal land counts for half, and it gives the past figures behind the rule. Karontoborg can now argue with the numbers instead of with a silence. That is how a rule should be tested. The Treasury should now give a date for its review.

### The chamber

Ms Bjornstad will speak in the People's Thing on Tuesday. Good. The committee heard her in private. On Tuesday the country will hear her too. She should say plainly whether the consent of 1 October still fits a coast that has changed. She should also say what would have to happen before a third escort sails.

### The number

The contract says each firm must train "a minimum number" of young workers. It does not say how many. The Finance Ministry says the Minister will set the number before Thursday's vote. Her spokesman says it will have "the same force as the contract".

If that is so, it costs nothing to write it into the contract. A minimum that a minister sets can be changed by the next minister. A number in a signed contract cannot be. The difference is not a lawyer's point. It is the difference between a promise and a term.

Sørstrand has given Laukvik five more weeks. Landvern decides this morning. The union speaks today. Each of them is waiting for the same figure. The Minister should name it today, and put it in the contract.
