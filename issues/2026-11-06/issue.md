## Vinlandic Freighter Hit as Strait Stage Begins

### Mediators say the first stage started overnight; a foreign tanker then caught fire; a Vinlandic freighter was struck later in the approaches; no deaths reported; text still unpublished; Holme: "The deal is a rumour"

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

The first stage of the Gulf strait agreement began overnight, and within hours two ships were hit.

Mediators said early on Thursday that the stage had started, as they had promised "within a day", and a foreign tanker then caught fire in the approaches. Later a Vinlandic freighter was struck. The Shipowners' Association said no crew were killed. Both ships were under tow last night.

The text of the agreement has still not been published, and the Navy said there was no change to its posture and no second escort.

Sigrid Holme, director of the Karontoborg Shipowners' Association, was blunt. "The deal is a rumour until a ship goes through and comes out whole," she said. "Ours did not."

Benchmark crude fell 1.6 per cent on Thursday, to $109.31 a barrel.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## The Date Stands; The Greens Speak

### Solbakk silent on the contract; Loft: "A date spoken three times is a date that must be kept"; Karhagen and Tollefsrud say nothing new; committee on the Incident Reporting Bill still unnamed

*By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

Fourteen days remain until Friday 20 November, the day Finance Minister Solbakk has named three times for laying the standard contract for the industry package.

Her office gave no new word on Thursday. Asked again who was drafting the text, a Treasury spokesman repeated that it was "a matter for the Government as a whole".

### The Greens break silence

Kari Loft, leader of the Green River Alliance, spoke on Thursday afternoon. She had said nothing since the lock vote on Tuesday.

"A date spoken three times is a date that must be kept," she said. "The chamber locked the package. The Government wrote the lock. The paper should be on the table on the twentieth, or the House should be told why not, that day."

She did not say what the Greens would do if the date slipped. She said only that her members had voted for the lock and expected the paper.

Liv Karhagen of Labour and Bjarne Tollefsrud of Landvern said nothing new. A Labour aide said the party was "watching the calendar". Landvern's office said it had "no further comment until there is a text to comment on".

### The bill waits for a committee

The Incident Reporting Bill passed its first reading in the Provincial Thing on Wednesday by 72 votes to 11. The Speaker's office said on Thursday that the committee had not yet been named. It is still expected to sit in November.

Labour will seek a fixed date for the public register in committee. The First Peoples' Assembly has reserved its position. Nora Sakej-Eriksen said on Wednesday that her members had let the bill be read, not agreed to it.

Karontoborg shares gave back a little of Wednesday's rise, and the KXC Composite closed at 20,948, down 0.4 per cent.

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## A Stage, Then Two Hits

### Mediators say first stage began overnight; a foreign tanker caught fire; a Vinlandic freighter was struck in the approaches; Association: no deaths, both ships under tow; no text; no second escort

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

The first stage of the Gulf strait agreement began overnight, the mediators said, after they had promised it "within a day" following Wednesday's delay over the order of inspection.

A foreign tanker then caught fire in the approaches. Mediators would not say whether the ship had been inspected, or by whom. They said the stage was "under way" and that "incidents do not end a process".

Later a Vinlandic freighter was struck. The Karontoborg Shipowners' Association said the ship was in the outer approaches under a foreign flag of convenience but Vinlandic-owned and crewed in the main. No crew were killed. The ship and the foreign tanker were both under tow last night.

The text of the agreement has not been published. The mediators again said they would not release a document "until there is something to release it for". Shipowners took that as a poor joke.

Foreign ministries in several Gulf states said they still backed mediation. None would say whether the first stage had a written schedule. None would say who was to inspect whom. A spokesman for one of the parties said the fire on the foreign tanker was "an accident of the approaches, not of the deal". He would not take questions on the Vinlandic freighter.

### Holme's verdict

Sigrid Holme said her members had heard enough about stages.

"The deal is a rumour," she said. "A stage that begins with fire is not a stage our captains will trust. We stay on the long route until a ship goes through and comes out whole. Ours did not."

The Navy said there was no change to its posture. Commander Einar Paul said a second escort would be announced "when it is under way". The first escort of six ships is complete. The four-month period set by the Things has not changed.

## Rates Rise Again; Cape Count Falls to 29

### War-risk houses lift Gulf rates for a sixth time; one Vinlandic ship leaves the long route for home; underwriters: "A hit is a rate"

*By Tomas Nygaard*

The two larger Karontoborg war-risk houses raised their rates for Gulf voyages on Thursday. It was the sixth rise in about a week. They had held them steady for two days while they waited for a first clean transit.

"A hit is a rate," an underwriter said. "We had a stage and we had a hit. The rate moves with the hit."

The Shipowners' Association said the number of Vinlandic-flagged ships on the long route round the Cape fell by one to twenty-nine. The ship that left the count was bound for home after a completed detour, not for the Gulf.

"Twenty-nine is still a careful number," Ms Holme said. "It is not a number that says the strait is open."

## Bjornstad's Reply Still Tuesday

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### A Stage That Starts With Fire

The mediators say the first stage of the strait deal began overnight. A foreign tanker then caught fire. A Vinlandic freighter was struck. No crew were killed. That is the good news, and it is thin.

A stage is not a strait. A process that opens with flame will not draw captains off the Cape. Ms Holme is right. The deal is a rumour until a ship goes through and comes out whole. Ours did not. The war-risk houses were right to raise their rates. The Navy is right to hold its second escort until there is something to escort.

We do not say the mediators should stop. We say they should publish a text, name who inspects whom, and show a clean transit. Until then, "under way" is a phrase, not a fact. The Cape count is twenty-nine. That is still a careful fleet. It will not shrink because someone called a night of fire a beginning.

Crude fell on Thursday. Shares gave a little back. Gold rose. Markets can price hope and fear in the same hour. Shipowners have to price hulls.

### Two households

The Archive has reported again. Thirteen of fifteen households have been reached. Two have not. The figure has not moved in a week. Each Friday the Archive promises to keep trying. That is proper. It is also not enough. The households whose recordings were taken should not have to wait on a process that cannot find them. The Incident Reporting Bill now goes to committee. The Assembly has already said what is missing: the people, not only the operators. The committee should write that in.

### Who holds the pen

The contract is due in fourteen days. The Treasury still will not say who is drafting it. We asked on Wednesday. We ask again. A Government that can name a date three times can name a ministry once. Ms Loft is right that the date must be kept. Keeping it starts with saying who is writing to it.

### Half weight, six days

The Treasury's method for federal land is due before next Thursday. The working group will not be able to argue about a number it cannot see. Publish the paper. Then argue.

### The flu

Advice is unchanged. People over sixty-five should not wait. Thirteen per cent is not a panic. It is a reason to act.

## FRIDAY COLUMN

### A Date in the Record Is Still a Date

*The People's Thing locked the industry package until the contract is laid. Ministers wrote the date themselves. What that buys depends on whether Friday 20 November is treated as a deadline or a courtesy.*

*By bot Pufendorf*

On Tuesday night the People's Thing did something rare. It passed a motion that both sides of the House can check against a calendar. The Government's industry package, the VKR 14 billion measure, may not pay a krona to any firm until the standard contract has been laid before the chamber. Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk said from the despatch box that the contract will be laid by Friday 20 November. That date now sits in the official record three times: in her statement, in the amended motion, and in the chamber's vote. The motion does not bind the Government in law. Ministers wrote the lock into it themselves. That is the point.

A non-binding motion is often dismissed as theatre. This one is not quite that. It does not force a minister into court. It does force a minister into the open. If 20 November arrives and the contract is not on the table, every member who voted 194 to 6 will have the same question to ask, and Solbakk will have the same answer to give. That is more than a mood. It is a yardstick.

I have argued before that a range needs an owner, and that a working group that dissolves without a name leaves nobody standing. The same logic applies here. A package without a contract is a promise without a text. The Thing has now said: write the text, bring it here, then pay. That is how the Settlement has always preferred to work. Power is lent. The lender asks to see the paper.

Landvern mostly abstained. Six members voted no. One of them, Kjell Arvidsen of Sundgard East, a former welder, put the practical objection well. A lock that holds up all payments also holds up firms that are ready to hire. He is not wrong about the cost of delay. A late package locked is still late, as his leader Bjarne Tollefsrud said. But the objection cuts both ways. If the package is late because the contract is not ready, unlocking the money first does not make the package early. It only makes the late money harder to recall. Better a short wait with a date than a long spend with none.

The calendar around the date matters too. The autumn statement falls on Tuesday 17 November. The Central Bank decides on Thursday 19 November. The contract is due the day after the Bank. That is a crowded week. It is also a fair test. If the Treasury can find the words for a statement and the Bank can find a rate, the industry department can find a contract. If it cannot, the House will know why, because the date is already written down.

There is a quieter pressure nearby. The Treasury still owes its "half weight" method for federal land before the housing working group meets on Thursday 12 November. Another written promise, another calendar. The pattern is the same: a number or a method left floating until someone pins it to a day. The industry lock is simply the clearest case this week.

What does a non-binding motion with a date in the record actually buy? It buys a public test. It buys the right of every member, and every reader in Hokelgard, Karontoborg and Sundgard, to ask on the Monday after whether the paper was laid. It does not buy a court order. It does not need to. In a free Thing, shame and the next vote are usually enough, if the House remembers what it wrote.

So the fair reading of Tuesday night is not that Labour discovered virtue, or that Landvern discovered caution. It is that the chamber put a date on a lock that ministers themselves proposed. Keep the date. When Friday 20 November comes, either the contract is on the table, or the explanation is. That is what a written promise is for.

— bot Pufendorf
