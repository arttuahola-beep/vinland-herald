# PAGE ONE

## Landvern Expects a Third Meeting as Solbakk Spells Out Housing Terms

### Tollefsrud tells Sunday radio "the door is open"; junior partner still unnamed; Finance Minister names land, completion and rent tests for the VKR 4.5 billion ceiling; Archive reaches thirteen of fifteen families

*By Sunniva Rask, Peter Okimaw and Elise Brattli, in Hokelgard and Karontoborg*

Landvern expects to talk again.

Bjarne Tollefsrud, the Landvern leader, told a Sunday morning radio programme that a third conversation with the junior coalition partner was "likely, and soon". He would not give a date or a place. He would not name the party. "The door is open," he said. "We did not open it alone." Thursday evening's meeting in Hokelgard was the second. Neither side has published who sat at the table. The junior partner did not comment on Sunday. A senior Commonwealth whip said the Government's majority "still holds".

In the same hour, on a different station, Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk set out the terms behind her housing range of VKR 3.5 billion to VKR 4.5 billion over three years. The floor is paid when a city signs a land plan. The rest comes in stages, tied to serviced land inside twelve months, finished homes and a quarter of homes let below market rent. She again declined to adopt the cities' 60,000-home gap as a Commonwealth target. But Treasury will now print its own count of finished homes every year beside the cities' figure. "The cities own their estimate," she said. "We own our money." The working group meets on 29 October.

The Archive of the Constitutional Peoples said on Saturday that it had now contacted thirteen of the fifteen families whose holdings were open on 14 and 15 July. On Friday the count was eleven.

The frigates *Gudrid* and *Kjipuktuk* are on passage across the Atlantic. Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad briefed party leaders behind closed doors on Saturday. First Minister Erik Halvorsen, asked about the ships on Sunday, said: "They sail on the consent of both chambers. That is the question before us, and it has been answered."

Landvern did not wait for the Free Thing this time. Its leader went to a microphone himself.

Bjarne Tollefsrud spent twenty minutes on a Sunday morning politics programme from Hokelgard. He said Thursday's meeting with the junior coalition partner had been "serious and long" and that a third was "likely, and soon". The junior coalition partner issued no statement over the weekend. Its office did not answer calls on Sunday afternoon. The Herald has still not been able to confirm its identity, and does not print rumour as fact.

A senior Commonwealth whip said the majority "still holds" and that every Government bill this week "has its votes". "A radio studio is not a division lobby," he said.

"That is an auction with the lights off." Hanne Loft of the Green River Alliance said a third meeting was "still not a vote" but that "the junior partner now owes the chamber a sentence".

First Minister Erik Halvorsen did not take a question on Landvern on Sunday. He spoke briefly to reporters on the steps of the Thing House about the frigates and then went inside. His office later said the First Minister "deals with majorities in the chamber".

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Chips Drag the KXC Down; Oil Climbs Again

### Composite falls 2.0 per cent to 20,936 and about 2.5 per cent on the week; crude up 1.1 per cent to $108.51; ten-year at 3.98 per cent; a North Bank cloud firm shelves a bond

*By Tomas Nygaard and Elise Brattli, Karontoborg*

Friday undid Thursday's bounce and then some.

The KXC Composite closed down 2.0 per cent at 20,936. It lost about 2.5 per cent over the week. North Bank chip designers fell hardest. Cloud and data-centre firms followed. Dealers said the selling began abroad, where chipmakers have fallen for days in a row. "The question used to be how fast these firms could grow," said a New Harbour fund manager. "Now it is who pays for the buildings, and when they pay back."

Benchmark crude gained 1.1 per cent to $108.51 on risk at the Gulf mouth and the southern narrows. Early on Monday, in Asian trade, crude gave back about one and a half per cent after a sanctions waiver abroad that could free more fuel for sale.

The ten-year Commonwealth yield fell two basis points to 3.98 per cent as money moved out of shares and into bonds. It ended the week four basis points lower. The krona was unchanged at 6.84 against the dollar and slightly weaker against the euro at 7.93. Gold rose to $4,030 an ounce.

Policy rate 3.50 per cent. Inflation 3.9 per cent. Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen did not speak. The two large Lakes lenders who moved to 7.2 per cent on five-year fixed loans held there. No third lender has followed.

### Glimra shelves its bond

Glimra Compute, a North Bank cloud firm, said on Friday afternoon that it had deferred a planned VKR 2.4 billion bond. The money was meant to pay for a second data hall near Forsgard. The company cited "market conditions" and said the project itself "is not cancelled". Its shares fell 6 per cent. A credit analyst in New Harbour said other firms with building plans "will be watching the price of money more than the price of chips this week".

## Solbakk: "The Cities Own Their Estimate. We Own Our Money."

### Finance Minister sets out the terms behind her range; floor paid on a land plan; ceiling only if first-year land targets are met; Treasury to print its count of finished homes beside the cities' figure every year

*By Elise Brattli*

Ingrid Solbakk did not change her numbers on Sunday. She explained how they work.

The Finance Minister told a Sunday radio programme that the floor of her three-year housing envelope, VKR 3.5 billion, would be "paid on signature of a land plan". A city signs a plan, and the first money moves. Everything above that comes in stages. Four tests apply.

First, land. Cities must bring serviced public land, with permits, inside twelve months of each grant. Second, completion. The last payment in every stage is made only on finished homes. "We pay for roofs," she said, "not for renderings." Third, rents. At least a quarter of the homes built with Commonwealth money must let below market rent. Fourth, the count. Treasury will publish its own count of finished homes each year, beside the cities' estimate of what is missing.

The ceiling of VKR 4.5 billion is reached only if the first year's land targets are met. The summer's VKR 6 billion remains "a path, not a promise".

She was asked whether she would now adopt the cities' estimate of 60,000 missing homes as a Commonwealth target. She would not. "The cities own their estimate," she said. "We own our money. The count belongs to everyone. If the distance between the two figures does not shrink, everyone will be able to read that. Including me."

Full written conditions will be published next month, as promised.

### Mayors: the quarter is fine; the twelve months is not

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg said on Sunday evening that the rent test was "the right test". The land test was the hard one. She pointed to old rail and port yards in the Lakes that sit on federal books. "If Treasury wants our land in twelve months, it can start by handing over its own."

Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie of Laukvik welcomed the annual count. "A number printed every year is harder to ignore than a number in a letter," he said. The cities will bring the land question to the working group on 29 October.

A Treasury official said federal land would be "part of the conversation". Ms Solbakk did not say so herself.

## On Passage, and Briefed

### Bjornstad tells party leaders the frigates are on schedule; position kept quiet; rules unchanged; Halvorsen: "That is the question before us, and it has been answered"

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

VNS *Gudrid* and VNS *Kjipuktuk* spent the weekend at sea on passage across the Atlantic.

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad briefed party leaders on Saturday morning in a closed room at the Defence Ministry in Hokelgard. Afterwards she said only that both ships were "on passage, on schedule and in good order". She would not give a position. She would not say when the frigates will reach the escort area. "We will tell the country when they arrive," she said. "We will not tell anyone where they are on the way."

Foreign Minister Anders Ohkwari-Dahl said partner navies were assembling the escort group and that the unclassified summary of the rules of engagement, shared with party leaders in late September, still governs. The initial escort period remains four months unless the Great Thing renews it.

Liv Karhagen said leaders were told "what we needed to know and no more". Hanne Loft called it "adequate, not generous". Landvern sent a deputy.

On Sunday afternoon First Minister Erik Halvorsen spoke for less than a minute on the steps of the Thing House. "The ships are on passage," he said. "They sail on the consent of both chambers. A crew does not need speeches from the shore. That is the question before us, and it has been answered." He took no further questions.

## Airport Reopens; One Tanker Hit at the Gulf Mouth

### Flights resume in full in the Gulf capital; monitors confirm one damaged tanker after two mine claims; north-shore power says its plan to reopen the strait is "still on the table"; rates hold, quotes widen; twenty-three ships on the Cape

*By Halldor Vik and Elise Brattli*

The airport in the Gulf capital reopened in full on Saturday morning, a week after the missile strike that killed fourteen people and hurt about 320. Screening is heavier. The two Vinlandic citizens hurt in that attack remain out of hospital. The Foreign Ministry's advice against all but essential travel to the country's airports stays in place for now.

At the Gulf mouth, the regional power on the north shore said two tankers had struck mines in the strait on Saturday while using what it called unauthorised routes. Independent maritime monitors confirmed that one tanker had been damaged and brought to a halt. Its crew was accounted for and safe. They could not confirm the second claim. The same power's foreign minister said on Sunday that its seven-day plan to reopen the strait to normal traffic was "still on the table". Partner governments did not respond in public.

Partner briefings said the large foreign power's air campaign against the militia that holds the southern Red Sea narrows continued through the weekend. Aid agencies still put the displaced on the militia coast above 180,000.

Karontoborg's war-risk underwriters left their published Gulf rates unchanged. Brokers said individual quotes for transits near the Gulf mouth widened on Friday afternoon. One more Vinlandic-flagged ship switched to the Cape route over the weekend. The count is now twenty-three. Sigrid Holme of the Karontoborg Shipowners' Association said owners were "pricing every voyage one at a time now".

## Two Days to the Status Committee

### Intertie sitting opens Wednesday; order of business still to come; Kaninaskum's maps ready; Grimsrud's officials "will listen"

*By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent*

The Provincial Thing's status committee on the constitutional peoples opens its first sitting on the Intertie on Wednesday 22 October. The committee clerk had not published an order of business by Sunday night. Officials expect it on Tuesday.

Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum of the Kaniapiskau Cree Council did not speak publicly over the weekend. A council spokeswoman said the maps marking the two burial sites on the preferred route were "ready and packed". The Grand Chief's line from Thursday stands: the burial sites are not for trade, and the line is still three hundred and ten kilometres across Cree lands.

Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud's office repeated that Government officials would attend "to listen". Skjoldkraft, the state power company, will bring route maps. Its head of northern projects, Jostein Harkavik, has said the burial sites "were never on our list of things to move".

The referral was dated on Tuesday 13 October under Speaker Marta Okimaw-Strand's order of 5 October. A status committee does not decide a route. It decides whether the constitutional peoples' status is touched, and on what terms. Under the Restoration of 1652, that question belongs to the Provincial Thing.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### Terms in the Daylight, Talks in the Dark

Two senior politicians went on Sunday radio. Only one of them said something the country can check.

Ingrid Solbakk's terms are a policy. The floor is paid on a land plan. The rest comes in stages. The last money in each stage goes only to finished homes. A quarter of those homes must let below market rent. The ceiling is reached only if the first year's land is ready. One can argue with every test. The twelve-month land rule is tight, and the mayors are right that the Commonwealth sits on old yards of its own. But each test can be read, measured and held to.

Ms Solbakk also moved, a little, on the point these pages have pressed. She still will not adopt the cities' 60,000-home gap as her target. But she will print Treasury's count of finished homes every year beside it. That is not ownership of the gap. It is a public ruler laid next to it, and a printed distance is one someone must explain. The working group on 29 October should build on it, and settle the federal land question before it argues the krona.

Bjarne Tollefsrud's weekend was something else. He says a third meeting with the junior coalition partner is likely. He will not say what is on the table or name the party. The party itself said nothing. The whip says the majority holds, and on the division lists it does. But a governing party that meets the opposition twice in a week, and lets the other side do all the talking, owes the chamber more than silence. If it is negotiating, it should say so. If it is not, it should say that. Two meetings and no sentence is not prudence. It is drift.

The First Minister said on Sunday that the frigates sail on the consent of both chambers. He is right, and Saturday's briefing was handled properly. That is the standard the coalition should apply to itself.

Elsewhere the slow work goes on. The Archive has reached thirteen of fifteen families. Hvelv's board minutes are still not with the Attorney General, and the window closes on 9 November. Oskar Brandvold is still unseen. On Wednesday the status committee sits on the Intertie, with the burial-site maps on the table.

Terms in daylight can be judged. Talks in the dark can only be guessed at. The junior partner should step into the light before the guessing becomes the story.
