“We Were Heard. We Were Not Answered.”

Cree Council speaks to the status committee in public for two hours; members then sit in private; chair promises a date for the finding “before the end of the month”

By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent, Hokelgard

The status committee of the Provincial Thing heard the Kaniapiskau Cree Council in public on Wednesday. It was the first open session on the Shield Intertie since the Council gave its written answers on 29 October.

Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum spoke and answered questions for about two hours. The committee then cleared the room and sat in private for the rest of the afternoon.

At the end of the day the chair made a short statement. The committee would set a date for its finding, the chair said, and would do so “before the end of the month”.

Ms Kaninaskum was not satisfied. “We were heard,” she said on the steps of the Provincial Thing. “We were not answered. Those are two different things, and the committee knows it.”

Treasury: Half Weight for All Federal Land

Officials tell Karontoborg that serviced sites will count at half, like the rest; Oyelaran-Skog: “Then the method is not about time”; working group meets today

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor

The Treasury will not count serviced federal land in full. Its officials told Karontoborg so on Wednesday, a day before the housing working group meets.

Karontoborg had argued that sites with water, sewers and a road already in place should count in full. The Treasury said every federal site would count at half weight, serviced or not.

“Then the method is not about time,” Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said. “It is about something else. We would like to know what.”

Half Weight, Serviced or Not

Treasury officials tell city that pipes in the ground do not change the count; city calls the answer “a new reason in an old coat”; working group meets this morning; thirteen sites go to the claims table tomorrow

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor

Karontoborg went to the Treasury on Wednesday with a simple case. It came away with a simple answer, and it did not like it.

City officials met Treasury officials ahead of today’s housing working group. The city’s case was this. The Treasury’s own method paper says federal land counts at half weight because it is slow to build on. It is slow because it lacks water, sewers and roads. So a federal site that already has water, sewers and a road should not be slow. It should count in full.

The Treasury did not agree. “Half weight applies to all federal land,” a Treasury spokesman said after the meeting. “The method looks at federal land as a whole. We will not count site by site. That way lies an argument about every pipe.”

The city’s reply

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said the answer did not fit the paper.

“The paper says half weight is a judgment about time,” she said. “Fine. We showed them sites where no time is lost. They said half weight anyway. Then the method is not about time. It is about something else. We would like to know what.”

A city official put it more sharply. “It is a new reason in an old coat,” the official said.

What happens next

The housing working group meets this morning. It brings together the Treasury, the large cities and the provinces. Karontoborg will make the same case there. It needs other cities to back it.

On Friday thirteen federal sites go to the claims table. That is where cities say which land they will count towards their targets under the federal housing compact. If half weight holds, Karontoborg will need roughly twice as much federal land to reach the same number of homes.

The Treasury spokesman said the working group was “the right place for this discussion”. He did not say whether the Treasury would move.

The Union Will See the Draft

Finance Ministry agrees to show the steel union and Laukvik the standard contract “a few days” before it is laid on 20 November

By Tomas Nygaard

The steelworkers’ union and the city of Laukvik will see a draft of the industry package’s standard contract before it goes to the People’s Thing.

The Finance Ministry said on Wednesday that it would share the draft with both “a few days” before Friday 20 November. It did not give a date. It said the draft would be shared “in confidence”.

The contract sets the terms for every firm that takes money from the VKR 14 billion package. No money can be paid until it is laid.

“A few days is not a long time to read a contract,” a union official said. “It is better than reading it in the newspaper.”

Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie’s office called it “a fair step”. Sørstrand Steel, the union and the city meet in Laukvik on the same Friday. One of the plant’s two strip lines is due to pause on 1 December. About 380 workers would go on short time.

Mikaelsen: “The Evidence, on the Day”

Governor’s few words give no hint; decision in a week

By Elise Brattli

Central Bank Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen broke his silence on Wednesday evening, but said little.

Speaking at a dinner of the Lakes bankers in Karontoborg, he was asked about next week’s decision. “The decision will be made on the evidence, on the day,” he said. “Not before, and not at dinner.”

The Fires Are Confirmed

Party to the Gulf war says two tankers burned on an “unauthorized route”; no Vinlandic ship involved; insurers take back about half of Monday’s cut

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

One of the parties to the Gulf war confirmed on Wednesday that two foreign tankers had caught fire near the strait on Tuesday. It said they had used an “unauthorized route”. It did not say how the fires began. It gave no word on the crews.

Reports of a third tanker striking a mine were not confirmed. No Vinlandic-owned or Vinlandic-flagged ship was involved, the Shipowners’ Association said.

The talks did not resume. One of the governments involved said it would not give way on what it called “the central question”. It did not say what that was. The mediators did not speak. The text of the strait agreement is still unpublished.

Insurers move

The two larger Karontoborg war-risk houses took back about half of Monday’s cut on Wednesday. Rates for the Gulf rose by about 4 per cent. On Monday they had fallen by about 8 per cent, the first cut since the crisis began.

“We said the cut could be taken back,” one underwriter said. “The fires were confirmed. So we took back half. We kept the other half because no Vinlandic ship has been hit since last Thursday.”

Holme: “Half a kindness”

Sigrid Holme of the Shipowners’ Association was dry about it.

“On Monday they called it a small kindness,” she said. “Today it is half a kindness. Our captains are on the long route. They will stay there.”

The count of Vinlandic-flagged ships on the long route round the Cape was unchanged at thirty-one.

Two Hours in Public, Then the Doors Close

Kaninaskum tells the status committee the Council’s families “have already agreed how to agree”; members ask about the 310 kilometres, the burial grounds and a second route; chair: date for the finding “before the end of the month”

By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent, Hokelgard

The committee room of the Provincial Thing was full on Wednesday morning. Some of the people in the public seats had driven from the north overnight.

Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum sat alone at the witness table. Two elders of the Kaniapiskau Cree Council sat behind her. She spoke without notes for about twenty minutes.

What she said

“You asked us two things,” she told the committee. “Who holds the ground. And how our families say yes or no. We told you who holds the ground. We told you, in writing, how our families agree. We answered in a day, because the answer is old. Our families have already agreed how to agree. They did it long before this committee was formed.”

She said the Council did not ask the committee to approve the line or to stop it.

“We ask you to find one thing,” she said. “Does this line touch our status? If it does, then the Provincial Thing must decide, as the Restoration says it must. If it does not, say so, and tell us why. Either way, tell us.”

The questions

Members asked about three things.

The first was the route. The proposed Shield Intertie would cross 310 kilometres of Cree land. One member asked whether any families along it had already said yes. Ms Kaninaskum said that was “in the written answers, which you have”. She would not say more in public.

The second was the burial grounds near the route. A member asked whether the Council would accept a route that went around them. “A route that goes around the dead is better than one that goes over them,” she said. “It is not the same as consent.”

The third was the second route. Skjoldkraft, the utility that would build the line, has not said what a second route would add to household power bills. Energy Minister Grimsrud has said it would cost the company an extra VKR 1.1 billion. “Money is not this committee’s question,” Ms Kaninaskum said, as she said in October. “It may be someone’s question. Not yours.”

Behind closed doors

At about half past twelve the chair asked the public and the press to leave. The committee sat in private until late afternoon. The chair’s office did not say whether the Council’s representatives stayed.

The chair then read a short statement. The committee had “heard the Council with care and with gratitude”, it said. It would “set a date for its finding before the end of the month”. It did not say when the finding itself would come.

“Not answered”

Ms Kaninaskum spoke to reporters on the steps of the building.

“We were heard,” she said. “We were not answered. Those are two different things, and the committee knows it. A date for a date is not a date. We will wait. We have waited before. But we are counting.”

Skjoldkraft gave no new figure on Wednesday. No route has been chosen.

Freezers by Hand

Attack locks the control systems at seven cold stores; staff check temperatures with handheld gauges through the night; deliveries to shops delayed; company will not say whether a ransom was asked

By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg

Seven cold-storage warehouses in the Lakes lost their computer control systems early on Wednesday after what their owner called “a criminal attack”.

The warehouses belong to Kaldhus Logistics, which stores frozen and chilled food for shops and restaurants across the Lakes. Its sites are in Karontoborg, Laukvik and Sundgard.

The attack began at about three in the morning. Screens at the warehouses went dark. The systems that watch and set the temperature in the cold rooms stopped reporting.

The night shift

The freezers themselves kept running. But for several hours nobody could see the temperatures on a screen. Staff walked the cold rooms with handheld gauges and wrote the readings on paper. Engineers set some units by hand.

“We went back to clipboards,” a Kaldhus spokeswoman said. “It worked. No food was lost. Nothing went above its safe temperature.”

Deliveries to some shops were delayed by several hours on Wednesday. The company expected them to be normal by today.

Who did it

The company would not say whether a ransom had been demanded, or whether it would pay one. Cyber Command said it was helping the company “at its request”. The police have opened an inquiry.

An official at the Ministry for Digital Affairs said cold stores were an easy target. “They are full of machines that were built to be reliable, not to be secure,” the official said. “Many of them were connected to the network years ago, for convenience. Convenience is a door.”

The Ministry did not say whether other firms had been hit.

The Herald’s View

Heard, and Then?

The status committee did one thing well on Wednesday. It heard the Kaniapiskau Cree Council in public. For months the important parts of this matter have happened behind closed doors: the record of the holders, the written answers, the committee’s reading of them. On Wednesday the country could at last hear the Grand Chief’s case in her own words. That was right.

Then the doors closed again, and the committee came out with a promise of a date for a date.

A date, not a season

We have asked for dates all autumn. We asked the Treasury who would draft the contract, and when. We asked the Defence Minister when her review would report. The answers came slowly, and often as seasons rather than days. The status committee has now joined the habit. “Before the end of the month” is the date by which it will choose a date.

Ms Kaninaskum is right that this is not an answer. The Council gave its record in October. It gave its written answers in a day. It came in person when asked. The committee has had nearly two weeks with those answers. It should be able to say now when it will decide.

Reasons in public

There is a second point, and it matters more. When the committee does find, it should give its reasons in public.

We do not ask it to publish the record of the holders. That record belongs to the families, as the Council has said from the start. We do not ask for the written answers either. But the committee’s reasoning is another thing. Whether this line touches the status of a constitutional people is a question about the Settlement itself. The Restoration gave the Provincial Thing its veto so that such questions would be settled openly, by the chamber, and not quietly by a ministry. A finding without reasons would be a ministry’s way of working in a chamber’s clothes.

Half weight

The Treasury said in its own paper that half weight was “a judgment about time”. On Wednesday it told Karontoborg that a serviced site, where no time is lost, still counts at half. Both cannot be the whole story. If the Treasury has another reason, it should say what it is at today’s working group. If it has none, it should count serviced sites in full.

The cold stores

Kaldhus Logistics deserves credit for its night shift. Clipboards kept the food safe. But the warning should be heard well beyond one company. A great deal of the machinery that feeds the Lakes was connected to the network for convenience and never made secure. The Ministry for Digital Affairs should say whether other firms were hit, and what it expects the rest to do.

The draft

The Finance Ministry will show its draft contract to the steel union and to Laukvik “a few days” before 20 November. That is a fair step, and we asked for it yesterday. A few days is short. The ministry could make it a week.
