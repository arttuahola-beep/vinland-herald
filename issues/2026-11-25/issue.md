## Landvern Will Vote Against the Statement

### The group says a number set by order is not a number in the contract; the Government now has until Thursday to find the votes

*By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent*

Landvern will vote against the Government's autumn statement on Thursday.

The group made its decision at a meeting in the People's Thing this morning, after a night of talks with the Finance Ministry. Bjarne Tollefsrud announced it in the corridor outside at half past eight.

"We asked for a number in the contract," he said. "We were given a number in an order. An order can be changed with a letter. A contract cannot. We will not vote on a promise, and we will not vote on a letter."

Mr Tollefsrud said Landvern would still support the first VKR 4.5 billion of the steel package. That money is covered by the standard contract laid on Friday. The group's objection is to the rest of the statement, which includes the later steel payments and VKR 105 billion of Commonwealth borrowing.

The Government needs Landvern's votes to keep its majority. A spokesman said the Finance Minister would "speak to every group before Thursday". He did not say what she would offer.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## Landvern Says No

### Tollefsrud: "An order can be changed with a letter"; the first VKR 4.5 billion of the steel money is still safe

*By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent*

Landvern's members met at seven o'clock this morning. They had read the Finance Ministry's note and order on Tuesday. They had spent much of the night in telephone calls with ministry officials.

Mr Tollefsrud said the group's vote was unanimous. He gave three reasons.

The first was the form of the number. "A minister signs an order. A later minister can sign another," he said. "We asked for something that a minister cannot change on a Tuesday afternoon."

The second was the note. It says the plans fit "broadly" with the Central Bank's forecast, and it lists two items that are "at risk if growth is weaker". "A note that says 'broadly' and 'at risk' is not a note that lets us sign up to years of borrowing," he said.

The third was the order of events. "We were told we would decide on a promise," he said. "Then we were told to decide on a number. Now we are told to decide on a letter. We have decided."

### What Landvern still supports

The group will back the first VKR 4.5 billion of the steel package. Mr Tollefsrud said that would be done through the standard contract, which does not need the statement to pass.

"Nobody in Laukvik should lose a day of work because of this vote," he said. "The first money is safe. We are voting on the rest."

### The Government's position

A Government spokesman said the Finance Minister regretted the decision. "The number is real. It is written down. It is in force," he said. "We will talk to every group before Thursday."

Asked whether the Government would change the order into an amendment to the contract, the spokesman said: "Nothing is ruled out." He did not say what would be offered or when.

Liv Karhagen, the Leader of the Opposition, made no comment on Tuesday.

### The vote

The statement is due for its vote on Thursday. It covers the whole of next year's plans, including the later steel payments and the borrowing. If Landvern votes against, the Government will have to find the votes elsewhere. A coalition member said on Tuesday evening that talks "would carry on until the bell".

## What the Order Says

### The Ministry's text sets 180 places a year, shared among firms; it can be changed by a later order

*By Sunniva Rask*

The Finance Ministry published its order at about three o'clock on Tuesday afternoon. It is two pages long.

It sets a minimum of 180 training places a year across all the firms that receive money from the steel package. Each firm's share is set by its size and by the money it receives. Sørstrand Steel's share is the largest. The order says firms must report their numbers to the Ministry every six months.

The Ministry said on Tuesday morning that the number would be written into the contract. By the afternoon it had changed course. A spokeswoman said the order would be "more flexible", because it could be adjusted if "conditions change".

That was the word that decided the matter for Landvern. A number that can be adjusted can also be lowered.

The Ministry said any change would have to be published in advance. It did not say how far in advance.

### The reaction

The steelworkers' union said it was pleased to see the number in writing. "A hundred and eighty is a decent figure," an official said. "We would have liked it in the contract. We have said so."

The union said it would hold back its recommendation. It wants to know that the number cannot be changed by one minister acting alone. Its leaders will meet again on Thursday.

## The Ministry's Note

### The Government's plans "broadly fit" with the Central Bank's forecast; two items are "at risk"

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

The Finance Ministry's note on the autumn statement runs to eleven pages. Its main finding is that the Government's plans "broadly fit" with the Central Bank's forecast.

The note says the Bank's forecast for growth, prices and borrowing costs is a "reasonable base" for next year's budget. It says VKR 105 billion of Commonwealth borrowing can be financed on that base.

But the note names two items that would be "at risk if growth is weaker than forecast". The first is the timing of the later steel payments. The second is the housing grant line for the Lakes.

"That is the sentence that matters," one economist said. "If growth falls, the Government will have to choose between those two. It does not say which."

The Central Bank declined to comment on the note. Its Governor, Hallvard Mikaelsen, said last Thursday that the VKR 105 billion was "in our forecast". The Monetary Council held rates by four votes to three. Three members wanted a rise.

The note's finding is a step back from the Ministry's earlier language. Last week a spokesman said the plans "fit within" the forecast. The note says "broadly".

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Computing Gives Back Some Gains

### KXC slips 0.4 per cent; Glimra falls 7.7 per cent; crude rises 2.2 per cent; gold loses $90

*By Tomas Nygaard*

The KXC Composite fell 82 points, or 0.4 per cent, to 20,384 on Tuesday.

Computing shares fell again. Glimra Compute lost 7.7 per cent, giving back about half of Monday's rise. Banks and shipping firms gained a little and held the index up.

Abroad, the move was sharper. The main foreign index fell by half a per cent. Its computing-heavy rival fell by one and a half. Shares in chip makers dropped by as much as ten per cent in a day. The biggest chip maker abroad, which had jumped by almost a third on Monday, fell by about 6 per cent.

"We had a huge day on Monday and now we are asking what it meant," one Karontoborg trader said. "The answer is: not much. The fear has gone. The questions have not."

### Bonds and the krona

The ten-year yield was unchanged at 3.82 per cent. The krona firmed to 7.00 to the dollar, as the dollar fell against most currencies abroad. It was 7.98 to the euro.

### Oil and gold

Benchmark crude rose 2.2 per cent to $109.47 a barrel. Traders said new attacks on ships near the mouth of the Gulf had lifted prices again, after Monday's fall.

"On Monday the market looked at the barrels," an oil trader said. "On Tuesday it looked at the ships."

Gold fell $90 to $3,663 an ounce. It lost ground as shares dropped and the dollar weakened, and it fell by 2.4 per cent in all.

### Mortgages

Lakes lenders made no change to their rates on Tuesday. One lender quotes 7.4 per cent for a five-year fixed loan. Most others quote 7.2 or 7.3 per cent.

## Glimra's Investors Ask for Reasons

### A group of pension and insurance funds asks the board for a written account of how it decided to bring back its chief executive

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent*

A group of Glimra Compute's larger shareholders has written to the board. It asks for a written account of how the board moved from one view to the other in eleven days.

The letter was sent on Tuesday and seen by the Herald. It is signed by the managers of three pension funds and one insurer. Together they hold a little over 9 per cent of the firm.

On 12 November the board said an inquiry had found that Viggo Tharaldsen had broken the firm's rules. On 23 November it said he would return as chief executive.

"We do not say the decision is wrong," the letter says. "We say the reasons should be on paper. A board that can change its mind in eleven days should be able to explain why in a page."

Glimra said it had received the letter and would reply "in due course". The company did not say whether the reply would be made public.

### The shares

Glimra's shares fell 7.7 per cent on Tuesday, after a rise of 15.2 per cent on Monday. Analysts said the fall followed the sector, not the letter. The firm's postponed share sale and bond, each of about VKR 1.7 billion, are still planned for the new year.

## City Letter Due Friday

### Karontoborg's site-by-site recount is still being prepared; the Treasury's review will cover every city

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said on Tuesday that the city's letter to the Treasury was still being prepared. It is due on Friday.

The letter will ask for a recount of the half-weight rule, site by site. Karontoborg has thirteen federal sites. The Treasury's claims table counts all of them at half weight, because it says federal land is "slow".

"We are checking each site with the planners," the mayor said. "Where there are pipes and roads in place, the land is not slow. We will say so, one site at a time."

The Treasury will report on the rule by the end of February.

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## Bjornstad: The Consent Still Fits

### The Defence Minister tells the People's Thing the frigates' October authority covers the coast as it is; no third escort before January

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad told the People's Thing on Tuesday that the October consent for the Red Sea escorts "still fits".

She spoke for twenty minutes. Her statement was short and plain.

"The consent of 1 October allows two frigates to escort merchant ships in the Red Sea approaches," she said. "The coast has changed. The task has not. The frigates escort ships. They do not hold ground."

She said the Government had no plan to send a third escort before January. "That is not a hope. It is an assessment," she said. "We will look at it again in the new year."

### The questions

Members asked her two things. Does the consent still fit the coast as it is? And what would have to happen before a third ship sailed?

On the first, she said the defence committee would receive a written update every week. On the second, she said the Government would come back to the committee before any change. She did not name a trigger.

A Labour member said the answers were "clear, but not complete". "She told us what will not happen in December," he said. "She did not tell us what would change her mind."

Ms Karhagen did not speak in the debate.

### Where the frigates are

VNS *Gudrid* and VNS *Kjipuktuk* are escorting ships in the approaches to the strait. The Defence Ministry said both were in good order and that crews would not be rotated before the new year.

## Insurers Hold; Cape Count Stays at Thirty-One

### Neither war-risk house has moved since the smaller one raised its rate on Tuesday

*By Halldor Vik*

Karontoborg's two war-risk insurers have both raised their Red Sea rates this week. The larger house raised its rate by about 5 per cent on Monday. The smaller raised it by about 4 per cent on Tuesday morning. Neither said it would move again today.

The count of Vinlandic-flagged ships on the long route round the Cape was unchanged at thirty-one.

## Rebel Port Struck Again

### Aircraft hit sites at the rebel-held port; the rebels warn they will target ports in the neighbouring kingdom

*By Halldor Vik*

Aircraft supporting the government struck sites at the rebel-held port on the Red Sea coast in the early hours of Wednesday. It is the same coast where a telephone and internet site was hit on Monday.

The rebels said they would target all the Red Sea ports of the neighbouring kingdom if the port were attacked. They told workers and shipping firms to leave those ports. Hours later the strikes came.

No casualty figures were available. The rebels hold most of the northern part of the coast. Government forces hold the main southern port.

Shipping agents in Karontoborg said they were "watching closely". The Vinlandic frigates are not involved in the fighting on shore.

## The Ministry Writes to the Cree Council

### A reply arrives on day seven; it offers a meeting in January but does not say where the road will go

*By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent, Hokelgard*

The Finance Ministry replied to the Kaniapiskau Cree Council on Tuesday afternoon. It was seven days after the Council's letter.

The Council's letter asked three questions about the new road north. Where will it go? Whose land will it cross? When will the nations be asked?

# PAGE SIX — SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY

## Why Computing Slipped Again

### A day after a huge rally, chip makers gave back part of it; analysts say the market is still asking one question

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent*

On Monday computing shares rose sharply, at home and abroad. On Tuesday many of them fell.

At home the fall was small. Abroad it was not. A leading chip maker lost about 6 per cent after rising almost a third a day earlier. A group of chip funds lost about 8 per cent. Some makers of memory chips lost 10 per cent.

Analysts gave three reasons. Some investors who bought on Monday sold on Tuesday to take a profit. Others were worried about inflation and the price of money, after strong figures on household confidence abroad. And some said Monday's rise had simply gone too far.

"One day does not make a recovery," said an analyst who follows the sector. "Monday said people were less frightened. Tuesday said they were not sure. We will know more when the big firms report their next quarter."

### What it means for Glimra

Glimra Compute fell 7.7 per cent. It is small by the standards abroad, but it is a leading name on the KXC. It still needs to raise about VKR 3.4 billion in the new year. A lower share price makes that harder.

The analyst said the share price would matter less than the board's reasons. "Buyers of shares and bonds will ask whether the board knows what it is doing," he said. "That is why the shareholders' letter matters."

## Flu Tests Reach 27 Per Cent

### The Public Health Agency says more than one test in four is positive; a third hospital limits visiting

*By Gunnhild Paquette, Science Correspondent*

More than one test in four for flu was positive last week. The Commonwealth Public Health Agency published its weekly figures early this morning. They show 27 per cent of tests positive, up from 20 per cent the week before.

The Agency said the rise was fast. It said hospitals in the Lakes and on the coast were seeing more admissions, mostly among people over sixty-five and young children.

A third large hospital in Karontoborg now limits visiting to one visitor per patient at a time. It joins the two that did so last week. All three said the rules would be reviewed on Monday.

The advice is the same. People over sixty-five and other vulnerable groups should be vaccinated now. People who are ill should stay at home.

# PAGE SEVEN — ARTS & CULTURE

## A Sunday Matinee for The Night Ferry

### The playhouse adds a performance on 29 November; seats go on sale at noon

*By Oda Fjellstad, Arts Critic*

The Old Quarter Playhouse has added a Sunday matinee of Marit Nesheim's *The Night Ferry* on 29 November, at two o'clock. Seats go on sale at noon today.

The play closes on Saturday 5 December. Every earlier performance has sold out. The returns list stays open, and a few seats come back most days.

I saw the play again this week, from a returns seat in the second row. It is a better play the second time. The first time one watches the plot. The second time one watches the people.

Marit Nesheim writes about a ferry crew on the last night of a route that is to be closed. The captain wants to say goodbye. The engineer wants to be paid. The cook wants to go home. Nobody gets what they want, and the audience is left to decide what they should have had.

The cast is very good. The second act, in which the cook reads out the passenger list, is the best twenty minutes in any theatre in the city this year.

If you cannot get a seat for the matinee, try the returns list on any weekday after four.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### Put It in the Contract

Landvern has said no. The Government can complain about it. It would do better to ask why.

The group asked for one thing. It wanted the training number written into the contract. On Tuesday it was given the number. It was not given the contract. The Ministry first said the number would be in the contract, and then said an order would be "more flexible". That one word did the damage. A number that is flexible can be made smaller. The union has said so. Landvern has said so. So has this newspaper, more than once.

### What it would cost

The Government says it has already done the hard part. It has set a number, signed an order and published a note. What more could be needed?

Very little. A short amendment to the contract, laid today, would put 180 places a year into the text that the firms sign. It would bind the firms. It would bind future ministers too. It would take an afternoon of drafting. If the Government believes in the number, it should be willing to sign it where it counts.

If it is not willing, people will draw their own conclusion. They will think the number is a gesture that can be taken back.

### The statement

The statement is larger than the number. It covers a year of spending and VKR 105 billion of borrowing. The Ministry's own note says that two items are "at risk if growth is weaker". Landvern is right that a year of plans should not pass on "broadly".

But the group should be careful too. Votes on the whole statement are rough tools. Landvern has said it will protect the first VKR 4.5 billion. Good. It should say plainly, before Thursday, what it would accept for the rest. The Government should say what it would give. Both sides have a day.

### The frigates

Ms Bjornstad gave the People's Thing a plain account on Tuesday. The consent of 1 October still fits. There will be no third ship before January. That was what we asked for. But she named no trigger. A country is entitled to know what would change a minister's mind. The weekly written update to the defence committee is welcome. It should be made public.
