## Landvern Will Not Vote on a Promise

### The group asks the Government for talks and will decide on Wednesday; the training number is due with this morning's Finance Ministry note

*By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent*

Landvern will not decide how to vote on the autumn statement until Wednesday.

Its members met for more than three hours in the People's Thing on Monday. When they came out, Bjarne Tollefsrud said the group had asked the Government for talks. It wants to see the training number before it decides.

"We will not vote on a promise," Mr Tollefsrud said. "On Tuesday we are told we will have the number and the note. We will read them both. On Wednesday we will decide."

The Finance Ministry said on Monday evening that the training number would be published with its note at ten o'clock this morning. It did not say whether the number would be written into the contract.

The vote is on Thursday. The Government needs Landvern's votes to keep its majority.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## Landvern Asks for Talks

### The group wants the number before it decides; Tollefsrud says the first part of the package still has its support

*By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent*

Landvern's members met at nine o'clock on Monday morning. They did not leave the room until after midday.

Mr Tollefsrud spoke to reporters in the corridor outside. He said the group had three choices on the table. It could back the statement. It could oppose it. Or it could ask for more time. It chose the third.

"Last week we said we wanted a number in the contract," he said. "On Friday we got a contract with no number in it. We got a note that says a number will come. That is not nothing. But it is not what we asked for."

He said Landvern had written to the Finance Minister on Monday afternoon. The letter asks for talks before Thursday. It asks two questions. What is the number? And will it be part of the contract?

### What has not changed

Mr Tollefsrud said Landvern would still vote for the first VKR 4.5 billion of the steel package. That part is covered by the standard contract laid on Friday.

"Nobody in Laukvik should worry about that," he said. "The first money is safe. The question is the rest of the statement, and what we are asked to sign up to for the years after."

The autumn statement covers all of next year's plans. That includes the later parts of the steel package and VKR 105 billion of Commonwealth borrowing. Mr Tollefsrud has called the later steel payments "three chances to cut".

### Talks

A Government spokesman said on Monday evening that the Finance Minister had received the letter. "Her door is open," he said. He did not say when the two sides would meet.

Asked whether Landvern might vote against the statement, Mr Tollefsrud said: "We will decide on Wednesday. I am not going to decide in a corridor on Monday."

## The Number Is Due This Morning

### The Finance Ministry says the training minimum will be published with its note at ten o'clock

*By Sunniva Rask*

The training number will be published this morning. The Finance Ministry said so in a short statement at about six o'clock on Monday evening.

The statement said the minimum number of young workers each firm must train would be set out "in full" with the Ministry's note on the package and the Central Bank's forecast. Both will be published at ten o'clock.

It did not give the number. It did not say whether the number would be added to the contract or set by a separate order. On Sunday a spokesman said the minimum would have "the same force as the contract".

### Solbakk

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk spoke briefly after the statement. "Landvern asked for a number. They will have it on Tuesday morning, in writing, two days before the vote," she said. "Then we can talk about the rest."

Liv Karhagen, the Leader of the Opposition, made no comment on Monday.

### The note

The note itself was promised last week. It will set out how the Government's plans fit with the Central Bank's latest forecast. On Thursday the Bank's Governor said the Commonwealth's VKR 105 billion of borrowing next year was "in our forecast". The Monetary Council held rates by four votes to three. Three members wanted a rise.

Members of the People's Thing will have two days to read the note before they vote.

## The Union Waits for Writing

### The steelworkers' union will not recommend the contract to its members until it sees the number

*By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg*

The steelworkers' union gave its view of the final contract on Monday. It was short.

"We like most of what we read," a union official said. "The local-hiring clause is strong. It has teeth. The training clause has a hole in it. We will not ask our members to vote until the hole is filled."

The union will hold back its recommendation until it sees the training number in writing. It said it would also want to know whether the number could be changed by a future minister.

"A number we can hold someone to is worth having," the official said. "A number that can be changed by a letter is worth less."

The union has said it will count its members' votes within a week of its recommendation. If it recommends this week, the count could end early next month.

### Laukvik

Sørstrand Steel said nothing new on Monday. One of its two strip lines at Laukvik is due to pause on 4 January. About 380 workers would go on short time.

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Shares Rise as Computing Recovers

### KXC gains 1.5 per cent, led by Glimra; crude falls 3 per cent; gold climbs $99

*By Tomas Nygaard*

The KXC Composite rose 296 points, or 1.5 per cent, to 20,466 on Monday.

Computing shares led the rise. Glimra Compute alone added 15.2 per cent after its board brought back its chief executive.

Abroad, the move was much larger. After a week of heavy selling, computing shares there jumped. The largest foreign index rose nearly 5 per cent. Its computing-heavy rival rose more than 6 per cent. Both had their best day since the spring of last year.

"Last week we fell by a little while they fell by a lot," one Karontoborg trader said. "Today it was the same story the other way round. We are not a computing market. We follow at a distance."

### Bonds and the krona

The ten-year yield was unchanged at 3.82 per cent. Investors did not sell the Commonwealth bonds they bought last week.

The krona eased to 7.02 to the dollar, as the dollar rose against most currencies abroad. It firmed against the euro, to 7.97.

### Oil and gold

Benchmark crude fell 3.0 per cent to $107.11 a barrel. Traders said more oil was coming from producers in the Gulf. A large oil pipeline in North Africa also started pumping again after a stoppage.

"There are still mines in the water and ships being hit," one oil trader said. "But for one day the market looked at the barrels, not the danger."

Gold rose $99 to $3,753 an ounce. It had fallen for most of the month.

### Mortgages

Lakes lenders made no change to their rates on Monday. One lender quotes 7.4 per cent for a five-year fixed loan. Most others quote 7.2 or 7.3 per cent.

## Back in the Chair

### Glimra's board says Tharaldsen has now declared the relationship; the share sale and bond are still planned for the new year

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent*

Glimra Compute's board met over the weekend. On Monday at seven in the morning it said Viggo Tharaldsen would return as chief executive.

The board's statement was four paragraphs long. It said Mr Tharaldsen had now declared the relationship with a senior employee, as the firm's rules require. It said the employee would no longer report to him, directly or through others. It repeated that its inquiry had found no misuse of company money and no harm to customers.

"The matter has been declared and dealt with," the statement said. "The board is satisfied that the company is best served by his return."

The finance chief, who had acted as head of the firm, goes back to the finance post.

### The market's answer

Shareholders welcomed the news. The shares opened 12 per cent higher and rose through the day. They closed 15.2 per cent up. On the day Mr Tharaldsen resigned, they had closed about 10 per cent lower.

Glimra has no plan to change its timetable. Its postponed share sale and bond, each of about VKR 1.7 billion, are still planned for the new year. A company spokesman said "nothing has changed" on that.

### Questions

Not everyone was pleased. One investor who manages pension money said the board had moved "very fast for a very serious matter". "Twelve days ago the board said he broke the rules. Today it says he is the best person for the job," she said. "Both may be true. But the board should explain how it got from one to the other."

Mr Tharaldsen did not speak to reporters. In a short message to staff, seen by the Herald, he said: "I made a mistake, and I have put it right. Now there is work to do."

## A Date for the Review

### The Treasury will finish its review of the half-weight rule by the end of February; the city's letter is due by Friday

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

The Treasury will finish its review of the half-weight rule for federal land by the end of February.

A Treasury spokeswoman gave the date on Monday. On Friday the Treasury's four-page method said only that the rule would be "reviewed in the light of the claims now made".

"The review will look at the claims from all cities, not only Karontoborg," the spokeswoman said. "It will report by the end of February."

Last week the Treasury's claims table accepted all thirteen of Karontoborg's federal sites. Each counts at half weight. The method says federal land is "slow". On past figures, homes on federal land have taken about twice as long to start as homes on city land.

### The city's letter

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said the city would send its letter to the Treasury by Friday. It will ask for a recount, site by site.

"We are going through their method line by line," she said. "Some of our sites have pipes and roads already in place. We will show them which ones. A date in February is welcome. But our families are waiting now."

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## Bjornstad Speaks Today

### The Defence Minister makes a statement on the Red Sea escorts at two o'clock; her office gives no hint of what she will say

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad will make a statement on the Red Sea escorts in the People's Thing at two o'clock this afternoon.

Her office said nothing on Monday about what she would say. Members will be able to question her after the statement.

It will be the first time she has spoken in the chamber on the escorts since the coast changed hands. Last week she briefed the defence committee in private. Its chair said she gave "a clear account".

### The questions

Two Vinlandic frigates, VNS *Gudrid* and VNS *Kjipuktuk*, escort merchant ships in the Red Sea approaches. The defence committee gave its consent on 1 October.

Since then much has changed. Rebel forces have taken the heights above the southern strait and an island inside it. They have reached the coast west of the main southern port. Last weekend they shelled the edge of the port.

Members from several parties want to know two things. Does the consent of 1 October still fit the coast as it is now? And what would have to happen before a third escort sailed?

Last week Ms Bjornstad said no third escort was planned this month.

## Second Insurer Raises Red Sea Rate

### The smaller war-risk house raises its rate by about 4 per cent from today; the Cape count holds at thirty-one

*By Halldor Vik*

The smaller of Karontoborg's two war-risk insurers raised its Red Sea rate by about 4 per cent from this morning. It had held its rate last week and said it would look again.

"We looked again," an underwriter said. "The coast has moved. We have moved with it."

The larger house raised its rate by about 5 per cent on Monday, its second rise in a week. Both houses have now raised their rates since the rebels reached the coast.

The count of Vinlandic-flagged ships on the long route round the Cape was unchanged at thirty-one.

Sigrid Holme of the Karontoborg Shipowners' Association said owners were watching the cost. "Every rise in the rate makes the Cape a little more attractive," she said. "Today it is still a choice for each owner. We would like it to stay that way."

## Air Strike on a Rebel Port

*By Halldor Vik*

Aircraft supporting the government struck a telephone and internet site in a rebel-held port on the Red Sea coast on Monday.

The rebels said there were casualties. They gave no figure. Telephone and internet services in the area were cut. No one else could confirm the reports.

The rebels hold most of the northern part of the coast. Government forces hold the main southern port and the road that leads to it.

## Two More Ships Hit in the Gulf

*By Halldor Vik*

Two more merchant ships reported being hit by objects fired from the shore near the mouth of the Gulf over the weekend. No one was hurt. Neither ship was Vinlandic.

On Saturday a tanker caught fire after striking a sea mine in the same waters. Its crew was reported safe.

Shipping officials abroad said attacks on ships near the mouth of the Gulf were now more frequent than at any time since the fighting there began. No group has said it was responsible.

# PAGE SIX — SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY

## Why the Computing Shares Swung

### Glimra's return and a recovery abroad lifted shares that had fallen for a week; analysts warn the questions have not gone away

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent*

For a week, computing shares fell almost every day. On Monday they rose sharply, at home and abroad.

The reasons were partly the same everywhere. Investors had sold heavily. Some decided prices had fallen far enough. Abroad, news from the largest computing firms helped. At home, Glimra's decision to bring back its chief executive did the same.

Glimra runs data halls near Forsgard and rents computing power to firms and public bodies. Like its rivals abroad, it has spent heavily on new buildings and chips. Earlier this month it put off a share sale and a bond until markets were calmer.

"Monday's rise tells you people were frightened, and then less frightened," said one analyst who follows the sector. "It does not tell you whether all this spending will pay. That question is the same today as it was on Friday."

### What it means for Glimra

Glimra still needs to raise money in the new year. A higher share price makes that easier. A leader in place also helps. Buyers of shares and bonds do not like a firm without a head.

But the analyst said buyers would also ask about the board. "They will want to know that the board's rules mean something," he said. "That is a fair question for any firm asking for VKR 3.4 billion."

# PAGE SEVEN — ARTS & CULTURE

## Prints That Read Like Charts

### Ingeborg Sylliboy's tide-chart prints draw a steady stream of visitors to the harbour gallery in Kjipuktuk

*By Oda Fjellstad, Arts Critic*

On a grey Monday afternoon the harbour gallery of the Kjipuktuk College of Art was quiet, but not empty. A school group sat on the floor in front of the largest of Ingeborg Sylliboy's prints. A retired fisherman stood beside them and pointed out the bays he knew.

Ms Sylliboy's prints look at first like old tide charts. Lines of numbers run down the edges. Thin curves mark the depth of the water. Look longer and the charts become something else. A curve turns into a shoulder. A column of numbers becomes a list of names.

"I wanted to make a map that remembered people as well as water," she says in the note that hangs by the door. The prints are made from carved wooden blocks, some of them cut from old boat timbers.

They are quiet works. They reward patience. The best of them, a long blue print of the harbour mouth at low tide, is worth the trip by itself.

In the next room Halvard Denny's burnt-wood panels are louder. Their dark, scorched surfaces catch the light from the windows. Together the two shows make a good pair: one about patience, one about fire.

Both run until 12 December.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### Ten O'Clock Is the Test

Landvern was right to wait. A party that is asked to vote for a year of spending should know what it is voting for. On Monday it did not. By ten o'clock this morning it should.

### The number

The Finance Ministry says the training number will be published with its note today. Good. For more than a week this newspaper has asked for that number. It has been promised "to follow", then "before the vote", then "with the same force as the contract". Today we will see it.

But a number is only half the answer. The other half is where it is written. If the number goes into the contract, it binds the firms that take the money, and it binds future ministers too. If it sits in a separate order, a future minister can change it with a letter. The union has said this plainly. So has Landvern. The Finance Minister has not yet said which it will be.

She should say so this morning. There is no good reason to keep the number out of the contract. A short amendment can be laid today. If the Government believes in the number, it should be willing to sign it.

### The chamber

At two o'clock Ms Bjornstad will speak in the People's Thing. The coast on which the committee gave its consent in October is not the coast of today. The rebels hold the heights and an island in the strait. Both insurers have raised their rates. The country deserves a plain account of what the frigates face, whether the consent of 1 October still fits, and what would have to happen before a third ship sailed. A statement that avoids those questions would be a wasted afternoon.

### The review

The Treasury has given a date for its review of the half-weight rule: the end of February. That is the right step, and it came quickly. Karontoborg's letter is due by Friday. Now both sides know the timetable. The argument can be about facts, not about silence.
