## VKR 14 Billion for Steel, Vehicles and a Road North

### Commonwealth and Lakes governments announce industry package today; officials give the size on Monday night but say conditions will come later; a road to the Shield's mineral belt raises the question of consent

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor, and Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

The industry package has a number. It does not yet have its rules.

The Commonwealth and the Lakes provincial government will today announce VKR 14 billion in support for steelmakers, vehicle makers and the mines of the Shield. Finance Ministry officials gave reporters the figure and its three parts on Monday evening.

The largest part is a steel fund of VKR 8 billion over five years. Vehicle works will get VKR 4 billion over two years for new production lines. The last VKR 2 billion will build a road to the mineral belt of the northern Shield.

What the money will ask of the firms is less clear. Officials said conditions would be agreed "firm by firm, in the contracts". They would not say whether any firm would have to keep jobs in return.

First Minister Erik Halvorsen said on Monday that the package was "for workers, not shareholders". Labour says that promise should be written down. And the First Peoples' Assembly has a question about the road: whose ground will it cross?

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## "Half a Law Is Not Worth a No"

### Tollefsrud gives Landvern's reasons for its two votes on the Incident Reporting Bill; Karhagen: "Four days to find a sentence"; Provincial Thing first reading goes ahead on Wednesday

*By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

For four days Landvern said nothing about its votes on the Incident Reporting Bill. On Monday its leader spoke.

Bjarne Tollefsrud told a news conference in Hokelgard that the two votes on Thursday had one reason. The bill, he said, was not finished.

On Thursday Landvern voted with Labour to send the bill back to committee. Twenty minutes later it abstained on the bill itself. The Tuesday before, it had abstained on an amendment.

"We voted to send it back because it had no date," Mr Tollefsrud said. "A register that opens 'in the spring' is a register that opens when the minister feels like it. We wanted a date."

When that failed, he said, the party had two choices. "We will not vote against a register. People want to know when a machine has hurt someone. But we will not vote for half of one either. Half a law is not worth a no, and it is not worth a yes."

He was asked why it had taken four days to say so. "We do not explain ourselves on the timetable of Hokelgard," he said. "We explained ourselves to our members first. Now we have explained it to you."

### "Four days to find a sentence"

Liv Karhagen, Leader of the Opposition, was not impressed.

"Four days to find a sentence," she said. "It is a reasonable sentence. Labour said the same thing on Thursday, in the chamber, on the day."

She said Landvern would now be judged by what it did in the second chamber. "If it wants a date, it can help us write one in. If it only wants to be seen to want one, we will know that too."

Minister Kari Lindqvist did not comment. Her ministry repeated that the minister "stands by the spring".

### Wednesday in the Provincial Thing

The Provincial Thing has a long day on Wednesday. The status committee sits at ten o'clock on the Shield Intertie. In the afternoon the chamber gives the bill its first reading. The Speaker's office confirmed on Monday that the order paper is unchanged.

The First Peoples' Assembly, which holds the balance in that chamber, has still not said how it will treat the bill.

Labour plans to use the committee stage, expected in November, to propose a fixed opening date for the public register. Its digital affairs spokeswoman, Runa Kahente-Moe, said on Monday that the party would "take any vote that is offered" for a date. "If Landvern means what it said today, we will have more of them," she said. "We will find out in November."

Mr Tollefsrud would not say how his party would treat a dated amendment. "Show us the date first," he said.

### Review continues

The Attorney General's office had nothing new to say on Monday about its review of the Hvelv board minutes. It has said the work will take "weeks, not days". The preservation notice served on 9 October remains in force.

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## The Package: Fourteen Billion in Three Parts

### Steel fund of VKR 8 billion over five years; VKR 4 billion for vehicle lines; VKR 2 billion for a road to the northern mineral belt; firms will apply; conditions "firm by firm"

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

The industry package is VKR 14 billion, Finance Ministry officials said on Monday evening. Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk and the Lakes provincial government will announce it in Karontoborg today.

The Commonwealth will pay VKR 8.9 billion and the Lakes government VKR 5.1 billion.

The money comes in three parts.

**Steel.** A fund of VKR 8 billion over five years will help mills buy new equipment, make new kinds of steel and find buyers abroad. A joint steel task force will look first at the cost of moving steel. Since the Gulf crisis began, that cost has risen sharply.

**Vehicles.** VKR 4 billion over two years will go to vehicle makers and large parts suppliers for new production lines.

**Minerals.** VKR 2 billion over three years will build a road to the mineral belt of the northern Shield. The Commonwealth will match the Lakes government's share.

Officials would not name any firm. Any steelmaker, vehicle maker or supplier in the Lakes may apply, they said. The steel mills of Sundgard and Laukvik and the Sundgard vehicle works are expected to be among the first.

### Conditions later

The officials were less clear about what the firms must give back.

"Conditions will be agreed firm by firm, in the contracts," one said. Asked whether firms would have to keep jobs, he said that would be "part of each discussion". Asked whether firms could pay dividends while they held the money, he said the same.

Oddvar Ruud, Labour's finance spokesman, set one test at the weekend: does the money keep people at work, or does it keep share prices up? On Monday night he said the package had not yet passed it.

"Conditions later usually means conditions never," he said. "If the First Minister means 'workers, not shareholders', he can write it on the first page of every contract."

Mr Halvorsen had used those words earlier on Monday. "This package is for workers, not shareholders," he said in the Thinghall. "That is the question before us."

Kjell Arvidsen, the Landvern member for Sundgard East, welded car bodies at the Sundgard vehicle works before he entered the People's Thing. He said the money was welcome but late.

"The men on the line have been asking for this since the summer," he said. "They will believe in it when the new line is running and they are still standing next to it."

The spokesman for the Lakes steel producers welcomed the figure. "We asked for a bridge," he said. "This is a bridge. We will read the toll sign when it is printed."

## Claims Table Gets a Date

### Treasury says the separate table on thirteen claimed sites will first meet on Friday 13 November; land-test question still unanswered after a week

*By Elise Brattli*

Treasury gave the separate table on the thirteen claimed housing sites a date on Monday. It will first meet on Friday 13 November.

The table will bring together the nations that claim the sites and federal officials. Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk set the thirteen sites aside last week. The housing working group will discuss terms for the other thirty-seven when it meets on Thursday.

The cities' other question is still open. A week ago Labour asked whether federal land counts toward the cities' land test. Treasury has not answered.

"One answer out of two," said Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg. "We will take it. On Thursday we will ask for the second."

The full list of fifty sites covers 613 hectares.

## Chips Lead, Oil Follows

### KXC rises 0.8 per cent to 21,126 as Glimra and chip designers rally; crude up 1.2 per cent to $119.07; krona weaker; economists expect a rate rise

*By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg*

The North Bank had a good day on Monday.

The KXC Composite rose 0.8 per cent to 21,126. Chip designers led the gains, and Glimra Compute rose most of all. Energy producers also rose with the oil price.

Benchmark crude rose 1.2 per cent to $119.07 a barrel, its highest close since the spring. Traders cited the late report of a tanker struck near the strait, and a ship on fire further north.

The ten-year Commonwealth yield rose one basis point to 3.96 per cent. The krona weakened to 6.85 to the dollar and 7.92 to the euro. Gold fell $18 to $4,011 an ounce.

The two large Lakes lenders kept their five-year fixed rate at 7.2 per cent.

### A rise expected

Most economists now expect the Central Bank to raise its policy rate at its next meeting. That was the finding of a survey of economists published by the Financial Vinlander on Monday. The survey followed Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen's speech on Friday, in which he said a lasting oil level would become the bank's "business".

The policy rate is 3.50 per cent. Inflation is 3.9 per cent.

"Friday was a warning," said Hedda Vang of the Lakes Mortgage Institute. "By Monday the market had decided it was a forecast. The Governor has not said that. But he has not said otherwise."

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## Fire in the North of the Gulf

### Tanker struck near the strait on Friday was disabled, says late report; a second ship burns far to the north; regional talks in the north-shore capital; insurers hold rates; Cape route at twenty-five

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

Two reports on Monday showed that the danger in the Gulf is spreading.

The first came late. A tanker leaving the Gulf through the strait was struck by a projectile on Friday morning, maritime security officials in the region said. The ship was disabled. Its crew were safe. It was not a Vinlandic ship. They did not name it, and they did not say who fired.

The second came on Monday. A ship caught fire in the far north of the Gulf, near the oil terminals at its head. Its name, its crew and the cause of the fire were not known. Officials called it "suspicious activity", not an attack.

"Late reports and unknown fires," one Karontoborg underwriter said. "That is what a war at sea looks like when nobody wants to say it is one."

### Talks in the capital

On the same day, senior officials from the large Gulf kingdom and two other states in the region met the government of the north-shore power in its capital. They said they wanted to prevent a wider war.

The Foreign Ministry said it welcomed "any talks that make the water safer". It gave no view on the meeting itself.

It was the first meeting of its kind since the crisis began. No statement was issued at the end of the day. Diplomats in Hokelgard said the kingdom wanted above all an end to the attacks on its oil and its airports. The militia that holds the southern Red Sea narrows fired a missile at the kingdom's capital only on Sunday.

### Rates hold; one more ship south

Karontoborg's war-risk underwriters left their published Gulf rates unchanged on Monday. They raised them by one step on Friday.

One more Vinlandic-flagged ship switched to the long route round the Cape, the Karontoborg Shipowners' Association said. That makes twenty-five.

"Our members read the same reports as everyone else," said Sigrid Holme of the association. "A ship on fire is not good for business, even when nobody knows why."

The Navy had nothing new on VNS *Gudrid* and VNS *Kjipuktuk*. Both frigates are still on passage. The initial escort period remains four months.

## The Ship Has Gone. The Questions Stay

### Foreign survey vessel leaves the outer Helluland Sea northbound; defence committee chair asks Bjornstad for a private briefing; Coast Guard says *Markland* is in "scheduled maintenance"

*By Aslak Kvande, Leifsbudir*

The foreign state survey vessel that crossed the outer Helluland Sea on Sunday left the area on Monday morning, heading north. The Defence Forces said it had not stopped near any undersea cable.

That did not end the matter in Hokelgard.

The chair of the People's Thing defence committee wrote to Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad on Monday. The chair asked for a private briefing on the transit before the minister replies to the committee's report.

Last Thursday that report said the Coast Guard had "too few hulls for too much water". On Sunday a Navy patrol aircraft watched the survey vessel. No cutter went out.

The Coast Guard said on Monday that VCGS *Markland* was in "scheduled maintenance" at Leifsbudir. It did not say when the work began or when it would end.

"Scheduled maintenance is a fine answer," said a retired Coast Guard captain in Leifsbudir. "It is also the problem. When your only cutter is in maintenance, you have no cutter."

Ms Bjornstad's office said she would answer the committee "in weeks". It did not say whether she would give the briefing. The draft allied agreement on undersea infrastructure is still unsigned.

# PAGE FIVE — NATIONS & THE SHIELD

## A Private Room on Wednesday

### Status committee chair grants the Cree Council a closed session for its record of trapping-ground holders; Grimsrud silent on who pays; Sakej-Eriksen: "A road is a line too"

*By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent*

The Kaniapiskau Cree Council will get its private room.

The chair of the Provincial Thing's status committee agreed on Monday to hear the Council's record of trapping-ground holders in a closed session. It will take place when the committee sits again at ten o'clock on Wednesday.

Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum asked for the session at the weekend. The record names the families who hold and use each trapping ground on the Shield. She has said it belongs to them and will not be made public.

"The chair has been courteous," she said on Monday. "We will return the courtesy on Wednesday."

The committee asked last Thursday who "the families" are. The Council's condition is that no line may cross a named trapping ground without the consent of the families who hold it. Members wanted to know who speaks for a family, and how consent is given and written down. Ms Kaninaskum said the Council would explain both.

The private session on the burial-site maps was due to last an hour. It lasted more than two. Committee staff have set aside the whole morning.

### Nothing new on the money

Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud said nothing new on Monday about who would pay the extra VKR 1.1 billion for Skjoldkraft's second route. That route is 21 km longer and keeps wider of both burial sites. On Sunday he said three times that the cost was "not decided".

### "A road is a line too"

Nora Sakej-Eriksen of the First Peoples' Assembly turned to a different line on Monday: the new road in the industry package.

The package includes VKR 2 billion for a road to the mineral belt of the northern Shield. Officials did not say where it would run.

"A road is a line too," Ms Sakej-Eriksen said. "It crosses ground. Someone holds that ground. We would like to know who has been asked, and when."

A Finance Ministry official said the route was "not settled". He said it would follow "the usual consultation".

"The usual consultation is why the status committee is sitting on Wednesday," Ms Sakej-Eriksen said. "The Government might learn from this week before it plans the next road."

The committee's finding is not expected on Wednesday.

### The Archive

The Archive of the Constitutional Peoples gave no new figures on Monday. Thirteen of the fifteen households whose holdings were open to the Hvelv portal in July have been reached. The line has had 109 calls. The Archive reports again on Friday.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### Fourteen Billion, and the Terms to Follow

Yesterday we asked the Commonwealth and the Lakes government for three things: the figure, the firms and the conditions. On Monday night they gave us one and a half.

The figure is VKR 14 billion. That is a serious sum, and it is spent on serious things. Steel mills in Sundgard and Laukvik are paying far more to move their goods since the Gulf crisis began. Vehicle works need new lines. A road to the northern mineral belt could open the Shield to the industries of the next half-century. We do not object to any of it.

The firms are half an answer. Any firm in the Lakes may apply. That is fair, but it means the public will learn who gets the money only after it is given.

The conditions are no answer at all. Officials say they will be agreed "firm by firm, in the contracts". Will a firm that takes the money have to keep its workers? Could it pay its shareholders while it holds public funds? "Part of each discussion," we are told.

Mr Halvorsen says the package is "for workers, not shareholders". We believe he means it. But a First Minister's sentence is not a contract. If the Government means it, the rule should be published today with the figure. Every contract should then say the same thing. Mr Ruud is right that conditions agreed later, in private, tend to be weak ones.

Then there is the road. Ms Sakej-Eriksen asks a fair question: whose ground will it cross, and who has been asked? This week the status committee will spend a whole morning in private with the record of the Cree trapping-ground holders. It is doing so because a line was drawn across the Shield before the families were asked. The Government should not draw a second line the same way. It should say now that the road's route will be settled with the nations whose ground it crosses, before the first survey stake goes in.

Two other matters deserve a word. Treasury has given the claims table a date, Friday 13 November. That is welcome. It has now had a week to say whether federal land counts toward the cities' land test. It should say so before Thursday.

And Landvern has explained its votes. Mr Tollefsrud says the bill was unfinished, and he wants a date for the register. That is a reasonable view. It would have been more persuasive on the day of the vote than four days later. The party now has a chance to act on it in the Provincial Thing. We will watch whether it does.
