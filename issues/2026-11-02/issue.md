## Government Offers a Date, Not a Condition

### Amendment to Labour's motion would have the standard contract laid before the Thing before applications open, but drops the rule that no money is paid until then; Landvern will abstain; Greens back Labour; the vote is tomorrow

*By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

Labour's motion for tomorrow says: read first, pay second. Ministers say: read first. They say nothing about paying.

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk's office published the amendment on Sunday afternoon. It would have the standard contract for the industry package laid before the People's Thing "before applications open".

That is earlier than Labour asked. But the amendment removes the words that tie the money to the contract.

"A condition has become a compliment," said Oddvar Ruud, Labour's finance spokesman. "We asked for a lock. We have been offered a promise."

Landvern will abstain. The Green River Alliance will vote with Labour. The debate is on Tuesday 3 November.

# PAGE TWO — THE GREAT THING

## "Before Applications Open"

### Government's amendment keeps Labour's demand that the contract be laid before the Thing, sets an earlier point for it, and cuts the link to payment; Ruud: "We asked for a lock"; Landvern to abstain; Greens with Labour

*By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard*

Ministers will not vote against Labour's motion tomorrow. They will try to rewrite it.

Labour's motion is one sentence long. It asks the People's Thing to resolve that no money from the VKR 14 billion industry package be paid to any firm until the standard contract has been laid before the Thing.

The amendment, published by Ms Solbakk's office on Sunday, is also one sentence long. It would replace Labour's words with these: that the House "welcomes the Government's commitment to lay the standard contract before the People's Thing before applications to the package open".

### Earlier, but softer

In one way the amendment asks more of ministers than Labour did. Firms apply before they are paid. A contract laid before the first bids would reach the Thing sooner than one laid before the first krona is paid.

In another way it asks less. Labour's text is a condition on the money. The amendment is a promise about a date. If the date slipped, nothing in the amendment would stop a payment.

"A condition has become a compliment," Mr Ruud said on Sunday evening. "We asked for a lock. We have been offered a promise. The minister's promises may be very good. A lock is better."

Ms Solbakk's office said the amendment "puts the contract in front of members at the earliest useful moment". It said ministers had "no intention of paying anyone before the contract is public", and saw no need "to write the obvious into a motion".

Applications are due to open "before the new year". Ms Solbakk has still given no date for the contract itself.

### Landvern will abstain

Landvern will abstain on both the amendment and the motion, its leader said on Sunday.

"We have said the package is late, and Karontoborg first," Bjarne Tollefsrud told a party meeting in Torsvik on Sunday evening. "Neither Labour nor the ministers have fixed that. We will not choose between two ways of signing the same cheque."

Liv Karhagen, the Labour leader, was not impressed. "Abstaining is a vote for whoever has more members in the room," she said. "Mr Tollefsrud knows that."

### Greens with Labour

The Green River Alliance will vote for Labour's motion and against the amendment.

"Read first, pay second is not a radical idea," said Hanne Loft, the party's co-leader, on Sunday. "It is how most families buy a car."

An opposition-day motion does not bind the Government. But a defeat on its own amendment would be awkward, two weeks before the autumn fiscal statement. First Minister Erik Halvorsen made no comment over the weekend.

### Wednesday's first reading

The Incident Reporting Bill has its first reading in the Provincial Thing on Wednesday 4 November. The First Peoples' Assembly has still not said how it will treat the bill. Landvern said nothing new about a dated amendment.

## Bishops "Cannot Support" Advance Requests

### Church letter read in parishes on Sunday opposes the part of the assisted-dying bill that the Lakes and the River said they would offer; Wapistan: "No one will be asked to act against conscience"

*By Sunniva Rask*

The bishops of the Vinlandic Church said on Sunday that they "cannot support" advance requests for an assisted death.

Their letter was read in parishes across the country. It does not oppose the whole of Health Minister Gunnar Wapistan's bill. It opposes the part that would let people write down, while they still can, when they would want help to die.

"A person who writes such a request cannot know the person they will become," the letter says. "We ask the Thing to protect that later person too."

Two provinces, the Lakes and the River, said last week they would offer advance requests if the bill passes. No other province has spoken.

Mr Wapistan said he respected the letter. "The bishops speak for many people, and they speak carefully," he said. "No doctor, nurse or chaplain will be asked to act against conscience. That is in the bill."

### The review continues

The Attorney General's office had nothing new to say over the weekend about its review of the Hvelv board minutes. It said on Thursday that the review would report "in November". The preservation notice served on 9 October remains in force.

# PAGE THREE — KARONTOBORG & THE ECONOMY

## Treasury Will Show Its Working

### Method behind "half weight" to be published before 12 November; Oyelaran-Skog will propose that federal land count in full, up to a cap; Wabano-Lie: "Show us the cap first"

*By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor*

On Thursday Treasury said a hectare of federal land would count as half a hectare toward the land test. On Friday it said it would explain why.

A Treasury spokesman said the department would publish "the method behind the figure" before the housing working group meets again on Thursday 12 November.

"The number did not come from nowhere," the spokesman said. "Members of the group are entitled to see where it came from."

### Full count, with a cap

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg said on Saturday that she would bring a different idea to the table on 12 November.

Federal land would count in full, she said. But no city could use it to meet more than a set share of its first-year target. The idea was suggested in this newspaper's leader on Friday.

"I do not want a city to meet its whole target by being lucky in its rail yards," she said. "I want a home on federal land to count as a home. A cap does both."

She did not say what the cap should be. "That is for the table," she said.

Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie of Laukvik was careful. Laukvik has less federal land than Karontoborg.

"A cap can be fair," he said. "It depends on the number. Show us the cap first, and then I will tell you if I like it."

### The rest of the table

The working group did not agree terms for the thirty-seven unclaimed federal sites on Thursday. The main questions are whether sites will be sold, leased or given, and who pays to clear them. The other thirteen sites go to the claims table, which first meets on Friday 13 November. The fifty sites cover 613 hectares.

The land test is one of four Ms Solbakk set for the compact on 18 October. The VKR 3.5 to 4.5 billion housing envelope reaches its ceiling only if first-year land targets are met.

## Flat in August, Perhaps Lower in September

### Statistics Office says output did not grow in August; early estimate shows a small fall the next month

*By Elise Brattli*

The economy did not grow in August, the Commonwealth Statistics Office said on Friday. Its early estimate for September shows a small fall.

Factories and builders did a little better. Wholesalers did worse. Shops had a good month.

"This is an economy holding its breath," said Hedda Vang, chief economist at the Lakes Mortgage Institute. "Fuel and shipping costs are biting. Nobody is spending ahead of the autumn statement."

The timing is awkward. Ms Solbakk presents her autumn fiscal statement on Tuesday 17 November. The Central Bank decides on interest rates two days later, on Thursday 19 November.

Most economists still expect the bank to raise its policy rate, now 3.50 per cent. Inflation is 3.9 per cent. Ms Vang was less sure than she was a week ago.

"Last week a rise looked very likely," she said. "It is still likely. But the bank does not like raising rates into a stall."

### A second lender moves

A second large Lakes lender raised its five-year fixed mortgage rate to 7.3 per cent on Friday. The first did so on Wednesday. The others are at 7.2 per cent.

## Lenders and Miners Lead a Fall

### KXC down 1.9 per cent to 20,541, its worst day for weeks; gold falls $79; crude up again; chip designers mixed

*By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg*

Karontoborg shares had their worst day for several weeks on Friday. The KXC Composite fell 1.9 per cent to 20,541.

Lenders fell most, after the growth figures and a second rise in mortgage rates. Mining companies fell with the price of gold.

Technology shares sold off heavily abroad. At home the picture was mixed. Chip designers ended slightly lower. Glimra Compute closed a little higher.

Gold fell $79 to $3,914 an ounce, its third fall in a row. Benchmark crude rose 0.7 per cent to $118.60 a barrel after the attack on a gas carrier in the Gulf.

The ten-year Commonwealth yield rose three basis points to 3.88 per cent. The krona weakened to 6.88 to the dollar and 7.95 to the euro.

"It was a day for selling what you could sell," one trader said. "Gold went because people needed cash."

# PAGE FOUR — THE WORLD

## Six Ships, No Incidents

### VNS *Gudrid* brings the first escort group clear of the southern approaches; Navy gives the number once the ships are through; Holme: "Six is a start"

*By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent*

On Thursday the Navy would not say how many ships were in its first escort. On Sunday, with the ships through, it said: six.

VNS *Gudrid* brought the six merchant ships clear of the southern approaches of the Red Sea on Saturday. There were no incidents, the Navy said. Nothing approached the group and no ship was delayed.

"The escort went to plan, from start to finish," said Commander Einar Paul, the Navy's spokesman. "That is what a good escort looks like. It is quite dull."

He would not name the ships. He said only that "several" were Vinlandic-owned.

VNS *Kjipuktuk* remains with the partner navies' escort group. The Navy did not say when the next escort would begin. "When it is under way, we will say so," Cdr Paul said. "When it is through, we will say more."

The People's Thing approved the escort on 1 October and the Provincial Thing on 13 October. The initial period is four months. Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad made no statement.

Sigrid Holme of the Karontoborg Shipowners' Association welcomed the news.

"Six is a start," she said. "Our captains will be glad of it. But six ships in the Red Sea does not help a single ship in the Gulf."

## A Gas Carrier Is Struck

### Liquefied-gas carrier hit by "unidentified objects" on Friday evening; crew unhurt and ship operational; two underwriters stop quoting until today; Cape count rises to twenty-nine; mediators talk of opening the strait in stages

*By Halldor Vik*

A liquefied-gas carrier was struck by "unidentified objects" on Friday evening while sailing in the Gulf, its owners said on Sunday.

None of the crew was hurt. The ship was "in a safe condition and operational", the owners said. They did not say what had hit it, or who they believed was responsible. An investigation has begun.

The owners are a state shipping company of one of the smaller Gulf states. Its government called the attack "a dangerous act" against the safety of shipping.

No Vinlandic ship was involved, the Shipowners' Association said.

### Quotes suspended

Two of the larger Karontoborg houses stopped giving new quotes for Gulf voyages on Friday afternoon, after the first reports of the attack. Existing cover was not affected. Both said they would quote again "on Monday".

"We do not price a risk until we know what it is," one underwriter said. "On Friday evening, nobody knew."

It is the first time in the crisis that the houses have stopped quoting. Published rates rose four times in the last week of October.

Two more Vinlandic-flagged ships have taken the long route round the Cape, the Shipowners' Association said on Sunday. The number is now twenty-nine.

"October was the worst month our members can remember," Ms Holme said. "November has not started any better."

### Talk of a reopening

Mediators between the north-shore power and its opponents are discussing a plan to reopen the strait in stages, officials in the region said on Sunday. They gave no details and no timetable.

"We have heard of plans before," Ms Holme said. "We will believe in this one when a tanker sails through without asking anyone's permission."

# PAGE SIX — SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY

## The Checker's Busy First Day

### Public tool for machine-made images and audio slows for an hour under heavy use; ministry gives no figure; city's comment period closes; count "this week"

*By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg*

The ministry's public checker for machine-made images and audio opened on Sunday. By lunchtime it was struggling.

For about an hour in the early afternoon, the checker answered slowly or not at all. The ministry said it had seen "heavy use, well above what we planned for". It added more capacity, and the tool was working normally by mid-afternoon.

The ministry would not say how many checks were made. "We will publish figures when we have a full week," a spokesman said.

The checker looks for the hidden marks that several large developers now put in their output. It tells the user what any label says.

It cannot prove that an image is real. A picture with no mark may be genuine, or may come from a developer that does not use marks. The ministry's page says so first.

"People were curious, and many of them were careful," said one Karontoborg teacher who tried it with her class. "That is a good start. The test is whether they still use it in a month."

### Comments closed

Karontoborg's draft contract terms for autonomous systems closed for public comment on Saturday. The city said it would publish the number of comments "this week".

The Incident Reporting Bill has its first reading in the Provincial Thing on Wednesday 4 November. Committee stage is still expected in November.

### Flu

The Commonwealth Public Health Agency said on Friday that flu vaccine uptake was "ahead of last year" among people over sixty-five. Its next weekly figures are due on Wednesday. Last week seven per cent of tests were positive. Its advice to vulnerable groups stands: get vaccinated now.

# PAGE SEVEN — ARTS & CULTURE

## The Ice Holds a Second Time

### At its second performance, Wolde's Third Symphony keeps its whole audience; the Academy's river sells out two nights running

*By Oda Fjellstad, Arts Critic*

On Thursday some of the audience left during the first movement of Ragnar Wolde's new symphony. On Saturday nobody did.

Perhaps word had got round. Either way, the second performance of the Third Symphony by the Lakes Philharmonic was heard in near silence from the first chord to the last.

I went again to check my first impression. It was right. This is a remarkable piece.

The long, high chords of the opening are no easier on a second hearing. But they are clearer. You begin to hear how each single changed note moves the music a little further on. It is like watching ice form. Nothing seems to happen, and then the lake has closed.

The low brass in the second movement were even better than on Thursday. Halla Tekarontake let them grow out of the strings so slowly that it was hard to say when they began.

The rough third movement was a little faster this time. The ending was just as quiet. When the last horn note faded, the hall waited a long moment before it clapped.

Mr Wolde came on stage for a bow. He did not make a speech. He seldom does.

The orchestra has not announced further performances.

### Two full nights at the Academy

The Royal Academy's late openings of Jonas Atsistaehron's *What the River Kept* sold out at the door on both Friday and Saturday, the Academy said. That makes three sold-out nights in a row.

"We are being asked whether we will extend the show," a spokeswoman said. "We have no plans to. Visitors should come before 15 November."

The next late openings are on Thursday and Friday, until ten o'clock.

### Still running

Marit Nesheim's *The Night Ferry* runs at the Old Quarter Playhouse in Hokelgard until 28 November.

## Laukvik Says No This Time

### Lynx beat the Lakers 5–4 at home; a late goal settles it; Lakers fall to 1–9–1; Rapids shut out the Pilots in Sundgard and lead alone

*By Kjetil Hammer*

The Karontoborg Lakers went back to the only rink where they have won this season. This time it did not help.

# PAGE NINE — OPINION

## THE HERALD'S VIEW

### More Than Labour Asked, and Less

Ministers have answered Labour's motion with a better date and a weaker rule.

Labour asks that no money from the industry package be paid until the standard contract is laid before the People's Thing. Their amendment says the contract will be laid before firms can apply. That is sooner. Members would see the terms before any firm applies, not just before any firm is paid. On timing, ministers have offered more than they were asked.

But a motion is not only about timing. Labour's text is a rule about money. Theirs is a promise about a date. Promises slip. Applications are due "before the new year", and Ms Solbakk has still not dated the contract. If the contract were late, Labour's rule would hold the money back. The promise would not.

The minister says she has no intention of paying anyone before the contract is public. We believe her. Then there is no cost in saying so. The cleanest answer is to keep both: lay the contract before firms apply, and pay nothing until it has been laid. Either side could table that tonight. Members tomorrow should vote for whichever text contains both.

Landvern will abstain. Mr Tollefsrud says he will not choose between two ways of signing the same cheque. But the two texts are not the same. One would stop the cheque if the terms were not ready. A party that says the package is late and badly aimed should care about that.

### Showing the working

On Friday we said Treasury should publish how it chose the figure of half. It will. That is welcome. Mayor Oyelaran-Skog will propose a full count with a cap, which we also suggested. Mayor Wabano-Lie is right to say the cap is the whole question. The working group should have Treasury's method in hand well before 12 November, not on the morning of the meeting.

### Six ships

The first escort was dull, as Commander Paul says a good escort should be. Six ships came through. The frigates have done what both chambers sent them to do. But the danger has moved. A gas carrier was struck in the Gulf on Friday, and Karontoborg's underwriters stopped quoting for a weekend. The escort is a help. It is not an answer.

### The bishops' letter

The bishops have written with care, and the Thing should read them with care. Their concern is for the later self of a person who signs an advance request. That is a serious point, not a slogan. Mr Wapistan is right that no one should be made to act against conscience. The committee that examines his bill should hear the bishops in person.
