Karontoborg · HokelgardMonday, 9 November 2026Late Edition

Hit Freighter Reaches Port as More Ships Burn

Vinlandic freighter towed in over the weekend, crew ashore and well; more foreign tankers hit in the approaches; mediators say the first stage “continues”; Holme: “Continues for whom?”; Halvorsen: “A harbour is not a passage”

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The Vinlandic freighter struck in the Gulf approaches on Thursday reached port on Saturday. It came in under tow. Its crew are ashore and well.

The Karontoborg Shipowners’ Association gave the news on Saturday evening. It did not name the port.

Over the same weekend more ships were hit. Foreign tankers caught fire in the approaches on Saturday and again on Sunday. No Vinlandic ship was among them. No deaths were reported.

The mediators said on Sunday that the first stage of the strait agreement “continues”. They again declined to publish the text.

Sigrid Holme, director of the Shipowners’ Association, was not impressed. “Continues for whom?” she asked. “Our ship is in a harbour with a hole in it. Theirs are on fire. Ours will stay on the long route.”

First Minister Erik Halvorsen spoke briefly on Sunday. “A harbour is not a passage,” he said. “We will judge the strait by ships that come out the far side.”

The Navy said there was no second escort.

Checker Ran 222,000 Times in First Week

Ministry’s first full report: about nine in a hundred checks found a label marking an image or recording as machine-made; most found none; city summary of 860 comments still to come

By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg

The public checker for machine-made images and audio was used about 222,000 times in its first eight days.

Bill Gets Its Committee

Speaker’s office names the justice and digital affairs committee to take the Incident Reporting Bill; first sitting Thursday 26 November; Labour still wants a fixed date for the register

By Sunniva Rask, Hokelgard

The Incident Reporting Bill has a committee. The Speaker’s office of the Provincial Thing said on Saturday that the bill will go to the justice and digital affairs committee.

The committee will first sit on Thursday 26 November. That is seventeen days from today. The Speaker’s office said the sitting would be public.

The bill passed its first reading on Wednesday by 72 votes to 11. It was tabled after the Hvelv breach of the Archive of the Constitutional Peoples.

Labour will seek a fixed date in committee for the public register to open. The First Peoples’ Assembly has reserved its position. Nora Sakej-Eriksen has said her members let the bill be read but did not agree to it.

A Labour aide said the party would set out its amendments “in good time for the twenty-sixth”. Speaker Marta Okimaw-Strand said nothing beyond the notice.

Eleven Days to the Contract

Solbakk’s office has nothing new; Labour and Landvern silent; the Treasury still will not say who is drafting

By Sunniva Rask

Friday 20 November is eleven days away. Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk has named that date three times. By then the standard contract for the industry package is to be laid before the People’s Thing.

Her office said nothing new over the weekend. A Treasury spokesman repeated on Sunday that drafting was “a matter for the Government as a whole”. He would not name a ministry.

Liv Karhagen of Labour and Bjarne Tollefsrud of Landvern also had nothing new to say. Hanne Loft of the Green River Alliance has said the date “must be kept”. She did not add to that over the weekend.

The motion passed on Tuesday by 194 votes to 6 locks the package. No krona of the VKR 14 billion may be paid until the contract is laid. The motion has no force in law. Ministers wrote the lock themselves.

Halvorsen on the Strait

First Minister speaks for the first time since the first stage began; “We will judge the strait by ships that come out the far side”

By Sunniva Rask

First Minister Erik Halvorsen spoke about the Gulf on Sunday afternoon. It was his first comment since the first stage of the strait agreement began on Thursday.

He spoke for under two minutes outside the Thinghall. “A harbour is not a passage,” he said. “Our freighter is safe in port. Our sailors are safe ashore.”

Asked whether the Government trusted the agreement, he said: “We will judge the strait by ships that come out the far side. That is the question before us. Not stages. Ships.”

He did not take questions on a second escort.

The Review and the Bishops

Attorney General’s Hvelv review still due by Friday 28 November; no word from Wapistan or the bishops

By Sunniva Rask

The Attorney General’s review of the Hvelv board minutes is still due by Friday 28 November. Its office said nothing more over the weekend. The preservation notice served on 9 October remains in force.

Health Minister Gunnar Wapistan did not speak on assisted dying. Nor did the bishops of the Vinlandic Church. No province changed its stated view on advance requests.

Shares Fall, Crude and Gold Rise

KXC down 1.2 per cent to 20,686 in a broad sell-off; crude up 2.2 per cent to $111.72 after the freighter hit; gold adds $39 to $4,184; ten-year yield up six points

By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg

Karontoborg shares fell on Friday. The KXC Composite closed at 20,686, down 1.2 per cent.

The fall was broad. Lenders, builders, miners and makers all lost ground. Few shares rose. Traders said the news from the Gulf on Thursday night had reached the market by the opening bell.

“Wednesday priced a deal,” one trader said. “Thursday priced a wait. Friday priced a freighter with a hole in it.”

Benchmark crude rose 2.2 per cent to $111.72 a barrel. It was its first rise of the week. Gold rose $39 to $4,184 an ounce. That is its fifth rise in a row.

The ten-year Commonwealth yield rose six basis points to 3.78 per cent. That reversed Thursday’s fall.

The krona was a little firmer, at 6.85 to the dollar and 7.92 to the euro.

Over the week the KXC rose 0.7 per cent, from 20,541 a week earlier. Most of that gain came on Wednesday, on hopes for the strait.

Glimra Names a Season

Cloud firm will “return to the market in the new year” with its share sale and bond; third data hall near Forsgard goes on “at the pace the firm can fund”

By Tomas Nygaard

Glimra Compute said on Saturday that it would return to the market “in the new year”. It pulled its share sale and its bond on Wednesday. Each was worth about VKR 1.7 billion.

Until now the firm had given no time at all. It had said only that it would wait until “conditions are settled”.

A spokesman said the board would “look again in January” at the size and timing of both. Work on the third data hall near Forsgard continues “at the pace the firm can fund”.

Three Days to the Method

Treasury half-weight paper still unpublished; Oyelaran-Skog repeats her call for full count with a cap; claims table on Friday; 37 sites unclaimed

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor

The Treasury promised its method “before 12 November”. That leaves three days. The paper had not appeared by Monday morning.

The paper is meant to explain why federal land counts at half weight toward the cities’ housing targets.

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog of Karontoborg repeated her view on Sunday. She wants federal sites to count in full, with a cap. No city should be able to meet more than a third of its first-year target from federal land.

“We cannot argue with a method we have not seen,” she said. “We can only argue for a better one. That is what we will do on Thursday.”

Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie of Laukvik gave no new view.

Fifty federal sites, covering 613 hectares, are on the Treasury’s list. Thirteen go to the claims table on Friday 13 November. The other thirty-seven are unclaimed.

Sørstrand: No Reply Yet

Union still has not answered the company’s letter; no joint meeting with the city; short time from 1 December

By Tomas Nygaard

There was still no word on Monday from the steelworkers’ union. It has not answered Sørstrand Steel’s letter of Tuesday 3 November. The company had nothing to add.

Mayor Wabano-Lie wants a joint meeting of the firm, the union and the city before 1 December. No date has been set.

About 380 workers will go on short time when one of the two strip lines pauses on 1 December.

Eight Days to the Statement

Autumn statement Tuesday 17 November; Central Bank Thursday 19 November; most economists still expect a rise from 3.50 per cent

By Elise Brattli

The autumn fiscal statement is eight days away. Finance Minister Solbakk’s office says it will “hold the line”. There will be no new spending beyond the industry package and the housing envelope.

The Central Bank decides on Thursday 19 November. Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen did not speak over the weekend. Most economists still expect a rise from 3.50 per cent. Inflation is 3.9 per cent.

Large Lakes lenders kept their five-year fixed rates at 7.2 per cent, except two at 7.3.

The week ahead

Tuesday: Bjornstad answers the defence committee. Wednesday: status committee; weekly flu figures. Thursday: housing working group. Friday: claims table and the Archive’s weekly report.

The Freighter Is In; The Fires Go On

Vinlandic ship reaches a regional port under tow; crew ashore; foreign tankers hit on Saturday and Sunday; a cargo ship’s crew taken off; mediators: stage “continues”; still no text

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The Vinlandic freighter struck in the Gulf approaches on Thursday is safe in port.

It arrived under tow on Saturday afternoon. The Karontoborg Shipowners’ Association said the crew had come ashore and that all were well. A surveyor will look at the hull this week. The owners will then decide whether to repair the ship in the region or bring it home.

The Association would not name the port. “The crew have had a hard week,” a spokesman said. “They do not need visitors.”

The ship is owned and crewed mainly by Vinlanders but sails under a foreign flag.

A weekend of fires

The approaches were not quiet while it was towed in.

On Saturday a foreign tanker caught fire close to the narrows. Another reported being struck by an object on its way out. On Sunday two more tankers were reported on fire. The coastguard of a Gulf state took twenty-two crew off a burning cargo ship further south. All were said to be safe.

One of the parties to the agreement said some of the ships had used “routes not agreed”. It did not say who had agreed the routes, or with whom.

No Vinlandic ship was hit over the weekend. No deaths were reported.

“Continues”

The mediators issued a short statement on Sunday. It said the first stage “continues”. It did not say what the stage includes.

They again declined to publish the text of the agreement. They have now said three times that they will not release it “until there is something to release it for”.

Holme: “Continues for Whom?”

Shipowners’ leader says the long route stands; rates held; Cape count still 29; Navy: no second escort

By Tomas Nygaard

Sigrid Holme spoke on Sunday evening. She was sharper than usual.

“Continues for whom?” she said. “A stage that continues while tankers burn is not a stage. It is a calendar. Our ship is in a harbour with a hole in it. We are grateful the crew are safe. We are not grateful to anyone else.”

She said her members would stay on the long route round the Cape. “When a ship goes through and comes out whole, call me.”

The count of Vinlandic-flagged ships on the long route was unchanged, at twenty-nine.

The two larger Karontoborg war-risk houses held their Gulf rates on Friday and did not move them over the weekend. They had raised them six times in about a week. An underwriter said on Sunday that the houses would look again today.

“There was no Vinlandic hit this weekend,” he said. “That is the only reason the rates did not move.”

No second escort

The Navy said there was no change to its posture. Commander Einar Paul said again that a second escort would be announced “when it is under way”. The first escort of six ships is complete. The four-month period set by the Things has not changed.

Bjornstad Answers Tomorrow

Defence Minister’s reply on the foreign survey-vessel transit due at the defence committee on Tuesday; VCGS Markland to return to service Thursday 19 November

By Aslak Kvande, Leifsbudir

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad gives her reply to the defence committee tomorrow. Her office confirmed the date on Saturday.

The reply covers the transit of the outer Helluland Sea by a foreign survey vessel on 25 October. No cutter went out that day. VCGS Markland stayed alongside in Leifsbudir.

The Coast Guard gave a date on Saturday. Markland will return to service on Thursday 19 November. Until now it had said only “mid-November”. “She will be back on station on the nineteenth,” a spokesman said.

What the Checker Found

About 222,000 checks in eight days; roughly nine in a hundred found a machine-made label; ministry: “No label does not mean real”; heaviest use on the first Monday

By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg

The Ministry of Digital Affairs published its first full report on the public checker on Monday morning.

The tool opened on Sunday 1 November. It lets anyone upload an image or a sound file and see whether it carries a label showing it was made by a machine.

In its first eight days the checker was used about 222,000 times. On Thursday the ministry had given a figure of about 80,000. Use rose sharply over the weekend.

About nine checks in every hundred found a label. The rest found none.

What “no label” means

The ministry was careful about that second number. “No label does not mean real,” a spokeswoman said. “It means the file did not carry a label. Some machine-made files carry none. Some labels are stripped. The checker tells you what is there. It cannot tell you what is missing.”

She said the heaviest use came on Monday 2 November, the first working day. The service slowed for about an hour that morning. It has not slowed since.

The ministry did not break the figure down by image and sound. It said a fuller breakdown would follow “with the monthly report”.

What comes next

Minister for Digital Affairs Kari Lindqvist did not comment on the figures. Her bill on incident reporting now has a committee in the Provincial Thing. It first sits on Thursday 26 November.

The city of Karontoborg has still to publish its summary of the 860 comments on its draft contract terms for autonomous systems. It has promised that, with a revised draft, “before the end of the year”.

At Last: Lakers Win at Home

Karontoborg beat the Royals 5–4 for their first home win of the season; late goal settles it; Lynx edge the Storm 3–2; Pilots beat the Rapids 5–4 and lead alone

By Kjetil Hammer

The Karontoborg Lakers have won at home. It took them most of the autumn.

The Lakers beat the Hokelgard Royals 5–4 in Karontoborg on Saturday night. It was their first home win of the season. It was only their second win of any kind.

The game was level at 4–4 with three minutes left. Then a Lakers forward collected a loose puck at the side of the net and put it in. The Royals pulled their goaltender for the last minute. The Lakers held on.

“I have stood in that room after a lot of games this year,” the Lakers coach said. “Tonight I did not have to say we played well. The score said it.”

The Lakers led 2–1 after the first period. The Royals went ahead 4–3 in the third before the Lakers scored twice more.

The Lakers are now 2–10–1. They remain last. The Royals are 3–3.

Lynx Edge the Storm

Laukvik win 3–2 in Stadakona on Friday; Storm lose share of top place

By Kjetil Hammer

The Laukvik Lynx beat the Stadakona Storm 3–2 in Stadakona on Friday night.

The Lynx scored twice in the second period and held a 3–1 lead into the last ten minutes. The Storm pulled one back but could not find a second. The Lynx goaltender made a string of saves in the final minute.

The Lynx are now 5–5. The Storm are 5–3.

The Herald’s View

Five Days, Five Promises

This week the Commonwealth will learn what its promises are worth. Most of them were made by ministers. All of them have dates.

Tomorrow the Defence Minister answers the defence committee. On Wednesday the status committee sits. On Thursday the housing working group meets. On Friday the claims table opens. And through it all, the strait agreement is said to “continue”.

A promise with a date is a good thing. It lets the public check.

The freighter

The Vinlandic freighter is in port. Its crew are ashore and well. That is the news that matters most this morning, and it is good news.

It does not change the rest. More tankers burned this weekend. The mediators say the first stage “continues”. They still will not publish what the stage is. Ms Holme asks: continues for whom? It is a fair question. The First Minister’s answer is also fair. A harbour is not a passage. The strait will be judged by ships that come out the far side.

Until one does, the Cape route stands. The war-risk houses held their rates this weekend only because no Vinlandic ship was hit. That is not confidence. It is relief.

The method

The Treasury has three days. It promised its half-weight method “before 12 November”. The housing working group meets on the twelfth. Mayor Oyelaran-Skog put it plainly: the cities cannot argue with a method they have not seen.

If the paper does not appear, the working group will meet to discuss a number with no reasoning behind it. That would waste a day the cities do not have. Publish the paper. Publish it before Thursday.

The answers

On Wednesday the status committee takes up the Cree Council’s written answers. Grand Chief Kaninaskum says the Council has said what it has to say. The committee owes it a careful reading and a date for a finding. It has given neither yet.

On Tuesday the Defence Minister answers for a survey vessel that crossed the outer Helluland Sea while no cutter went out. Markland returns on 19 November. That is welcome, and late. The minister should say how the north will be watched the next time a cutter is in port.

The checker

About 222,000 checks in eight days is a good start. People want to know what they are looking at. The ministry was right to warn that “no label does not mean real”. That warning belongs on the front of the tool, not in a press release.

The contract

Eleven days remain to Friday 20 November. The Treasury still will not say who holds the pen. We have asked twice. We ask a third time. A date in the record is only worth something if someone is writing to it.

Saturday night

The Lakers won at home. It took most of the autumn. Karontoborg will take it.
