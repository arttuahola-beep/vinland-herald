Vote on the Statement Today, With No Deal in Sight

The Finance Minister talked to every group on Wednesday but offered nothing new; Landvern and the Opposition both say they will vote no

By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent

The People’s Thing votes this afternoon on the Government’s autumn statement. When Wednesday ended, the Government had no deal with the group it most needed.

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk spent the day in meetings. She saw the leaders of the smaller groups in the morning and several independents after lunch. She did not meet Landvern. Its leader, Bjarne Tollefsrud, said no meeting was asked for and none was offered.

“We have been told that the door is open,” he said on Wednesday evening. “We have not been shown a door. We have not been shown a letter. We have not been shown an amendment.”

Late in the evening a Government spokesman said the statement would be voted on “as laid”. He said the Finance Minister was “confident of the result”. He would not give a count.

Liv Karhagen, the Leader of the Opposition, broke her silence of the week. She said her group would vote against the statement “whatever the Government does between now and then”. She said the plan was “a year of borrowing on a promise”.

Landvern says it will still back the first VKR 4.5 billion of the steel package. That money is covered by the standard contract and does not depend on the vote.

Union Leaders Meet Today on the Training Number

The steelworkers say 180 places a year is a fair figure, but they want to know who can change it

By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent

The steelworkers’ union leaders meet this morning to decide whether to recommend the steel contract to their members.

On Wednesday evening an official said nothing had changed since Tuesday. The number is 180 places a year. It is in a ministerial order, not in the contract. “A later minister could change it,” he said. “We would like to hear someone say that cannot happen.”

If the leaders recommend the contract, the members’ count would follow within a week.

The Day Without a Deal

Ms Solbakk saw the smaller groups and the independents; the vote falls this afternoon

By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent

The Finance Minister began on Wednesday at nine o’clock. By six in the evening she had met the leaders of four smaller groups and, by one count, five independents. Her office said the talks were “open and useful”. It did not say what had been offered.

One independent said afterwards that nothing had been put on the table. “She listened,” he said. “She explained the number and the note. She did not give me a reason to change my mind.”

What the Government says

A Government spokesman said late on Wednesday that the statement would be voted on as laid. “The training number is real. It is in force. We do not see why the Thing should wait,” he said.

Asked about an amendment to the contract, he said: “The Minister has said all along that the order has the force of the contract. That has not changed.”

He declined to say how many votes the Government expected. The whip’s office said only that all members would be present.

What Landvern says

Mr Tollefsrud said his group had not moved. “On Tuesday we said we would not vote on a promise or a letter. Nobody has since offered us anything else. We have nothing to reconsider,” he said.

The Opposition

Ms Karhagen said on Wednesday evening that her group would vote against. She said the statement asked the Thing to approve VKR 105 billion of borrowing on a note that said “broadly” and “at risk”.

“We will not be a spare pair of hands for either side,” she said. “We are the Opposition. We will oppose a plan that has not been made firm.”

Government members said she was voting against steel jobs. Ms Karhagen said she was voting against a vague plan.

The arithmetic

The Government needs Landvern’s votes to keep its majority. Without them it needs some of the smaller groups and independents. Several said on Wednesday that they had not decided.

The vote is expected at about four o’clock. The result will be in tomorrow’s Herald.

The Union’s Test

Leaders ask who can change a number written in an order

By Sunniva Rask

The steelworkers’ union leaders will meet at ten o’clock this morning in Laukvik. They have three papers: the contract, the order and the Ministry’s note.

The union has said it is pleased with the number. It has also said it wants it in the contract. On Wednesday an official repeated both points and added a third. “We will want to know what happens if the Thing votes down the statement,” he said. “The contract is unaffected. The order is a separate matter. We would like that in writing too.”

Sørstrand Steel said nothing new on Wednesday. Its strip-line pause is still due on 4 January. The company said it would “watch the vote”.

Shares Hold Up as Oil Jumps

KXC edges up 0.1 per cent; Glimra rises 3.1 per cent; crude gains 3.1 per cent; gold gives back $16

By Tomas Nygaard

The KXC Composite rose 23 points, or 0.1 per cent, to 20,407 on Wednesday. It was a quiet day on the surface. Abroad it was not.

Shares in the largest foreign market fell sharply in the early hours of trading. At the low the main index was down more than 3 per cent. Its computing-heavy rival was down nearly 5 per cent. Then prices turned. By the close the main index was down a quarter of one per cent and the rival was down six-tenths.

“Somebody woke up and decided it was not the end of the world,” a Karontoborg trader said. “By noon we were in the black. I would not call it confidence. I would call it relief.”

Computing shares were weak again abroad. The biggest chip maker lost about 4 per cent. A smaller chip designer plunged by almost a fifth after a weak forecast. At home Glimra Compute rose 3.1 per cent after Tuesday’s fall.

Bonds and the krona

The ten-year yield was unchanged at 3.82 per cent. The krona held at 7.00 to the dollar and 7.98 to the euro.

Abroad, new figures showed price rises in the largest economy speeding up to 3.8 per cent over the year. Traders there now expect their central bank to raise rates in December and again in January. That is one reason shares fell so far at the start of the day. The Commonwealth Central Bank, which held rates last Thursday by four votes to three, will watch the dollar closely.

Oil and gold

Benchmark crude rose 3.1 per cent to $112.92 a barrel. The rebels in the Red Sea region said they had struck ports in the neighbouring kingdom, and traders added a premium for risk. The foreign market that sets the global price will be shut today for a national holiday. Traders said that made some of them cautious about holding positions over the break.

The group of oil producers meets on Sunday. Some want to raise output next year, and some do not. The result could move the price on Monday.

Gold fell $16 to $3,647 an ounce, a drop of 0.4 per cent. It held its ground better than it did on Tuesday, when it lost $90.

Tariffs abroad

The largest economy also announced new import duties on a dozen trading partners on Wednesday. The Commonwealth is not on the list. Shipping brokers in Karontoborg said it was too early to say how the new duties would change trade routes.

Mortgages

Lakes lenders made no change to their rates on Wednesday. One lender still quotes 7.4 per cent for a five-year fixed loan. Most others quote 7.2 or 7.3 per cent.

Glimra Has Not Yet Replied

The shareholders who asked for a written account of the chief executive’s return are still waiting

By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent

Glimra Compute has not yet replied to the investors who wrote to its board on Tuesday. A company spokesman said on Wednesday that the letter was “being considered by the board”.

The investors, three pension funds and an insurer with a little over 9 per cent of the firm between them, asked for a written account of how the board moved from one view to the other in eleven days. On 12 November the board said an inquiry had found that Viggo Tharaldsen broke the firm’s rules. On 23 November it said he would return as chief executive.

The spokesman said the board would reply “in due course”. He did not say whether the reply would be public. Two of the funds said they would not comment before they had read an answer.

Glimra’s shares rose 3.1 per cent on Wednesday, after Tuesday’s fall of 7.7 per cent. The postponed share sale and bond, each of about VKR 1.7 billion, are still planned for the new year.

City Asks for a Recount at Six Sites

Karontoborg’s letter to the Treasury says the half-weight rule is wrong for land where pipes and roads are in place

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor

Karontoborg sent its letter to the Treasury on Wednesday, two days before the date it had set. It asks for a recount at six of the city’s thirteen federal sites.

The Treasury’s claims table counted all thirteen sites at half weight. It said federal land was “slow”, because the building of homes usually starts three years or more after a claim is made. The Treasury’s four-page method was published on 20 November.

The city’s letter argues that six of the sites are not slow. Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said each one has its roads and pipes in place, or is about to. “It is a short list on purpose,” she said. “We chose the six that are ready. If the Treasury looks at them and still says half weight, we will have a different conversation.”

The letter says the other seven sites may be slow, but asks that each be judged on its own facts.

The Treasury said it would study the letter. Its review of the rule for all cities will report by the end of February.

Rebels Say They Struck Ports in the Neighbouring Kingdom

The kingdom says its air defences stopped most drones; a day after the government-side strike on the rebel port, the shipping agents ask what comes next

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The rebels who hold the northern Red Sea coast said on Wednesday that they had attacked two of the neighbouring kingdom’s Red Sea ports. They named the kingdom’s largest port on that coast and another one to the south.

The rebel spokesman said the attacks caused damage at both. The kingdom said its air defences had shot down several drones in the east and the west of the country. It said nothing about damage in the ports.

Neither claim could be checked. No casualties were reported by Wednesday evening.

The rebels had warned the ports on Tuesday and told port workers and shipping firms to leave. That was before government-side aircraft struck the rebel-held port early on Wednesday.

Shipping

Shipping agents in Karontoborg said the ports were still open. “Several ships have been told to wait outside,” one agent said.

The foreign shipping press reported that three tankers were hit by unidentified objects near the mouth of the Gulf earlier in the week. All continued their voyages. A bulk carrier at anchor caught fire in the same region. No one was hurt. None of the ships was Vinlandic.

Our frigates

VNS Gudrid and VNS Kjipuktuk are still escorting merchant ships in the approaches to the strait. The Defence Ministry said they had not been asked to change their task. They are not involved in the fighting on shore.

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad told the People’s Thing on Tuesday that she would send the defence committee a written update every week. The committee has not yet received the first. A Ministry spokesman said it would be sent “in the usual way”, and did not give a date.

War-Risk Rates Hold; Cape Count Rises to Thirty-Two

Neither insurer moved on Wednesday; one more Vinlandic ship takes the long route

By Halldor Vik

Karontoborg’s two war-risk insurers made no new changes to their Red Sea rates on Wednesday. Both have raised them this week: the larger by about 5 per cent on Monday and the smaller by about 4 per cent on Tuesday.

One more Vinlandic-flagged ship has taken the long route round the Cape. The count is now thirty-two, up from thirty-one.

Sigrid Holme of the Karontoborg Shipowners’ Association said the move was “one owner’s sum, not a trend”. She added: “If the ports in the kingdom come under real fire, the sums change for everyone.”

What Happened to the Scare

A morning that looked like a crash turned into a quiet close; analysts say the reasons have not gone away

By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent

For a few hours on Wednesday it looked as if the computing sell-off abroad would turn into a rout. By evening it had faded.

Early in the day the biggest foreign index fell by more than 3 per cent. The computing-heavy index fell by nearly 5. Some funds that follow the chip makers were down by more than a tenth. Then buyers appeared. Traders put some of that down to thin trading before the holiday abroad. Some pointed to a note from a well-known investor, who said he was now almost entirely buying shares. Others said the first fall had simply gone too far.

“It was a very quick turn,” an analyst said. “It tells you that a lot of people were waiting for a fall to buy. It does not tell you that the worry is over.”

Three worries

Analysts named three worries. The first is the price of money. The foreign central bank is expected to raise rates twice, and chip makers are valued on profits many years ahead. Higher rates make those profits worth less today.

The second is the strain on households. Prices abroad are rising by 3.8 per cent a year, faster than many wages.

The third is whether the new computing systems will earn what their makers promised. Weak forecasts from smaller firms made that doubt louder.

At home

Glimra Compute rose 3.1 per cent. It remains a small firm by foreign standards, but it is one of the larger names on the KXC. It still has to raise about VKR 3.4 billion in the new year.

“What happens abroad sets the weather,” the analyst said. “What happens in Karontoborg decides whether Glimra can sail in it. The board’s reply to its investors is part of that.”

Flu Follow-Up: Review on Monday

No new national figures this week; the three hospitals that limit visiting say they will decide on Monday

By Gunnhild Paquette, Science Correspondent

The Commonwealth Public Health Agency publishes flu figures once a week, on Wednesdays. There are no new numbers today. On Wednesday it said 27 per cent of tests were positive, up from 20 per cent the week before.

The three large Karontoborg hospitals that limit visiting to one visitor per patient at a time said on Wednesday that the rules stand. They will be reviewed on Monday. No fourth hospital has said it will follow.

A Public Health Agency official said the next figures, due on Wednesday 2 December, would show whether the rise is slowing. “A week is a long time in flu,” she said. “We are watching the hospital admissions as well as the tests.”

The advice is the same as it was on Wednesday. People over sixty-five and other vulnerable groups should be vaccinated now. People who are ill should stay at home.

Two Weeks to Find a Seat

The Night Ferry closes on 5 December; the returns list is the best route, and the Sunday matinee is nearly gone

By Oda Fjellstad, Arts Critic

The Old Quarter Playhouse has told this newspaper that the Sunday matinee of Marit Nesheim’s The Night Ferry is almost sold out. It starts at two o’clock on 29 November. Seats for the final week are also nearly gone.

The play closes on Saturday 5 December. Every earlier performance has sold out. The returns list stays open. Most days a few seats come back.

The longest returns queue this week was on Saturday, at about forty people by two in the afternoon. The shortest was on Tuesday, at about a dozen.

Several readers have written to say they went for the cook and stayed for the engineer. The engineer, who only wants to be paid, has the funniest lines in the play. He also has the saddest. They are often the same line.

If you cannot get a seat, the returns list on a weekday after four is your best chance.

Pilots Lose in Overtime; Storm Beat the Lakers

Rapids win 4–3 in Sundgard; Stadakona edges Karontoborg 2–1; Kawennote sits out and is “looking good for Friday”

By Kjetil Hammer

Wednesday’s games left the top of the Commonwealth Hockey League table close.

In Sundgard the Forsgard Rapids beat the Pilots 4–3 in overtime. The Pilots had led in the third period, but the Rapids scored late to tie and won it in the extra period. The Pilots took a point and the Rapids took two.

The Pilots again played without Sindre Kawennote, who did not dress. The coach said after the game that Kawennote had taken contact in the morning skate and “came out of it fine”. “If he does the full practice today and has no reaction, he plays Friday,” the coach said. “I would like him back. So would everybody in Sundgard.”

Stadakona

The Stadakona Storm beat the Karontoborg Lakers 2–1. It was a tight game in the cold, with a single goal separating the sides for most of it. The Lakers scored first. The Storm tied the game in the second period and won it with a goal in the third.

“They did what I asked,” the Storm coach said. “They dressed warm, they played simple. A side with three wins all season is not an easy side to beat.” The Lakers coach said his players had “earned a point and did not get one”.

The Table

By Kjetil Hammer

The Pilots are 9–3–2, with 20 points from 14 games. The Storm are 9–4, with 18 points from 13 games. The Pilots lead by two points. The Storm have a game in hand.

The Forsgard Rapids are 7–5–1, with 15 points from 13 games. The Laukvik Lynx are 6–8. The Hokelgard Royals are 5–5. The Karontoborg Lakers are 3–13–1, with 7 points from 17 games.

Friday

There are no games on Thursday. On Friday the Hokelgard Royals host the Pilots, and the Lakers host the Lynx.

The Commonwealth Sport Integrity Office gave no new note on Wednesday.

The Herald’s View

Count the Votes, Then Count the Cost

The Thing votes this afternoon. The Government has chosen to go ahead as laid. It has not found an amendment. It has not found a deal. It says it is confident, and it may be. That is its right.

But the vote will not settle the question that Landvern asked. It will only settle who counted better.

What Landvern asked

Landvern asked for one thing: the training number in the contract. The Government gave it a number, in an order. For two days the Government has told the country that an order has “the same force as the contract”. If that is true, there is no cost in saying so in the contract. If it is not true, the country should know.

The Finance Minister had a clear day on Wednesday and a clear chance. A short amendment, laid in the morning, would have put 180 places a year into the document the firms sign. It would have cost the Government a few lines. She did not lay one.

We wrote on Wednesday that a number that can be adjusted can also be lowered. We still think so. We do not say the Government wants to lower it. We say a promise is only worth what it costs to break.

What the Government risks

Ministers will say that the first VKR 4.5 billion is safe. They are right. They will say Landvern and the Opposition are voting against jobs. That is not fair. The first money does not depend on this vote.

The statement is larger. It asks the Thing to approve VKR 105 billion of borrowing on a note that says “broadly” and lists two items “at risk if growth is weaker”. Last Thursday the Monetary Council held rates by only four votes to three. A foreign central bank is expected to raise its own rates twice. Prices are rising in the world around us. A Government that wants to borrow this much should expect questions about how it will pay.

If the Government wins today, it will win with the votes of members who have not been given much. If it loses, it will have lost a vote it could have tried to win with a paragraph.

What to watch

We do not know the result. Tomorrow we will ask whether any member who voted yes said why, and whether the Government promised anything in the lobby that it would not say in the chamber.

The union leaders meet this morning. Their question is a fair one: who can change the number? The Government should be able to answer it in one sentence. So far it has needed three.

The frigates

The Defence Minister promised the defence committee a written update every week. It is Thursday and nothing has arrived. The first one need not be long. It should say where the ships are, what they are doing and what has changed. It should be sent, and a copy should be public.
