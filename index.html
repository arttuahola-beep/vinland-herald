Solbakk Splits the Fourteen Billion Over Three Years

VKR 4.5 billion next year, 4.5 billion the year after, 5 billion in the third; borrowing rises to VKR 105 billion; the weighting of federal land will be reviewed after Friday’s table

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk gave the numbers on Tuesday afternoon.

The VKR 14 billion industry package for steel, vehicles and a road north will be paid in three parts. VKR 4.5 billion comes in next year’s budget. Another VKR 4.5 billion comes the year after. The last VKR 5 billion comes in the third year.

The Commonwealth will borrow VKR 105 billion next year, she said. This year it is borrowing VKR 101 billion. Most of the rise, she said, comes from higher interest on debt the Commonwealth already owes, not from new spending.

“We said we would hold the line,” Ms Solbakk told the People’s Thing. “We have held it. The money is real, it is spread out, and it is paid for.”

She also made one move on housing. The Treasury’s rule that gives federal land only half weight in the cities’ claims will be reviewed after Friday’s claims table has sat.

Shares rose after the speech. The ten-year Commonwealth yield rose four basis points to 3.87 per cent.

An Hour, Three Numbers and One Review

Solbakk keeps her promise to hold the line; the opposition asks what happens to the later years

By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent

Ingrid Solbakk spoke for fifty-eight minutes. The chamber was full. The public gallery was full too, and a row of steelworkers from Laukvik sat near the front in their union jackets.

She began with the Commonwealth’s position. Growth has slowed. Prices are still rising faster than the Central Bank wants. Borrowing costs are higher than they have been for some years. “These are not easy months,” she said. “I will not pretend they are.”

Then she came to the package.

The split

The VKR 14 billion for steel, vehicles and a road north will be spent over three budgets. Next year’s budget carries VKR 4.5 billion. The second year carries another VKR 4.5 billion. The third carries VKR 5 billion.

That adds up to VKR 14 billion. Ms Solbakk said the later parts would be “set in the plan now, not left for later”. She did not say what would happen to them if tax income fell.

“The first part pays for the contracts that can start at once,” she said. “The later parts pay for the road, which takes longer to build, and for the training of young workers.”

Borrowing

The Commonwealth will borrow VKR 105 billion next year. That is VKR 4 billion more than this year’s VKR 101 billion.

Ms Solbakk said most of the rise came from interest. “Money we borrowed years ago now costs more to carry,” she said. “That is the price of the times. It is not new spending.” She did not borrow more to soften the blow, she said, and she would not.

Housing

On housing she made one move. The Treasury gives federal land only half weight when it measures what the cities can build. The cities say that rule is unfair.

“The rule will be reviewed,” Ms Solbakk said. “But it will be reviewed after the claims table has sat on Friday, not before. We will look at what the cities bring to the table. Then we will look at the rule.”

The Bank

She was asked by a Labour member whether she would like the Central Bank to hold rates on Thursday. She did not take the bait.

“The Bank decides. I do not,” she said. “That is how it should be.”

“A Promise on the Instalment Plan”

Karhagen says the jobs are being paid for later; Tollefsrud sees “three chances to cut”

By Sunniva Rask

Liv Karhagen, the Leader of the Opposition, replied for Labour.

“The minister has given us a promise on the instalment plan,” she said. “The first part is real. The second and third parts are promises. And promises made in a hard year have a habit of shrinking in the next one.”

She said Labour would support the first part of the package. It wanted the later parts written into law, so that a future budget could not cut them quietly.

On housing, she said the review came “a week late”. “The cities have to make their claims on Friday under a rule the minister now says may be wrong,” she said. “That is the wrong way round.”

Bjarne Tollefsrud, the leader of Landvern, spoke last. His party’s votes have kept the Government’s majority since its rise in the Lakes.

“Three budgets means three chances to cut,” he said. “We will vote for the first. We will be watching the second and the third very closely. And we will read Friday’s contract line by line.”

He did not say whether Landvern would vote for the statement as a whole. The vote is due next week.

What the Split Means for Laukvik

First-year money covers the contracts that can start at once; the union wants to know if that includes the mill

By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg

In Laukvik the first question on Tuesday evening was simple. Does the first VKR 4.5 billion reach the mill?

The minister did not say which contracts would come first. She said only that the first part would pay for work “that can start at once”. Steel for public building and for the road could fit that description. So could orders for vehicles.

“If the first year buys steel, then the first year saves jobs,” the union official said. “If the first year buys plans and studies, then it does not. We will know when we read the contract on Friday.”

Sørstrand Steel plans to pause one of its two strip lines at Laukvik from 1 December. About 380 workers would go on short time. The company meets the union and the city on Friday. It gave no comment on the statement.

Still reading

The union is still reading the draft standard contract. It now says it will give its full view on Thursday, a day before the contract is laid in the People’s Thing.

On Monday it said the local-hiring clause was “stronger than we feared”. It also said the clause on training had no numbers. On Tuesday the minister said the later parts of the package would pay for training. The union official said that was welcome. “But a training clause still needs a number,” she said. “Money in year three is not a number of young people in work.”

The city of Laukvik is still reading too. Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie’s office said it had nothing to add.

Shares Rise After the Statement

KXC gains 0.5 per cent as investors read the numbers as steady; bond yields rise on borrowing; krona slips

By Tomas Nygaard

The KXC Composite rose 105 points, or 0.5 per cent, to 20,574 on Tuesday. Most of the gain came after Ms Solbakk sat down.

“There was no shock,” one trader said. “The split was what people expected. The borrowing was a little higher. Nobody had to sell.”

Shares abroad fell again. Large computing companies were sold ahead of results from a large foreign chip maker. There were also reports of an outage on a computer network used by a foreign government. That government later said the outage was routine maintenance, not an attack. Glimra Compute fell 2.9 per cent.

Bonds went the other way. The ten-year Commonwealth yield rose four basis points to 3.87 per cent. Investors will have to buy more Commonwealth debt next year, and some wanted a higher return to do it.

The krona weakened to 6.98 to the dollar.

Benchmark crude fell 0.6 per cent to $111.31 a barrel. A mine struck another ship in the Gulf, but shipments from the Gulf kept recovering. Gold fell $19 to $3,876 an ounce.

Thursday

The Central Bank decides on Thursday. The policy rate is 3.50 per cent. Inflation is 3.9 per cent.

Economists are split. Some expect the Bank to hold. Others expect a quarter-point rise. “Higher borrowing and a weaker krona both push the same way,” one bank economist said. “The Bank has to decide whether slower growth pushes harder the other way.”

Six Maps, Seven to Go

Karontoborg publishes five more site maps; Treasury still silent; Mayor welcomes the review “but not the timing”

By Elise Brattli

Karontoborg published maps of five more federal sites on Tuesday. With Monday’s first map, the city has now published six of the thirteen sites on its list.

Each map shows the water mains, the sewers and the roads already in place. Two of the new sites are near the old rail yards on the north side of the city. Three are along the harbour.

“These are the sites the Treasury says are slow to build on,” Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said. “Look at them. The pipes are there. The roads are there. What is slow is the rule.”

She welcomed the minister’s review of the half-weight rule. “But not the timing,” she said. “We are asked to make our claims on Friday under a rule the minister now admits she will look at again. We will make them anyway. We will publish the last seven maps before we do.”

The Treasury gave no comment on Tuesday. The claims table sits on Friday.

The Minister Will Speak

Bjornstad to brief the defence committee on Thursday; government forces dig in before the main southern port; strait quiet on Tuesday

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

Ragnhild Bjornstad said on Tuesday evening that she would brief the People’s Thing defence committee on Thursday about the southern strait.

The briefing will be private. The minister said some of what she would say about the Navy’s plans could not be said in public. “But the committee will hear all of it,” she said.

“No third escort will sail before I have spoken to the committee,” she said. “They gave their consent on 1 October. They are entitled to know what has changed since.”

She did not say whether a third escort was being planned. She did not say whether she would ask the committee to look again at its consent.

The committee’s chair welcomed the briefing. “That is the right order,” the chair said. “First the facts, then the ships.”

On the ground

The strait itself was quiet on Tuesday. No attacks on ships were reported there.

Inland, government forces sent more troops to a district on the road to the main southern port, officials in the region said. The district is seen as the first line of defence for the city. The rebels hold the hills above the strait and the island in it. They made no new statement on Tuesday.

The Navy

The Navy said nothing new about its ships. VNS Gudrid brought five merchant ships through on Saturday. VNS Kjipuktuk remains with the partner navies’ escort group.

Not in the Statement

The northern grid gets no line in the autumn statement; the road north does; the Cree Council stays quiet

By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent, Hokelgard

The autumn statement had a great deal to say about steel. It had nothing to say about northern grids.

Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud told the climate talks on Monday that the Commonwealth would publish a plan for its northern grid “in the coming year”. Ms Solbakk’s statement on Tuesday did not mention that plan. It did not mention the Shield Intertie.

A Finance Ministry official said the grid plan was “not yet ready to be paid for”. “When there is a plan, there will be a number,” the official said.

The road

The package does include a road north. The minister said the later parts of the money would pay for it. She did not say where it would run.

In Hokelgard that was noticed. A road, like a power line, crosses land. Some of that land belongs to the constitutional peoples or is held under their treaties. The minister did not say whether any of the nations had been asked about the route.

“We heard the word ’road’,” said one adviser to a northern council, who asked not to be named. “We did not hear the word ’route’. When we hear that, we will have questions.”

The Council

The Kaniapiskau Cree Council made no statement on Tuesday. On Monday one of its advisers said only: “We listened.”

The Provincial Thing’s status committee was also silent. Its chair has said it will set a date for its finding on the Intertie before the end of the month. That means by Monday 30 November.

Skjoldkraft, which would build the Intertie, gave no new figure.

Flu Figures Due This Afternoon

Last week 16 per cent of tests were positive; the agency’s advice has not changed

By Gunnhild Paquette, Science Correspondent

The Commonwealth Public Health Agency publishes its weekly flu figures this afternoon.

Last week 16 per cent of tests for breathing illness were positive for flu. The week before it was 13 per cent. The week before that it was 7 per cent. The figure has more than doubled in a fortnight.

The agency said last week that hospital admissions for flu were rising. It did not give a number. Doctors in Karontoborg said on Tuesday that waiting rooms were busy, but not yet as busy as in the worst winters.

“We are early in the season,” one family doctor said. “That is why the advice matters now, not in January.”

The advice has not changed. People over sixty-five and other vulnerable groups should be vaccinated now.

The Night Ferry Sails a Week Longer

Nesheim’s play extended to 5 December after the extra matinee sold out; tickets for the new week go on sale on Thursday

By Oda Fjellstad, Arts Critic

Marit Nesheim’s The Night Ferry will run for one more week at the Old Quarter Playhouse. The last performance will now be on Saturday 5 December, not 28 November.

The playhouse said on Tuesday that people had kept asking for seats since the extra matinee on Sunday 22 November sold out. “We could not keep saying no,” a spokesman said.

The play follows two sisters, Inga and Tove, on the last night of the ferry they have run for thirty years. A bridge opens in the morning. Hedvig Sund plays Inga, who steers. Anna Kahrhonnes plays Tove, who takes the fares and does the talking.

Why it lasts

For an hour it is a comedy. Then the engine stops in the middle of the River, and the sisters finally talk. At the end they tie up the boat and stand on the dock, and do not leave.

It is a play about a promise to keep going that nobody ever made out loud. In a week when the Commonwealth is arguing about promises paid in parts, it has found its audience at the right time.

Tickets for the extra week go on sale on Thursday morning.

Still Running

By Oda Fjellstad

The Kjipuktuk College of Art’s two shows run until 12 December: Ingeborg Sylliboy’s tide-chart prints and Halvard Denny’s burnt-wood panels.

Jonas Atsistaehron’s What the River Kept will go to Hokelgard in the spring. The Royal Academy has not yet named the hall or the dates.

The Herald’s View

A Plan in Three Parts Needs Three Locks

For three weeks this newspaper asked the Finance Minister one question. When will the VKR 14 billion be spent? On Tuesday Ingrid Solbakk gave a clear answer. VKR 4.5 billion next year. VKR 4.5 billion the year after. VKR 5 billion in the third year.

That is a fair answer, and we thank her for it. It is also a sensible plan. A road takes years to build. Training takes years to pay off. Spreading the money over three budgets keeps next year’s borrowing in check. Ms Solbakk promised to hold the line, and on the numbers she has.

The weak point

But a plan in three parts has a weak point. Only the first part is money. The second and third are promises.

Ms Solbakk says the later parts are “set in the plan now”. We believe she means it. But a minister cannot bind next year’s budget, still less the one after. If growth slows further, or if borrowing costs rise again, the third part will be the easiest thing in the Commonwealth to cut. Nobody will lose a job that day. The jobs will simply never come.

Mr Tollefsrud put it bluntly: three budgets, three chances to cut. Ms Karhagen called it a promise on the instalment plan. Both are right about the risk.

Three locks

There are three ways to make the promise hold.

The first is law. The package should be written into a single act of the People’s Thing, with all three sums in it. A future government could still change the act. But it would have to do so in the open, by a vote, not by a quiet line in a budget.

The second is the contract. Friday’s standard contract should say what each year’s money buys, and when. If the first year buys steel, Laukvik should be able to see it. If training waits for year three, the union should know the number of young people it will pay for.

The third is reporting. The Finance Ministry should publish, every six months, what has been spent and what is still owed. A promise that is counted in public is harder to break.

Housing, a week late

On housing, the minister has agreed to review the half-weight rule for federal land. That is welcome. The cities have argued for it for weeks, and Karontoborg has now put six maps on the table to make its case.

But the timing is wrong. The cities must make their claims on Friday under a rule the minister now says she will look at again. The review should begin now, and the claims made on Friday should be counted again under whatever rule comes out of it.

The strait

Finally, a word on the southern strait. Yesterday we asked the Defence Minister to go to the defence committee before any third escort sails. On Tuesday evening she said she would, on Thursday. That is the right order, and we say so plainly. The committee should now ask the question that matters: does the consent of 1 October still fit the sea as it is?
