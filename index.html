Solbakk Sets Housing Range at VKR 3.5–4.5 Billion; Frigates Get a Window; Intertie Committee Named for 22 October

After Kaupang, Finance publishes a three-year envelope; coalition tells Navy to be ready “within days”; status committee sits next Wednesday

By Elise Brattli and Peter Okimaw, in Kaupang and Hokelgard

Finance Minister Ingrid Solbakk published a housing range on Wednesday afternoon. It was the figure the mayors had come to Kaupang to hear.

The Commonwealth will put between VKR 3.5 billion and VKR 4.5 billion into a three-year urban housing envelope. Ms Solbakk said the range was “a first envelope, not a final account”. She said it could grow toward the VKR 6 billion figure discussed in the summer if cities met building and land conditions the Treasury will publish next month. She would not adopt the eleven cities’ claim of a 60,000-home gap as a Commonwealth target. “We fund what we can count,” she said. “Homes are counted when they are built.”

The mayors called the floor “serious” and the ceiling “thin”. Karontoborg’s Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said the cities would “take the 3.5 and argue every krona above it”. Laukvik’s Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie said the refusal to endorse 60,000 homes was “a political choice dressed as arithmetic”. Talks continue. A joint statement after the morning meeting in Kaupang set out the range and the next working group for 29 October.

In Hokelgard the escort moved from paper to the waterfront. Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad said the multinational coalition had signalled a sailing window “within days”. The frigates VNS Gudrid and VNS Kjipuktuk are to prepare to slip from Kjipuktuk. No calendar day was named. Dual-chamber consent was completed on Tuesday. The Navy says both ships remain alongside and will leave on the coalition’s signal, not before.

The Provincial Thing’s status committee on the constitutional peoples will hold its first sitting on the Shield Intertie on Wednesday 22 October. Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud welcomed the date. Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum of the Kaniapiskau Cree Council said: “A dated room is better than a dated referral that sits empty. We will be there. The burial sites are still not for trade.”

Price data from the republic to the south came in hotter than dealers had marked. The KXC fell 0.9 per cent. Oil rose. Published Gulf war-risk rates in Karontoborg were raised one step. Twenty-two Vinlandic-flagged ships are now routing around southern Africa.

“Answer Through the Inquiry”

AG requests Hvelv board minutes; Brandvold’s counsel breaks eight days of total silence with one sentence; Free Thing keeps the second-meeting line alive

By Sunniva Rask and Peter Okimaw, Hokelgard

The Attorney General moved from lock to drawer.

A spokesman said on Wednesday that the office had written to Hvelv Systems to request the minutes of the board’s internal review of alert escalation. That review was promised on Saturday 10 October and is still inside its thirty-day window. The preservation notice served at 15:30 on Friday 9 October “remains in force”. The inquiry “continues”. The spokesman would not say whether the request named Oskar Brandvold personally. He said only that “board records of the review are now required”.

Counsel for Mr Brandvold answered with a single sentence, issued mid-afternoon: “Mr Brandvold will answer questions through the inquiry.” It was the first word from his side since the Privacy Ombud’s finding of Wednesday 7 October. He has not been seen in public for eight days. He did not sign the board’s Saturday statement. He did not comment on Tuesday’s Provincial Thing judgement that named 14 and 15 July as the days never produced. A receptionist at the North Bank head office again told reporters that media questions go to counsel. Counsel took no follow-ups on whether Brandvold had read the judgement text.

Hvelv’s board did not issue a new public statement. People familiar with the thirty-day review said the minute request would “concentrate minds” inside the firm. It does not shorten the review’s published timetable.

Liv Karhagen, Leader of the Opposition, called the counsel line “the least a man can say when the chamber has already counted to two”. “Minutes are not testimony,” she said. “They are a list of who sat in a room. The inquiry still has to open the drawers the notice locked.”

Hanne Loft of the Green River Alliance said the request for minutes was “proper and late”. Bjarne Tollefsrud of Landvern said Brandvold should “walk into the inquiry, not hide behind a letterhead”. A Commonwealth whip said the Government was “content to let the Attorney General work” and would not add a political timetable.

Free Thing: meeting “still planned”

The Free Thing published a short note on Wednesday. It said a second meeting between the junior coalition partner and Landvern was “still planned”. It gave no date and no place. The junior partner remains unnamed. The Herald still cannot confirm Monday’s report that such a meeting would follow the Archive sitting. A Landvern source said only that “channels are open”. The Commonwealth majority, a whip repeated, “holds”.

First Minister Erik Halvorsen did not speak about Brandvold or the coalition rumour. His office pointed reporters to Tuesday’s line in the chamber — that the Government accepts the record as the chamber finds it — and to the Attorney General. He has always preferred procedure to performance. On Wednesday he kept that preference.

After Tuesday’s judgement

The Provincial Thing’s judgement of Tuesday evening stands. It records that Hvelv’s recordings of 14 and 15 July were never produced. It does not rehearse the 24 August alert, the 27 August switch-off, the Ombud finding or the AG notice. Those facts remain on the public record. Nora Sakej-Eriksen, who moved the judgement, said Wednesday that the Assembly’s work in the chamber was “done for now” and that “the Archive and the inquiry carry the longer page”.

Hotter Prices Abroad Knock the Exchange; Oil Holds the High Ground

KXC falls 0.9 per cent after southern republic data; crude up 0.6 per cent to $106.60; ten-year eases to 4.00 per cent; Solbakk’s range lands in the afternoon

By Tomas Nygaard and Elise Brattli, Karontoborg

Wednesday was a data day first and a housing day second.

The KXC Composite closed down 0.9 per cent at 21,172. North Bank chip designers and machine-learning names led the fall. Dealers said price data from the republic to the south had come in hotter than the morning marks. Talk of firmer policy abroad hit the stocks that had ridden cheap money and long duration. Banks were mixed. Energy held up on the oil tape. “You could feel the rate fear in the first hour,” said a New Harbour fund manager. “Everything that needs patience got sold.”

Benchmark crude rose 0.6 per cent to $106.60. There was no new confirmed strike by the large foreign power on Wednesday. Traders said the market was still pricing the first week of a wider air war on the southern narrows, and the risk to shipping at the Gulf mouth. The krona firmed to 6.85 against the dollar. Gold was little changed at $3,980 an ounce.

The ten-year Commonwealth yield fell three basis points to 4.00 per cent. Dealers said the equity sell-off had done more than the oil bid. Policy rate 3.50 per cent. Inflation 3.9 per cent. Governor Hallvard Mikaelsen did not speak. The two large Lakes lenders who moved to 7.2 per cent on five-year fixed loans last week held there. No third lender has followed.

The range, in numbers

Ms Solbakk’s envelope is VKR 3.5 billion to VKR 4.5 billion over three years. Treasury officials said the bottom of the range assumes slower municipal land release; the top assumes cities clear sites on the timetable the working group will set. The summer’s VKR 6 billion figure remains, in Solbakk’s words, “a path, not a promise”. The cities’ 60,000-home gap is acknowledged as their estimate. It is not written into the Commonwealth envelope.

Mayor Oyelaran-Skog said 3.5 billion was “a floor we can stand on” and 4.5 billion was “not enough for the Lakes alone, let alone eleven cities”. She said the campaign would not abandon the 60,000-home arithmetic in public, even if the Commonwealth refused to print it. “Our gap does not vanish because Treasury will not say the number,” she said. Mayor Wabano-Lie said he would bring the same two spreadsheets to the 29 October working group: “Hers and ours. Hers just got a number. Ours still has a shortfall.”

A mayor from the Atlantic seats, speaking for three smaller cities, called the envelope “better than another summer of adjectives”. A Lakes housing officer said privately that 3.5 billion at the bottom would not clear the worst waiting lists without land the cities do not yet control. That is why Solbakk tied any climb toward VKR 6 billion to land and building conditions. The mayors know it. They still want the ceiling raised before winter tenders.

Ms Solbakk, asked whether the range was a climb-down from the summer talk, said: “It is a range. Ranges have bottoms. This one is real.” She took no further questions.

Coalition Names a Window; Rates Rise; No New Strike Confirmed

Gudrid and Kjipuktuk told to be ready within days; published Gulf war-risk book stepped up; twenty-two ships take the Cape; airport in the Gulf capital still shut until Saturday

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The escort is no longer only a vote.

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad said on Wednesday that the multinational coalition had given Vinland a sailing window “within days”. The frigates VNS Gudrid and VNS Kjipuktuk are to complete final stores and be ready to slip from Kjipuktuk. She did not name a calendar day. “We sail when they call the hour,” she said. “We will not be late for it.”

Foreign Minister Anders Ohkwari-Dahl said partners had been informed that dual consent was complete and that Vinland was “operationally ready”. He said the unclassified summary of the rules of engagement, shared with party leaders in late September, still governed. The initial escort period remains four months unless the Great Thing renews it.

On the waterfront in Kjipuktuk a Navy spokesman said both ships remained alongside on Wednesday night. Leave had been cancelled for sailing crews. Families were told to expect short notice. Tugs were warned for a possible dawn slip. “Within days means within days,” the spokesman said. “Not within speeches.”

No new confirmed strike by the large foreign power against the militia that holds the southern Red Sea narrows was reported on Wednesday. Partner briefings said the air campaign against energy and logistics sites continued as planned. The militia claimed further fires. Independent tallies were not available. The death toll in the Gulf capital from Saturday’s airport strike remained fourteen. About 320 people were hurt in that attack. The airport stays closed until Saturday. The Foreign Ministry’s advice against all but essential travel to the country’s airports stays in place. The two Vinlandic citizens hurt on Saturday remain out of hospital.

Rates up one step; more ships south

Karontoborg’s war-risk underwriters raised published Gulf rates by one step on Wednesday. Quotes for some voyages had already widened on Tuesday. Twenty-two Vinlandic-flagged ships are now routing around southern Africa, five more than on Tuesday. Sigrid Holme of the Karontoborg Shipowners’ Association said owners were “paying for a wider war and for a quieter day that did not feel quiet on the water”. Approaches to the Gulf mouth remain hazardous. The port town on the strait coast remained unconfirmed.

Displaced people on the militia coast are still put by aid agencies above 180,000.

Power Cuts and a Slowed Satcom Tool

Defending capital of the eastern European war reports hundreds of thousands without power; early-warning dispute enters a third night; partner satcom service degraded

By Aslak Kvande

Missiles and drones again struck the defending capital of the eastern European war on Wednesday. Power and heat failed across large districts. Local authorities put the number without power at about 650,000 by nightfall. The defending side said it had received no early warning from partners for a third night. It also said a widely used partner satcom tool had been slowed, cutting the reach of some interceptor and reconnaissance drones until partial service returned late in the day.

The attacking side did not comment on the warning claim or the satcom complaint. An energy truce announced abroad on Sunday looked, by Wednesday, like a phrase without a grid. Hokelgard studied the reports. Mr Ohkwari-Dahl’s office said Vinland would “align its sanctions and its language with partners where it can”. It named no capitals and no firms.

Status Committee Sits 22 October

Kaninaskum and Grimsrud welcome a date; burial sites still not for trade; Archive’s longer page reaches eleven families

By Mary Kanatiio Berg, Constitutional Affairs Correspondent

The Provincial Thing committee on the status of the constitutional peoples will open its first sitting on the Shield Intertie on Wednesday 22 October.

Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum of the Kaniapiskau Cree Council spoke after the date was published. “A dated room is better than a dated referral that sits empty,” she said. “We will be there. The line is still three hundred and ten kilometres across our lands. Two burial sites sit on the preferred route. Those sites are not for trade. Everything else is for the table.”

Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud said the Government “welcomes the committee’s calendar” and would “send officials ready to listen”. Skjoldkraft’s head of northern projects, Jostein Harkavik, said the utility would attend with route maps that “do not move burial grounds”. He repeated that the burial sites “were never on our list of things to move”.

The Intertie referral was dated Tuesday 13 October, the same day the Archive sitting ended and the escort cleared dual consent. Speaker Marta Okimaw-Strand’s order of 5 October held. Dating the referral opened the file. Dating the committee opens the room.

Families: eleven of fifteen

The Archive’s count moved from eight to eleven of the fifteen families with holdings open on 14 and 15 July. The work remains private. Keeper Annie Tsawenhohi Grann did not add to Monday’s promise. A spokesman said the longer page was “being written with the families, not about them”.

The Herald’s View

A Range, a Window, a Date

Wednesday gave the country three numbers it can use.

The first is a housing range. Ingrid Solbakk put VKR 3.5 billion to VKR 4.5 billion on the table after the Kaupang meeting. That is less than the cities wanted and more than a shrug. She refused to write their 60,000-home gap into Commonwealth law. She left a path toward the summer’s VKR 6 billion figure if land and building conditions are met. Mayors are entitled to call the ceiling thin. They are not entitled to say they were given nothing. A range with a real floor is how grown governments start. The working group on 29 October will show whether the floor rises.

The second is a sailing window. Dual consent for the Red Sea escort was finished on Tuesday. On Wednesday the coalition said “within days”. That is not a calendar, and it should not be. The frigates have waited for a proper vote. They should not wait for a speech. When the hour is named, Gudrid and Kjipuktuk should slip.

The third is a committee date. The Shield Intertie referral was dated Tuesday. The status committee will sit on Wednesday 22 October. Grand Chief Kaninaskum is right: a dated room beats a dated letter. Burial sites are not for trade. The rest of the three hundred and ten kilometres is for argument across a table, not for another month of silence.

Oskar Brandvold’s counsel finally spoke. One sentence. He will answer through the inquiry. The Attorney General has asked for the board’s minutes of the alert review. That is the right next step. It is not the last. Silence after a judgement that named two missing days was already bad. A single line through counsel is better than nothing and worse than showing up.

The Free Thing says a second meeting with Landvern is still planned. The junior partner is still unnamed. Rumour is not a crisis until it becomes a vote. The majority holds for now.

Name a range. Name a window. Name a date. Wednesday did all three. The ships will sail when called. The cities will argue every krona. The Cree Council will sit on the 22nd. The man who ran the firm that lost two days of voices has taken one step toward the inquiry. He has more steps to take.
