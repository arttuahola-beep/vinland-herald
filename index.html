Five Ships Through as Rebels Gain Ground at the Southern Strait

Navy’s second escort ends safely; government forces pull back near the mouth of the Red Sea; Navy says it has “adjusted” the route for any next convoy

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The Navy’s second escort is over. VNS Gudrid brought five merchant ships safely to open water in the north of the Red Sea on Saturday, the Navy said.

On the same weekend, the ground shifted at the other end of the sea. Rebel fighters advanced along the coast near the narrow southern strait. Government forces backed by the rebels’ foreign enemies pulled back.

“Five ships went in. Five came out,” said Commander Einar Paul, the Navy spokesman. “That is the job. We have adjusted the route for any future escort. We will not say how.”

No third escort has been announced.

Grimsrud to Speak on “Northern Grids”

Energy Minister reached the climate talks on Saturday; the Shield Intertie will be in the room, if not in the speech

By Sunniva Rask, Political Correspondent

Energy Minister Petter Grimsrud arrived at the climate talks on Saturday, a day later than first planned. He speaks for the Commonwealth this afternoon.

His office said the speech would be about “northern grids”. It would not say more.

That phrase will be read closely at home. The largest northern grid project in the Commonwealth is the Shield Intertie. Skjoldkraft wants to build it across 310 kilometres of Cree land. The Provincial Thing’s status committee has still to decide whether the line touches the constitutional status of the Kaniapiskau Cree. If it does, the Provincial Thing’s veto applies.

The committee gave no word over the weekend. Its chair has said it will set a date for its finding before the end of the month. Grand Chief Ruth Kaninaskum said on Thursday that the Council would ask for the committee’s reasons in writing.

One official who has worked on the talks said Mr Grimsrud would speak “about the cost of moving clean power over long distances, and who carries it”. He would not say whether the minister would name the Intertie.

Any line in the speech about consent will be noticed in Hokelgard. A minister who speaks abroad about northern grids, while a committee at home weighs a people’s consent, cannot easily avoid the subject.

Solbakk’s Statement Tomorrow

Finance Minister has promised to “hold the line”; three things to watch

By Sunniva Rask

Ingrid Solbakk gives her autumn statement in the People’s Thing at two o’clock tomorrow. She said on Thursday that rising borrowing costs would not change her course. She did not speak again over the weekend.

There are three things to watch.

The first is borrowing. The ten-year Commonwealth yield rose to 3.89 per cent on Thursday, then eased to 3.85 per cent on Friday. Each rise makes the Commonwealth’s debt a little dearer to carry. Ms Solbakk has said she will not borrow more to soften that.

The second is the industry package. The VKR 14 billion for steel, vehicles and a road north has been promised. The statement should show when the money is spent, and from which year’s budget.

The third is housing. The cities want to know whether the Treasury’s half weight on federal land is a matter of method or of money. The statement may not say. If it does not, the question goes to Friday’s claims table unanswered.

Four Days to Read a Contract

Union and Laukvik get the draft at nine this morning; union says it will start with the clause on local hiring and training

By Tomas Nygaard, Karontoborg

The draft standard contract for the industry package goes to the steelworkers’ union and the city of Laukvik at nine o’clock this morning. The Finance Ministry confirmed the time on Sunday.

The draft will be shared in confidence. It will be laid in the People’s Thing on Friday, four days from now.

The union said on Sunday where it would start. “The first clause we will read is the one on local hiring and training,” a union official said. “If the money is public, the jobs should be local, and the young people should be trained here. If that clause is weak, the rest does not matter much.”

She said the union had asked for a week to read the draft. “We have four days. So we will read the clause that matters most first.”

Laukvik

Laukvik’s council will read the draft alongside the union. The office of Mayor Tomas Wabano-Lie said the city would comment once it had read the text.

Landvern

Bjarne Tollefsrud, the leader of Landvern, said on Sunday that his party would read the contract on Friday “line by line”.

“Steel jobs in Laukvik are Lakes jobs,” Mr Tollefsrud said. “The Lakes will read this contract too. We will not take the Government’s word for what is in it.”

Landvern’s votes matter. The Government’s majority in the People’s Thing has been thin since Landvern’s rise in the Lakes. The People’s Thing committee that will examine the contract first sits on Thursday 26 November.

Sørstrand

Sørstrand Steel, which owns the Laukvik mill, gave no comment over the weekend. It meets the union and Laukvik in Laukvik on Friday. It has said it will pause one of its two strip lines there from 1 December. About 380 workers would go on short time.

The Mayor: “We Will Bring the Pipes”

Oyelaran-Skog says Karontoborg will bring maps of every serviced federal site to Friday’s table; Treasury silent

By Elise Brattli, Economics Editor

Mayor Petra Oyelaran-Skog said on Sunday that Karontoborg would come to Friday’s claims table with maps of every serviced federal site in the city.

“We will bring the pipes,” she said in a statement. “Water, sewers, roads, on a map, site by site. If the Treasury still says those sites are slow to build on, it can say so looking at the map.”

The Treasury gave no new word over the weekend. On Thursday it said that half weight “applies to all federal land” and that “the method is the method”.

Laukvik and Sundgard backed Karontoborg at Thursday’s working group. Neither said anything new over the weekend.

The claims table sits on Friday 20 November. Thirteen sites are on the list.

A Small Rise to End the Week

KXC up 0.1 per cent on Friday and 0.9 per cent on the week; crude falls 1.6 per cent; gold recovers a little; yield eases

By Tomas Nygaard

The KXC Composite rose 21 points, or 0.1 per cent, to 20,882 on Friday. It ended the week 0.9 per cent higher.

Technology shares were mixed. Glimra Compute, which fell about 10 per cent on Thursday after its chief executive resigned, was little changed.

Benchmark crude fell 1.6 per cent to $110.90 a barrel. Traders said some of Thursday’s rise had been “a fright, not a fact” after rebels claimed strikes on Gulf oil sites that could not be confirmed.

Gold rose $37 to $3,913 an ounce. It is still near its lowest price in more than a year.

The ten-year Commonwealth yield eased four basis points to 3.85 per cent. The krona was unchanged at 6.95 to the dollar.

“A quiet Friday after a loud Thursday,” one trader said. “Nobody wanted to bet big before the statement and the Bank.”

The weekend brought new attacks on tankers near the Gulf strait. Traders expect oil to open higher this morning.

The Central Bank decides on Thursday. The policy rate is 3.50 per cent. Inflation is 3.9 per cent.

The Southern Gate

Rebels advance on the coast near the mouth of the Red Sea; government forces withdraw; the Navy’s escort ends safely as the map changes

By Halldor Vik, Defence Correspondent

The Navy’s second escort through the Red Sea ended on Saturday. VNS Gudrid and the five merchant ships she led reached open water in the north of the sea. No ship was hit. No sailor was hurt.

It was a good end to a hard week. But the map behind the convoy changed over the weekend.

On the coast

Rebel fighters advanced along the coast near the narrow southern strait that leads into the Red Sea. Government forces pulled back from several positions, according to reports from the region. The government’s foreign backers did not comment.

The southern strait is the gate that every ship must pass to reach the Red Sea from the south. Whoever holds its coast can reach the shipping lane with drones and missiles more easily.

“A rebel advance at the southern gate makes any escort harder,” one retired Navy officer said. “Not impossible. Harder.”

The Navy

Commander Paul said the Navy had “adjusted the route” for any future escort. He would not say how. He said VNS Kjipuktuk remained with the partner navies’ escort group.

“We go where we are asked, and we come back with the ships,” he said. “If the risk changes, the plan changes.”

Defence Minister Ragnhild Bjornstad did not comment over the weekend. The People’s Thing gave its consent to the escort mission on 1 October. It has not been asked to look again.

The Gulf

At the other end of the Arabian peninsula, two foreign tankers were attacked near the Gulf strait over the weekend. Both were damaged. Their crews were reported safe. No group claimed the attacks by Sunday evening.

On Sunday a Gulf state said its air defences had shot down missiles fired at its capital, as well as several drones aimed at its oil region.

No Vinlandic-owned or Vinlandic-flagged ship was involved, the Shipowners’ Association said. The strait talks did not resume.

Rates and routes

The two larger Karontoborg war-risk houses raised Gulf rates again on Friday evening, by about 3 per cent. That was their second rise in two days.

One Vinlandic-flagged ship left the Asia trade over the weekend and will now sail the Atlantic. The count on the long route round the Cape fell to thirty-one.

Sigrid Holme of the Shipowners’ Association said the Navy had “done what it said it would”. She added: “Five ships home safe is worth saying out loud. So is the fact that the strait is no safer than it was.”

Three Leaders May Meet

By Halldor Vik

The leaders of the world’s three largest powers may meet next week, officials in two of the capitals said over the weekend. They would discuss wars in Europe and the Gulf, nuclear weapons and new technology. No date has been confirmed.

Foreign Minister Anders Ohkwari-Dahl said the Commonwealth would follow the meeting closely. “We will read what they agree,” he said. “We will judge it by what it means for our ships, our north and our trade.”

The Commonwealth Public Health Agency said the risk to the Commonwealth was very low. It said it was in touch with partner agencies and with the main airports.

A Second Firm, and a Door That Held

Wholesaler reported an attempted intrusion last week; ministry confirms it after days of silence; Kaldhus back at all seven sites

By Ingvild Sørhus, Technology Correspondent, Karontoborg

A food wholesaler reported an attempt to break into its computer systems last week, the Ministry for Digital Affairs said on Sunday. The attempt was stopped.

It is the first time the ministry has said that any firm other than Kaldhus Logistics was targeted. The ministry did not name the wholesaler.

“The attempt was found and stopped before it reached any machine that runs a store, a freezer or a lorry,” a ministry spokeswoman said. “No food was put at risk.”

She would not say whether the attempt was linked to Wednesday’s attack on Kaldhus. “That is for the police and Cyber Command to say,” she said. “They are looking at both.”

Kaldhus

Kaldhus said on Saturday that control systems were working again at all seven of its cold stores. Staff had checked temperatures by hand at two of them since Wednesday.

“We are back to normal working,” a Kaldhus spokeswoman said. “We are not back to normal thinking. That will take longer.”

The company said last week that the attackers had demanded a ransom. It has said it will not pay.

The letters

Kari Lindqvist, the Minister for Digital Affairs, sends her letters to large food firms today. They ask three questions. Which machines are on the network? Who can reach them? What do you do when the screens go dark?

“We now know of two firms,” Ms Lindqvist said on Sunday. “One was hit. One held the door. I want every firm to know which of those it would be.”

She said firms would have until the end of the year to reply.

Glimra Hires a Search Firm

Board aims to name a new chief executive “before the new year”

By Ingvild Sørhus

Glimra Compute’s board has hired a search firm to find a new chief executive, the company said on Saturday. It aims to name one “before the new year”.

Viggo Tharaldsen resigned as chief executive on Thursday. A board inquiry found he had not declared a personal relationship with a senior employee. The finance chief is acting as head of the firm.

Glimra plans to raise about VKR 1.7 billion in new shares and the same again in bonds in the new year. The board said the timing of the search and the timing of the raise were “linked”.

“No investor wants to put money into a firm with nobody at the top,” one analyst said. “The board knows that. It has given itself six weeks.”

The River Closes

What the River Kept ends its run at the Royal Academy with about 83,100 visitors

By Oda Fjellstad, Arts Critic

Jonas Atsistaehron’s What the River Kept closed at the Royal Academy on Sunday evening. The Academy said about 83,100 people had seen it. On Wednesday the figure was about 77,000.

The last weekend was the busiest of the run, the Academy said. On Sunday afternoon the queue reached the street.

I went back on Friday night, at the last late opening. The long room was full, and quiet. People stood still in front of the slow water, as the show asks them to. A child near me asked her father why the river did not move. “It does,” he said. “Wait.”

That is the lesson of the show. It asked a busy city to wait, and more than eighty thousand people did.

The Academy has not said where the work goes next.

The Herald’s View

A Week That Owes Us Reasons

This is a week of answers. The union gets a draft today. The Finance Minister speaks tomorrow. The Central Bank decides on Thursday. On Friday the contract is laid and the cities make their claims on federal land.

Each of those answers will come with a number. The Herald asks that each also come with a reason.

The contract

The union has four days to read the draft. It will start with local hiring and training. That is the right place to start. Public money for steel should buy jobs and skills where the steel is made. If the contract says so clearly, the union and Laukvik should say so too. If it does not, the Finance Ministry should explain why before Friday, not after.

The statement

Ms Solbakk has said she will hold the line. Holding the line is a choice, and it may be the right one. But a line is only as good as the reasons for it. Tomorrow she should say plainly when the VKR 14 billion is spent, and what she will not do so that it can be.

The claims

The Treasury has said nothing since “the method is the method”. Mayor Oyelaran-Skog will bring maps to Friday’s table. That is a fair challenge. The Treasury should answer it with a short written note before Friday. It has had a week. It should not need two.

The escort

The Navy brought five ships through the Red Sea without harm. That is good work, and it should be said. But the coast at the southern strait changed over the weekend. Commander Paul says the route has been adjusted. That is the Navy’s business. Whether to send a third escort into a harder sea is the People’s Thing’s business too. Before any third escort sails, the Defence Minister should tell the People’s Thing what has changed.

The food chain

On Friday we asked whether other firms had been hit. On Sunday the ministry answered: one more firm was targeted, and it held. That is useful. It should become a habit. When firms in the food chain are attacked, the public should hear about it promptly, in a short regular note, without waiting for a newspaper to ask.
